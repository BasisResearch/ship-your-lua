## Positions on the two hard questions

**Is redefining `Step` or `LuaSem` as data acceptable?**

- **For `Step`: yes, redefine it**, as the R1-4 #1, R1-5 #1 and R1-6 #1 kernel.
  - `luaV_execute` is itself a function. A kernel `match` on the read vector is at least as close to the `vmcase` text as an inductive constructor with premises.
  - Keeping the inductive and proving it equal to the kernel once is **not** "once". Every future opcode (15 in F2, 7 in F3, 2 in F4) would owe one equivalence arm, of about 3–5 lines each. That is a permanent per-rule tax of roughly a third of today's 8.5 lines, and R1 compression counts it.
- **The real threat is not trust but non-determinism.** If the order of `next`, or an evaluation order the manual leaves unspecified, is modelled as a relational choice, every "graph of a function" idea degrades:
  - R1-3 #3's `choose` node, and the determinism formats of R1-5 #3 and R1-6 #3, lose determinism;
  - Layer B has to become a backward simulation, which is where CompCert paid for determinacy and receptiveness **[check: Leroy, JAR 2009, §on "determinate" semantics]**.

  Idea 2 below keeps every semantics a function by making the choice a *read of an oracle*. With it, "data" versus "inductive" costs the same under F2 and F3.
- **The heap** enters a kernel as one coarse read/write port. That is enough for L-B1 and for definite initialisation, which only concern registers. It does **not** give the machine-level frame for A1: see idea 4.
- **Closures** make the state a stack of frames. Register indices stay frame-relative and static, *except* for multi-result windows, where every static-footprint idea breaks: see idea 3.

**Which idea reduces A1?**

- **Not the ALU orbits** of R1-5 #4 and R1-6 #4.
  - The coordinator's data shows identity orbits only for {BAND, BOR, BXOR}, {LTI, LEI, GTI, GEI}, {ADDK, SUBK} and {LT, LE}. That is 11 arms folding into 4.
  - These are the *cheapest* arms: straight-line code with no calls.
- **For the F1 straight-line arms**, a verified per-block checker with an untrusted certificate (idea 5) makes orbits irrelevant.
- **From F2 on**, almost every new arm is a thin wrapper around a C callee (`luaV_finishget`, `luaV_finishset`, `luaV_concat`, `luaV_objlen`, `luaH_*`, `luaD_precall`) plus a shared `checkGC` tail **[check the list against lvm.c]**. So the unit of A1 cost is the *callee* and the *wrapper shape*, not the arm (idea 4).
- **The cheapest falsifier** is idea 5's pilot: an unverified `#eval` checker with hand-written certificates for ADD, SUB, MUL and MULK. It needs no proofs and takes about an afternoon.

## 1. Three-port kernels: read / define / kill (the CONCAT write set, CALL clobbering and entry garbage as one port)

**Acts on:**
- R1-4 #1 (RCW), R1-5 #1 (port-typed kernels), R1-3 #1 (static-footprint kernel) and R1-6 #2 (cloister): **(c)** their two-port design (reads, writes) is unsound or unfaithful for H2.
- **(b)** It combines those kernels with R1-6 #5 (low-equivalence as the representation) and R1-1 #3 (X registers), without R1-1 #3's cost of changing `Value`.

**The objection, with a counterexample.**

Every round-1 kernel has one kind of output port, `wr`. The must-analysis treats a written register as *defined* afterwards. H2's range write needs a third kind: registers the instruction leaves holding garbage.

Take the hand-written Proto:

```
LOADK 0 "a" ; LOADK 1 "" ; LOADK 2 "c" ; CONCAT 0 3 ; <print R[1]>
```

`luaV_concat` runs two passes **[check this trace against lvm.c 5.4.7, the `isemptystr(top-2)` branch]**:

- Pass 1: the top two operands are `""` and `"c"`. The first is empty, so it runs `setobjs2s(top-2, top-1)`, which sets `R[1] := "c"`.
- Pass 2: it concatenates `"a" .. "c"` into `R[0]`.

The machine prints `c`. Each round-1 spec fails differently on this program:

- **R1-5 #1 (`outs = [(pc+1, [A])]`) and R1-6 #2 (`concat : fp = (range A B, [A])`):** the spec says `R[1]` is unchanged (`""`), and `Supported` admits the read. So Layer A's theorem is *false* for this program.
- **R1-3 #1 and R1-4 #1 (write set = the whole range):** the must-analysis marks `R[1]` defined, so the kernel must *produce* `"c"`. That means transcribing `luaV_concat`'s pass structure, including its empty-string shortcuts and its in-place `tostring` of integers (lvm.c:649-650, where `5` becomes `"5"`, visible to a later `EQK R[1] 5`), into the trusted base.

**The idea.** Each edge of a kernel carries three masks, the three bits of a port kind:

| bit | port | meaning in `Step` | meaning in the must-analysis |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | `rd` | value in | must be defined here |
| 2 | `def` | value out, computed by the body | defined afterwards |
| 1 | `kill` | **left unchanged by the spec**; arbitrary on the machine | undefined afterwards |

- **CONCAT A B:** `rd = rmask A B`, `def = {A}`, `kill = rmask (A+1) (B-1)`. The body is just the right fold with `%d`.
- **CALL:** `def = rmask A (C-1)` (the results), `kill` = everything at `A` and above except the results. `keepMask` disappears as a special case; it *is* CALL's kill port.
- **Entry:** every register is killed. That is today's `st[0] = 0`.

The sweep becomes `st[t] &= (st[pc] &&& ~~~kill_e) ||| def_e`. It reads all three masks off the kernel, so there is no `keepMask` table beside it.

**Core rules.**

- **L-B1** (unchanged in form, now also true for H2): agreement on `rd` gives the same edge, equal values on `def`, and every register outside `def` unchanged. Killed registers are "unchanged" in the spec, which is exactly what makes the kernel deterministic without transcribing garbage.
- **One new theorem, `kill_unobservable`:**
  - *Statement:* `Supported p → (BcSemHavoc p out ↔ BcSem p out)`, where `BcSemHavoc` may set every `kill_e` register to anything on edge `e` (and every register at entry).
  - *Proof:* R1-6 #5's unwinding with `LowEq (Γ pc)`. Consistency is L-B1 plus `rd ⊆ Γ pc`. Local respect holds because `kill_e ∩ Γ t = ∅` by the sweep.
  - *Subsumes* `bcSemFrom_iff` (entry) and `cbcSem_iff` (CALL). Two special proofs become one instance each.
- **A1 representation:** a machine state represents `s` at `pc` iff they agree on `Γ pc`. The CONCAT arm owes nothing about `R[A+1..]`, and the CALL arm owes nothing above its results.

**What it makes free.**

- H2's faithfulness problem, which becomes a port declaration instead of a transcription of `luaV_concat`'s loop.
- `keepMask` and the CALL special case in `sweep`.
- The two definite-initialisation theorems, which become instances of `kill_unobservable`.
- At A1, every clause about dead or clobbered slots: CALL, CONCAT's scratch, and F3's `luaD_poscall` moves.

**What it costs.**

- One more mask per edge. About 40 lines for `kill_unobservable` on top of R1-6 #5's unwinding (about 150).
- The trusted spec deliberately *lies* about killed slots: it says "unchanged" where the machine holds garbage. This is already true today for CALL. Reviewers must accept it as a named convention, justified by the theorem.
- Hand-written Protos that read a killed register are rejected. `luac` never produces them **[check on the test corpus: no read of `R[A+1..A+B-1]` after CONCAT before a write]**.

**Theories.**

- *Known, compilers:* gen/kill bit-vector frameworks. Kildall, "A unified approach to global program optimization", POPL 1973; Kam and Ullman, "Monotone data flow analysis frameworks", Acta Informatica 7 (1977). `def`/`kill` is gen/kill made a field of the *semantics* rather than of the analysis.
- *Known, physics:* Landauer, "Irreversibility and heat generation in the computing process", IBM J. Res. Dev. 5 (1961). Erasure is a different operation from a write: it destroys information without producing a specified value. The kill port is erasure, and the spec need not say what the erased cell holds.
- *Known, logic and verification:* weakening in linear logic, i.e. discarding a resource (Girard, "Linear logic", TCS 50, 1987), and Boogie's `havoc` (Barnett, Chang, DeLine, Jacobs, Leino, "Boogie: a modular reusable verifier for object-oriented programs", FMCO 2005).
- *Novel:* a kill port whose spec-level value is "unchanged", licensed by one noninterference theorem, so that the trusted spec never models garbage and the machine is never required to preserve it.

**Pilot.**

- **Smallest build:** R1-4 #1's `OpSpec` with a `kill` field, for MOVE, CALL and CONCAT only; `kill_unobservable` for that three-opcode ISA. Show that `cbcSem_iff` and `bcSemFrom_iff` are its instances.
- **Cheap falsifier:** run the Proto above on the ELF under Sail, with a kernel-checked output as in the existing validations.
  - If it prints `c`, the two-port H2 specs are refuted, and the kill port is required.
  - If it prints an empty line, my `luaV_concat` trace is wrong. The kill port then buys only the `keepMask`/`cbcSem_iff` unification.
- **H1–H4:**
  - H1 is a `LOADK` admission change.
  - H2 is `rd`/`def`/`kill` plus the fold.
  - H3 and H4 are kernel arms.
  - Footprint and definite-initialisation proof lines are 0 in each case.
- **R1:** delete `Step.sim`, and re-derive both lifting theorems from `kill_unobservable`.

**Seed.** `421`: three bits of one flag word, read as three port kinds (4 read, 2 define, 1 kill). The lowest bit, the one round 1 lacked, is the kill.

## 2. Oracle ports: non-determinism as a read of a prophecy, so "graph of a function" survives F2

**Acts on:**
- R1-3 #3 (free-call-monad rulebook) and its `choose` node; R1-5 #3 and R1-6 #3 (determinism formats and LL(1)): **(c)**.
- R1-4 #2 (construct trees) and R1-4 #1 (RCW): **(a)** the oracle is one more read port.

**The objection.**

- F2 brings `pairs`/`next`, whose order the manual leaves unspecified. In the implementation it depends on the hash layout, and so on the string-hash seed (`luai_makeseed`) **[check what the bare-metal build defines]**.
- **R1-3 #3's own mitigation fails.** It adds a `choose` node, and a decidable "choose-free" check restores determinism per arm. But determinism is compositional: `Graph.det` for a block needs it for every sub-call. So every block that *contains* a `for … in pairs` loses it transitively.
- **The rule-format checks fail exactly there.** For R1-5 #3's `DetFormat` and R1-6 #3's `ll1`, the `next` rule has no disjoint guard. The check fails at the first F2 program, and it gives no fallback.
- **The kernels** (R1-4 #1 and others) become `List`-valued. L-B2 then becomes membership, and Layer B needs backward simulation (see the positions above).

**The idea.** Extend `Host`, which already parameterises the semantics by an implementation-defined fact (`showBuiltin`), into an **oracle**:

```lean
structure Oracle where
  rank : TabId → (version : Nat) → Key → Nat     -- next order = keys sorted by (rank, canonical tiebreak)
  -- later: tostring of a table's address, like showBuiltin today
```

- **Why a ranking.** A ranking can only produce a permutation of the current keys, so no well-formedness side condition is needed. Every permutation is some ranking, so nothing is lost.
- **`version` increments only on key insertion.** This follows the manual's rule that a traversal survives assignment to existing fields and clearing them **[check: the manual, §6.1 on `next`]**.
- **Both layers read the same oracle.** Table identities are allocation-order counters. The two layers allocate in the same order because `NEWTABLE` runs where the constructor is evaluated.
- **Other unspecified orders** (for example the order of multiple assignment) are *pinned* to `luac`'s order in `LuaSem`, because the claim is about this implementation. They need no oracle.

**Core rules.**

- **L-A1, per oracle:** `∀ O, LuaSem H O c out ↔ ∃ fuel, run H O fuel c = some out`. R1-3 #3's `Graph_iff_run` and `Graph.det` go through unchanged: the `pairs` arm computes `order O t.id t.ver t.keys` as a pure `let`.
- **L-B1/L-B2 for `TFORCALL`/`next`:** the kernel has a static port "oracle at (id, version)". It is still a function, so `Iff.rfl` survives.
- **Layer statements:**
  - Layer B: `∀ O, BcSem O (compile s) out ↔ LuaSem O s out`.
  - Layer A: `Halts c out 0 → ∃ O, BcSem O p out`.
  - The machine's `O` is *defined from its run*: `rank` is the position in `luaH_next`'s traversal at that version. It is a Skolem function (a prophecy), not something proved per program.

**What it makes free.**

- Determinism by construction across F2, with no `choose` node, no weakening of `Graph.det`, and no backward simulation.
- The source/bytecode agreement on iteration order, which becomes `rfl` on a shared parameter.
- It costs 0 on H1–H4, which contain no choice.

**What it costs.**

- An `O` parameter threaded through both semantics and the theorem statements, the same kind of cost `Host` already has.
- A *law* to verify at A1: the implementation's order at a given version is a function of (table, version). That fails if a GC step reorders a table without an insertion (dead-key clearing in weak tables) **[check `clearkey`/`clearbyvalues` in lgc.c]**. F2 has no weak tables, but F4 metatables bring `__mode`.
- An allocation-order correspondence of table ids between the two layers. This is a Layer B invariant, stated once.

**Theories.**

- *Known, complexity theory:* a probabilistic machine is a deterministic machine with a random tape. Gill, "Computational complexity of probabilistic Turing machines", SIAM J. Comput. 6(4), 1977. The choice moves into an input, and the machine stays a function.
- *Known, verification:* prophecy variables. Abadi and Lamport, "The existence of refinement mappings", TCS 82(2), 1991. The machine's future `next` order is a prophecy fixed at the start.
- *Known, economics:* Arrow–Debreu state-contingent commodities. Debreu, *Theory of Value*, 1959, ch. 7. Uncertainty is removed by indexing every good by the state of nature, and each agent's plan is then a function of that state. The oracle is the state of nature, and `LuaSem O` is the contingent plan.
- *Novel:* the oracle as a *ranking* (a permutation by construction), versioned by key insertion to match the manual's traversal rule.

**Pilot.**

- **H1–H4:** 0 lines. The oracle is unused, so the pilot measures only that it costs nothing.
- **Smallest build:** add a `pairs`-like construct to R1-4 #2's toy `Tree` rulebook, consulting `O`. Check that `holds_det` needs 0 new lines.
- **Cheap falsifier, before any Lean:** a native-Lua law check.
  - Build random tables, traverse them, assign to existing fields and to nil, traverse again, and compare orders.
  - Also check `collectgarbage()` between traversals.
  - Any order change without an insertion refutes the versioning. Then `version` must count something else (for example GC cycles), which leaks the collector into the spec.

**Seed.** The three mirrors around the pivot 7 (`y7y`, `w7W`, `u7U`). Both flanks, the source and the bytecode, read one shared centre, and they agree up to representation (case). The pivot is the oracle.

## 3. Anaphoric open top: dependent footprints for multi-result windows (where every static kernel breaks, F3)

**Acts on:**
- R1-6 #1 (avatar microcode), R1-4 #1, R1-3 #1 and R1-6 #2: **(c)**. Their footprints are functions of the instruction word, and F3 breaks that.
- R1-6 #1 notes the problem ("the footprint theorem weakens there") but leaves it open. This idea goes past it **(d)**.

**The objection.** In F3:

- `CALL A B 0` (multi-result) and `VARARG A 0` set `L->top`.
- The *next* instruction, `CALL A 0 C`, `RETURN A 0` or `SETLIST A 0`, reads `R[A..top-1]`.

So the read set depends on a *value*. That is Mokhov et al.'s monadic, not selective, case. `ins : Word → List Nat` cannot state it, and the two escape routes are both bad:

- Over-approximating to `R[A..maxstack)` makes the must-analysis reject all `luac` output, because the registers above `top` are garbage after a multi-result call.
- A value-dependent L-B1 has no single proof.

**The idea.** Add one ghost register `T` (the open top) and one port form, **range-to-T**.

- A **producer** (`CALL … C=0`, `VARARG … C=0`) *introduces a referent*: its def-port is `A..T`, and it writes `T`.
- A **consumer** (`… B=0`) is an *anaphor*: its read-port is `[T] ∪ rmask A (open − A) ∪ (open..T)`.
- The must-analysis domain grows from `mask` to `mask × Option Nat` (an open base):
  - a producer sets `open := some A`;
  - a consumer requires `open = some b` and `rmask A (b − A) ⊆ mask`, then resets `open := none`;
  - an instruction whose ports stay below `b` preserves `open`, which is anaphora resolution across intervening material.

**Core rules.**

- **L-B1 as a dependent footprint, proved once:**
  - *Statement:* if `s` and `t` agree on the static ports including `T`, and on `range A s.T`, then they step alike. Agreement on `T` makes the dynamic range the same set for both, so the proof is R1-4 #1's `map_congr` applied twice.
  - This is "selective with exactly one bind, through one ghost register". It is the smallest monadic extension that keeps a single footprint theorem.
- **Definite initialisation:** one soundness lemma for the extended domain, by the same unwinding as idea 1. Open-base state is a Γ refinement.
- **A1:** `T` is represented by `L->top`, and only while `open ≠ none`. Otherwise `L->top` is unconstrained, which is what the machine does (`L->top = ci->top` at most dispatch heads **[check]**).

**What it makes free.** F3's multi-result opcodes without a per-rule footprint argument, and without over-approximation. The static-kernel convergence of round 1 stays valid past F2.

**What it costs.**

- A ghost field in `State` (about 20 lines), a domain change in `sweep` (about 40), and the dependent L-B1 (about 40).
- Faithfulness risk: `luac` might separate a producer from its consumer by an instruction that touches the window. The persistence rule would then reject valid output.
- 0 benefit on H1–H4 or on the A1 pilot arms. This idea buys insurance, not a held-out saving.

**Theories.**

- *Known, linguistics:* discourse representation theory. Kamp, "A theory of truth and semantic representation", in *Formal Methods in the Study of Language*, 1981; Heim, *The Semantics of Definite and Indefinite Noun Phrases*, PhD thesis, UMass 1982 (file change semantics). An indefinite introduces a discourse referent, and a later pronoun must find an *accessible* one. The producer is the indefinite, the consumer the pronoun, and "accessible" is the open state of the must-analysis.
- *Known, PL:* selective against monadic dependencies (Mokhov, Lukyanov, Marlow, Dimino, ICFP 2019). This idea is a named point between them.
- *Known, logic:* linear types (Wadler, "Linear types can change the world!", IFIP TC2 Working Conference on Programming Concepts and Methods, 1990 **[check the venue]**). The open referent is used exactly once.
- *Novel:* a must-analysis whose domain carries an anaphoric referent, so that a value-dependent read set is still discharged by one footprint theorem.

**Pilot.**

- Not measurable on H. Report it as an F3 readiness check.
- **Cheap falsifier:** compile the Lua 5.4 test suite and `c/tests` with `luac -l`. For each `C=0` producer, check that its consumer is the next instruction, or that everything in between stays below the producer's A. Any counterexample means the anaphor must span more, and the domain must record which instructions are transparent.
- **Smallest build:** a four-opcode toy (MOVE, `CALL` with `C=0`, `CALL` with `B=0`, RETURN0), the extended sweep, and the dependent L-B1.

**Seed.** The stem-plus-index pairs `pA5 … pA8` and `4N0 … 4G1`. A stem is introduced, and a later occurrence refers back to it with a new index: an antecedent and its anaphor.

## 4. A1 is priced per callee, not per arm: wrapper shapes under call-target substitution, and GC by a space budget

**Acts on:**
- R1-5 #4 and R1-6 #4 (ALU orbits) and R1-3 #2 (ALU templates): **(c)**. They optimise the cheapest arms, and the coordinator's data caps even that saving.
- R1-2 #4 (δ, with "one contract per C primitive") and R1-4 #5 (the Core, "function-contract idiom"): **(a)** extends them from F4 primitives to the whole wrapper-and-callee structure of F2+.
- **(d)** GC: no round-1 idea prices `checkGC`. R1-1 #5 names it as "the hard part".

**The objection.**

- **The ALU-orbit saving is capped.** On F1 the identity orbits cover 11 arms in 4 orbits. ADD/SUB split on branch sense, and MUL/MULK split on register allocation and scheduling, so R1-6 #4's `Relabel` (shift plus positional substitution) does not transport them. The best case is 7–10 arms saved, all of them straight-line.
- **From F2 on, arms are wrappers around callees.** GETTABLE, GETI, GETFIELD, GETTABUP and SELF share `luaV_fastget`-or-`luaV_finishget`. SETTABLE, SETI, SETFIELD and SETTABUP share `luaV_fastset`-or-`luaV_finishset` plus a barrier. CONCAT, NEWTABLE and CLOSURE end in `checkGC` **[check the list against lvm.c 5.4.7]**. The per-arm straight-line part is small. The cost is in the callees, and each callee is shared by several arms.

**The idea.** Split L-C1 into two kinds of obligation, neither of them per arm:

1. **Wrapper shapes.** A wrapper is, for example: `savepc; call f(args); updatetrap/updatebase; store`, or `fastget ? store : Protect(finishget)`, or `…; checkGC`. Each shape gets *one* simulation theorem, parameterised by a hypothesis `Contract f C`:
   - "for every `f` meeting `C`, `wrapper[f]` simulates the rule whose kernel calls `δ_C`";
   - the orbit group is *call-target substitution* (plus argument marshalling), not ALU substitution.
2. **Callee contracts.** Each C function gets one contract. It is stated as refinement of a δ function over an abstract heap, extending R1-4 #5's Core to tables: `Core.get? : Heap → Value → Value → Option Value`. The contract is proved once and used by every arm and by both semantic layers.

The per-opcode residue is membership: `arm_o = wrapper_k[f_o]`. That is decided by idea 5's checker, not proved.

**GC, the shared tail nobody priced.** `checkGC` runs `luaC_step` when `GCdebt > 0` **[check the 5.4 macro]**, and the incremental collector's safety proof (tri-colour invariant, barriers in the SET arms) is enormous. Stage it:

- **(a) Budget, for F4.**
  - *The lemma, proved once:* if `debt + allocated ≤ 0` then `checkGC` is a no-op.
  - *The budget* is `−GCdebt` in the VmLoaded boot witness, a concrete number.
  - *The charge* against it is an upper bound on allocation, computed from `LuaSem`'s run (each created string costs `sizeof(TString) + len + 1`, rounded as dlmalloc does **[check]**; interning only lowers it).
  - *The Layer A theorem* gains the hypothesis "allocation ≤ budget". It is weaker, but explicit.
- **(b) Collector, later:** one rely step, "`luaC_step` preserves Rep", where Rep mentions only the *payloads of reachable objects*. It ignores mark bits, `gclist`, the string-table bucket layout and `strcache`. Lua's collector does not move objects, so no address renaming is needed.

**Core rules.**

- **L-C1** is `wrapper_sim k : (∀ f, Contract f C → Sim (wrapper_k[f]) (rule_k δ_C))` (one per shape), plus `contract_f` (one per callee), plus a decidable membership per arm.
- **GC (a):** `checkGC_noop : debt + alloc ≤ 0 → checkGC σ = σ` up to the dispatch head.

**What it makes free.**

- The count of A1 proofs from F2 on falls from #arms to #shapes + #callees. My guess is about 6 shapes. The callees are shared by 4–5 arms each. **[check by counting call targets per arm in the ELF]**
- The contracts double as the semantics of δ, so Layer B's operator agreement stays `rfl` (R1-2 #4).
- GC disappears from the F4 pilot under the budget.

**What it costs.**

- Contracts for `luaV_concat`, `luaS_newlstr` (interning, `luaS_resize`) and `luaV_objlen` are real proofs over dlmalloc and the string table. The VsaIris dlmalloc route helps, but I have not read it.
- The budget restricts the claim, and it must be presented as staging, not as the result.
- **A risk that kills (a):** the boot (`luaL_openlibs`) may leave `GCdebt` near 0. Then the budget is tiny.
- 0 benefit on the A1 pilot arms ADD, EQI and FORLOOP, which call nothing.

**Theories.**

- *Known, economics:* the hard budget constraint. Kornai, "The soft budget constraint", Kyklos 39(1), 1986. An agent whose spending is capped in advance needs no bailout (a collection) to be modelled.
- *Known, PL and resources:* space credits under GC. Madiot and Pottier, "A separation logic for heap space under garbage collection", POPL 2022 (PACMPL 6). Also Hofmann, "Linear types and non-size-increasing polynomial time computation", LICS 1999.
- *Known, software architecture:* linking against interfaces (Cardelli, "Program fragments, linking, and modularization", POPL 1997), and rely–guarantee for the collector as environment (Jones, "Tentative steps toward a development method for interfering programs", TOPLAS 5(4), 1983).
- *Novel:* orbits under *call-target* substitution as the proof quotient of an interpreter loop, and a GC budget read off the boot witness as the staging device.

**Pilot.**

- **A1 held-out arms:** no gain. Say so.
- **Cheap falsifiers, both without Lean:**
  1. Dump `g->GCdebt` (and `GCestimate`) at the VmLoaded point from the boot witness, or from a Sail run of the ELF. If the budget is under a few KiB, drop (a).
  2. `objdump` every F2/F4 arm, list its call targets, and group arms by call target. If most arms have a private callee, the wrapper quotient saves nothing.
- **Smallest build:** the `Protect`-call-store shape theorem with an abstract `Contract` hypothesis, instantiated for LEN (`luaV_objlen`, H3's arm) and for a second, stubbed callee. Measure the marginal cost of the second instance.

**Seed.**
- The wrapped cores `T·DUg·T` and `A·f·A`: a call wrapped by the same code on both sides.
- `EOR` (XOR) at the *end* of the string, not in its bulk: the ALU op is the tail of A1's cost, not its body.

## 5. Certificate-checked arms: an untrusted lifter, a verified per-block checker, and the quotients absorbed by the certificate

**Acts on:**
- R1-2 #5 (verified `sym`), R1-1 #5 (verified lifter + Foata) and R1-4 #4 (verified idiom parser): **(c)** plus **(b)**.
- R1-6 #4's `Relabel`: **(c)**.

**The objection.** The coordinator's orbit data names the three perturbations that gcc actually introduces between sibling arms: **branch polarity** (ADD vs SUB), **register renaming** and **scheduling** (MUL vs MULK). Each round-1 L-C1 idea handles only some of them:

- **R1-6 #4's `Relabel`** handles none of them. It has a shift and a positional substitution only.
- **R1-1 #5's Mazurkiewicz/Foata quotient** handles scheduling. It does not handle polarity, which is a block-layout change rather than a trace commutation, and renaming needs a separate α-equivalence.
- **R1-4 #4's idiom grammar** must add a production for each variant; its own statistic ("new idioms per arm") will show this.
- **R1-2 #5's verified `sym`** handles all three. But its soundness proof must cover whatever machinery handles them: normalisation, and merging paths after branches.

**The idea.** Move all of that machinery out of the proof.

- **A certificate** is data produced by an untrusted tool (a script over `objdump`, or a Lean `#eval` heuristic). For each arm it gives:
  - for every *exit* of the arm (every path to the dispatch head), which **kernel edge index** it realises (R1-4 #1's `Fin nEdge`);
  - at every merge point and every call site, the symbolic state: which machine register or stack slot holds which spec-level term, and which Rep facts hold.
- **A verified checker** `check : Arm → Cert → OpSpec → Bool`:
  - it symbolically steps each basic block from its annotated entry, one RV64 instruction at a time;
  - it checks that the result entails the next annotation;
  - it checks that each exit's state is the kernel's write on the claimed edge.

  It does no search, no idiom recognition, no normal-form computation over schedules, and no fixpoint at joins.

For an acyclic arm with no calls, the certificate is just the exit-to-edge map, and the checker *is* `sym`. Certificates carry information only at merges and around callees (idea 4's contracts are instantiated at call sites).

**Core rules.**

- **L-C1 → `check_sound`**, proved once: `check arm cert (spec o) = true → ArmSim o`, dispatch head to dispatch head.
- It composes to `VmSim` through R1-2 #5's first-return-map argument.
- **Per opcode:** `check (arm o) (cert o) (spec o) = true` by `decide`.
  - Polarity, renaming and scheduling change only `cert o`, never the checker or its proof.
  - This is why the orbit question stops mattering: SUB costs what ADD costs, which is a certificate the tool writes.

**What it makes free.**

- The F1 part of `a1-arm-sim`, whatever the orbit structure.
- The idiom-grammar maintenance of R1-4 #4, and the Foata normaliser and its soundness from R1-1 #5.
- Kernel edge indices double as exit labels, so the "some edge with that target" refinement (TESTSET) is checked at the machine level for free.

**What it costs.**

- `check_sound` against Sail is the dominant setup, the same order as R1-2 #5's `sym` soundness. It is smaller by the normaliser, and by whatever `sym` would need at merges.
- **The certificate is per-arm data.** It is generated, never hand-maintained, and checked, never trusted. Its size must be reported in the bake-off: if the coordinator counts it as a per-site table, the idea is disqualified.
- Kernel `decide` time on long arms such as FORLOOP, since `native_decide` is excluded.

**Theories.**

- *Known, complexity theory:* NP as the class of certificate-checkable problems. Cook, "The complexity of theorem-proving procedures", STOC 1971. Finding the correspondence is hard (untrusted), and checking it is easy (verified).
- *Known, accounting:* double-entry bookkeeping (Pacioli, *Summa de arithmetica*, 1494). Each entry is checked for local balance, and the books are correct without anyone recomputing the business. Block annotations are the entries, and the checker is the audit.
- *Known, PL:* translation validation (Pnueli, Siegel, Singerman, TACAS 1998) and verified validators (Tristan and Leroy, "Formal verification of translation validators", POPL 2008). Here the "compiler" is gcc, and the source is the kernel table.
- *Novel:* using the semantic kernel's *edge index* as the exit label in machine-code certificates, so the bytecode footprint law and the machine simulation share one index.

**Pilot.**

- **Cheapest falsifier (no proofs, about a day):**
  - Write `check` as an unverified Lean `def` over the roughly 15 RV64 instructions in the arms.
  - Hand-write certificates for ADD, SUB, MUL and MULK (one per perturbation) and `#eval`.
  - If any of the four needs a new term constructor in the checker, "polarity, renaming and scheduling are absorbed" is false. Then say which perturbation broke it.
  - Also time `decide` on FORLOOP in the kernel.
- **A1 held-out arms ADD, EQI and FORLOOP:** prove `check_sound` for their instruction subset, then report per-arm proof lines (target 0), certificate sizes, and the marginal cost of SUB/BOR/MULK as second instances.
- **H cases:** the H2/H3 arms call `luaV_concat`/`luaV_objlen`, so their certificates carry a contract instantiation at the call site (idea 4). Report them separately.

**Seed.**
- `Dq`/`qD`/`DQ`: one pair, swapped (branch polarity) and case-changed (register renaming).
- `ZYrgXW`/`ZWrQxW`: the same frame with one hole (the ALU op) and a flipped case.

Three surface perturbations of one thing is exactly what a certificate absorbs and a syntactic orbit does not.
