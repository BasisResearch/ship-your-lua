## 1. Swap-keyed, φ-pushed answer atoms: no feasibility filter anywhere

**Acts on:**
- **(c)** R5-R1-1 A3: its "flagged: decided by search" coupling rule, and its
  prediction that FORPREP is "the search case's first instance".
- **(c)** R5-R1-2 A2: "FORPREP: three atoms, `decide` over 64 cases".
- **(c)** R5-R1-3 A's `Φ` and E's order-closure checker E4.
- **(c)** R5-R1-4 B's `feas`/`FeasSound`.
- **(a)** R5-R1-5 #1's "soundness does not need feasibility": this idea makes
  *completeness* hold too, so the free product never fails on an
  infeasible point.
- **(c)** M-sym (R5-R1-1 A2, R5-R1-3 B, R5-R1-4 A): subsumed.

### The idea

Round 1's trees name one atom **per call site**, for example
`o₁ := compare st 0` and `o₂ := compare 0 st`. Two call sites on the same
operands then become two "independent" variables. The free product contains
points no input reaches (`o₁ = lt`, `o₂ = lt`), so round 1 adds a coupling
search or a feasibility filter.

Instead, key each atom by the **unordered pair of operand terms**, with an
orientation bit:
- `key (x, y) = if id x ≤ id y then (⟨x, y⟩, false) else (⟨y, x⟩, true)`;
- the answer is `o` or `o.map Ordering.swap`.

`F64.compare_swap` (`F64.lean:1012`, already proved) is applied *at
atomisation*, once, on both the kernel side and the machine side. The term
ids are positions: `κ.reads` index, constant, or call-node result.

A second normalisation pushes `compare` through φ-terms:
`compare (ite p a b) c = ite p (compare a c) (compare b c)` (`apply_ite`).
The machine needs this because it merges values before comparing them.

**The artefact shows the coupling search is never needed in the pilot:**
- **FORPREP** (`0x8001c16c–c1b8`, the other side at `0x8001ecf4`):
  - `__eqdf2(step, 0)` `bnez` (zero: `0x8001fdf0`, `luaG_runerror`);
  - `__gedf2(step, 0)` `bgtz`;
  - then `__ledf2(limit, init)` `srli 63` **or** `__gedf2(limit, init)`
    `sgtz`, the two joining at `0x8001c1b8` (`beqz a4`).
  - The kernel (`forprepK`, `Semantics.lean:501–503`) tests `beq fs 0`,
    `lt 0 fs`, then `lt fl fi` or `lt fi fl`.
  - Keyed by pair, that is **2 atoms**: `(step, 0)` and `(limit, init)`. So
    16 points, not 64, and no coupling.
- **FORLOOP** (`0x8001e17c–e1bc`, `0x8001edf0`):
  - `__gedf2(step, 0)` `blez`;
  - `__gedf2(limit, idx′)` `not; srli 63` or `__ledf2(limit, idx′)`
    `slti 1`, joined at `0x8001e194`.
  - The kernel tests `lt 0 st`, then `le idx l` or `le l idx`.
  - That is again 2 atoms on 2 pairs.
- **The f2i chain** (for example `0x8001f88c–f8e8`, `F2Iceil`):
  - `floor`;
  - `__eqdf2(n, ⌊n⌋)` `beqz` skips `__adddf3(⌊n⌋, 1.0)`, and the two meet at
    `0x8001f8bc` with `s6 = φ(⌊n⌋, ⌊n⌋+1)`;
  - `__gedf2(s6, −2^63)` `bltz`, `__ledf2(s6, 2^63)` `bgez`;
  - `__fixdfdi`.
  - The kernel (`Arith.lean:89`) has the φ as two *branches*:
    `numberToInteger (add f 1)` or `numberToInteger f`, with
    `le minIntF n` (swapped against the machine).
  - After `apply_ite` and swap keying, the machine's atoms are the kernel's
    atoms. Both sides are then the **same function of the same atoms**, so
    they agree on infeasible points too.
  - The dependency R5-R1-3 E built an order closure for (`⌊n⌋ < −2^63` and
    `⌊n⌋ ≥ 2^63` together) is never consulted.

**Why the filter disappears in general.** A free-product check fails only
where the two sides compute *different* functions of the atoms on points no
input reaches. Two things produce that:
- call-site naming (fixed by keying);
- a kernel written in different primitives from the machine (fixed by one
  bridge per primitive, proved once).

The f2i bridge is R5-R1-2 A3's `f2i_canon`. It rewrites
`flttointeger x m` into the decision function on `compare (n, ⌊n⌋)` and the
range compares. It is the **only** place where order reasoning happens, and
it happens once per mode.

### Core rules

| law | rule (proved once) | decided at a use site by |
|---|---|---|
| L5-comm (compare part) | **`atom_key`**: `F64.compare x y = orient (key x y) (F64.compare (fst (key x y)) (snd (key x y)))`, from `compare_swap` | **computation**: `key` compares closed term ids |
| (new) φ push | **`compare_ite`**: `F64.compare (if p then a else b) c = if p then F64.compare a c else F64.compare b c` (`apply_ite`, one line) | syntactic: applied by `simp only` to every call-node answer whose operand is a φ |
| L5-idiom | R5-R1-5 #2's `relOf R ι : Option Ordering → Bool`, one interpreter, `rfl` per routine | computation on 4 points |
| L5-fork / L5-read′ | **`agree`**: `∀ ō, mach ō = kern ō`, with both sides closed functions of the keyed atoms, checked by a **tree walk** that cases an atom only at the fork that reads it (R5-R1-3 A's walk, not a full `4^k` product) | `decide` on closed data. **Not search.** |
| L5-chain | `f2i_canon` (R5-R1-2 A3), once per mode, with the `floor` premise | rewrite |
| O-ord | none needed: swap keying is the O-ord rule | — |

**Symbolic data.** Floats stay opaque inside the terms whose ids are keyed.
The kernel's `Float.Model` tests reach `F64.compare` through `lt_eq_model`,
`le_eq_model` and `eq_eq_model` (`F64.lean:845–853`, proved).

**M-sym is subsumed.** The group generators of R5-R1-1 A2, R5-R1-3 B and
R5-R1-4 A each reduce to something smaller:
- `σ` (swap) is `atom_key`;
- `ν` (negate the code) and `β` (branch polarity) are what `relOf`
  evaluates: polarity is just which successor the closed Boolean selects;
- `τ` (sign flip) survives only on the spec side (`F64.sub = F64.add ∘ neg`,
  about 15 lines). Its machine side is false as a cost claim (coordinator
  correction).

So the group adds a 40–80 line layer whose per-site saving `relOf` plus
`atom_key` already gives. Also, `β` acts on successors while `σ`/`ν`/`τ` act
on values, so "a group of ≤ 16 elements acting on one set" (R5-R1-3 B) is not
even well-typed without a semidirect product.

### What it makes free

- **Every feasibility obligation in the pilot:** FORLOOP, FORPREP, LT
  (forecast).
- **The f2i chain's correlated atoms at all 20 sites:** no `Φ`, no E4.
- **MOD's sign fix (`0x8001e49c–e4bc`):**
  - `fmod`, then `__gedf2(m, 0)` `blez`, `__ledf2(b, 0)` `bltz`;
  - the atoms `(m, 0)`, `(b, 0)` and lvm.c's `(b, m)` are three pairs;
  - `m = fmod a b` is correlated with `b`, but both sides are the same
    function of those atoms, so no filter.
- **O-ord** at every site (GTI's `__gedf2(x, imm)`, FORLOOP's
  `__adddf3(step, idx)` by `add_comm` in the same keying pass).

### What it costs

- `atom_key` and `orient`, about 30 lines. `compare_ite` is 1 line.
- The term-id assignment is generated data: register positions and
  call-node indices, which `float_paths.py` already enumerates.
- **What gets harder.** A φ-term must be visible as an `ite` in the call
  node's argument. That needs idea 2's join assertion, or the generated
  `f<k>_eq` stating its operand as the φ.

### Theories

- **Canonical forms modulo commutativity / congruence closure** (known):
  Downey, Sethi & Tarjan, "Variations on the common subexpression problem",
  *JACM* 27(4), 1980.
- **Canonical SMILES in cheminformatics** (known): Weininger, Weininger &
  Weininger, "SMILES 2. Algorithm for generation of unique SMILES notation",
  *J. Chem. Inf. Comput. Sci.* 29, 1989. One molecule gets one name whatever
  the traversal order. Here one comparison gets one atom whatever the call
  order.
- **If-lifting in SMT preprocessing** (known **[recall: check a citable
  source, e.g. the SMT-LIB `ite`-lifting passes in Barrett et al.'s CVC
  papers]**).
- **Novel as an application:** the observation that keying removes the
  feasibility layer.

### Pilot

- **Held-out FORPREP:**
  - the float clause is 2 atoms, 16 points;
  - the eqdf2-zero leaf is the error exit, not a `Step`;
  - the join at `0x8001c1b8` is idea 2's cut.
- **Held-out LT (forecast, not read on the ELF):**
  - flt/flt: 1 atom;
  - int/flt with `intfitsf`: `fits` plus 1 atom `(ofI i, f)`;
  - not fitting: the chain atoms after `compare_ite`, plus `icmp (i, fi)`
    and `(f, 0)` (`ltIntFloat`'s `none ⇒ lt zero f`, `Arith.lean:156`).
  - About 6–8 tree leaves per class under the walk.
- **Held-out SUB:** no atoms. This is the control.
- **Refactor FORLOOP:** 2 atoms, 16 points. `model_add_comm` disappears
  into keying.
- **Smallest thing:** `atom_key`, `compare_ite` and `relOf`, plus FORPREP's
  float clause transcribed as two closed functions of `(o_step, o_bound)`.
- **Cheap falsifier:**
  - `decide` (or `#eval`) `∀ o₁ o₂, machFORPREP o₁ o₂ = kernFORPREP o₁ o₂`;
  - the same for the ceil chain after `compare_ite`.
  - If any point fails, keying is insufficient and a coupling is genuinely
    needed. That would vindicate R5-R1-1 A3's search flag.

## 2. Cut-points at the arm DAG's joins: nodal analysis, not path enumeration

**Acts on:**
- **(b)** R5-R1-3 D (Floyd-style assertions on the level-(b) DAG) with
  R5-R1-5 #1 (φ nodes as `OB.ite`). It moves D's mechanism to the **arm**
  side.
- **(c)** R5-R1-1 A3 `run_tree`, R5-R1-2 A2 `run_fork` and R5-R1-4 B
  `ForkTree`. A tree runner removes prefix replay but **reintroduces suffix
  replay after every machine join**.

### The idea

The machine's float paths form a DAG, not a tree. GCC merges them:

| arm | join | what merges there |
|---|---|---|
| FORLOOP | `0x8001e194` | two sign sides (`not; srli 63` of `__gedf2` / `slti 1` of `__ledf2`) merge into `a5`, then one `bnez`, the stores `e1b0–e1b8` and the tail `c240` |
| FORPREP | `0x8001c1b8` | the same shape |
| FORPREP | `0x8001c13c`, `0x8001c154`, `0x8001c16c` | each operand's coercion rejoins the inline path |
| SUB | `0x8001e4ec`/`e4f0` | the only `__subdf3` call site in OP_SUB. There are 2 sites in all of `luaV_execute`, SUB and SUBK. It is reached from `0x8001dfbc` (`beq` → `e4ec`, C float), `0x8001dfd4` (`j` → `e4f0`, C int after `__floatdidf`) and the fall-through from `0x8001e4e8` (B int) |
| f2i ceil | `0x8001f8bc` | `s6 = φ(⌊n⌋, ⌊n⌋+1)` |

A tree runner unfolds each join and runs the suffix once per incoming path:
- **FORLOOP:** the stores and tail on the two back-jump outcomes, 6.3k, plus
  `at_close` 4.3k + 4.2k (ROUND-5 §1.2), are the same suffix twice.
- **SUB:** the call, store and tail three times.
- **LT:** the chains' joins nest.

Put an **assertion** at each join and prove each DAG edge once:
- every in-edge proves the assertion;
- the out-run is proved once *from* the assertion.

The assertions are **not free-form**. They come from the kernel combinator's
own intermediate terms, as named structures:

| combinator | join assertion (named-field `structure`) |
|---|---|
| `opArith` | `Coerced r w x`: register/slot `r` holds `F64` bits of `tonumberns (regs w) = x` |
| `forloopK`/`forprepK` | `SignedBound a5 st x y R`: `a5 ≠ 0 ↔ (if lt 0 st then R x y else R y x)` |
| the f2i chain | `F2IAt s6 n m`: `s6 = if beq n ⌊n⌋ then ⌊n⌋ else ⌊n⌋ + 1` (ceil), and at the exit `Option` result plus `fixdfdi` value |
| `tonumber` operand | `SlotNum (sp+o) v x`: stack slot holds `F64` bits of `tonumber? v = x` |

**A new sharing that L5-share missed.** FORPREP's float clause and FORLOOP's
float clause are the *same* signed-bound shape:
- lvm.c writes `luai_numlt(0, step) ? luai_numlt(flimit, init) : luai_numlt(init, flimit)`
  and `luai_numlt(0, step) ? luai_numle(idx, limit) : luai_numle(limit, idx)`;
- the machine has two compares, a φ, and one test, in both.

So `SignedBound` with the relation `R ∈ {lt, le}` as a parameter is proved
once, and serves both arms. L5-share is false between FOR*'s int and float
clauses, but **true between FORLOOP-float and FORPREP-float**.

### Core rules

| law | rule | decided at a use site by |
|---|---|---|
| L5-fork (refined) | **`run_dag`**: given a cut table `J : List (pc × AsnId)` and, for each DAG edge (cut to cut), a run from the source assertion to the target assertion (or to a leaf's `VmRel`), conclude `SimArmOn o Q`. Stated `∀ (Wp : MachWP M)` with `Wp.run` and `wp_segW`/`wp_callW` (`VsaIris/MachWP.lean`). A join assertion is an intermediate postcondition, so this is sequencing plus a disjunction-free midpoint | the cut table is generated **data**: pcs with ≥ 2 float-path predecessors, from `float_paths.py`'s enumeration. The assertion id comes from a closed per-combinator table. A wrong id fails at the edge proof. **Not search.** |
| L5-read′ | the in-edges into an `opArith` join are exactly the per-operand classes (idea 3) | syntactic |
| (new) `SignedBound` | **`signedBound_sim R`**: the 2-compare-φ-test machine shape simulates `if lt 0 st then R x y else R y x` | instantiated with `R := le` (FORLOOP) or `lt` (FORPREP). Atoms by idea 1 |

**Symbolic data.** Assertions mention kernel terms (`x`, `st`, `⌊n⌋`) and
machine locations. Nothing is enumerated except the atoms at a fork
(idea 1).

### What it makes free

- **The suffix half of the replay.** Prefix replay is removed by any
  tree runner; suffix replay is removed only by cuts. The proof's
  heartbeats become Σ over **DAG edges**, not Σ over paths.
- **FORLOOP:** 4 leaves become 2 cut-to-leaf edges after `e194`. The stores
  and tail run once, and `at_close` drops from about 10.8k to about 5.5k.
- **SUB:** 3 classes reach one call. The call, store and tail run once.
- **The f2i chain:** a cut pair (entry, exit) **is** R5-R1-1 A4's summary
  keyed by entry pc. Combined with idea 1, R5-R1-1 A4's layout certificate
  is unnecessary: the layout only fixes which in-edge is which, and the edge
  proofs are generated segments.
- **Generated at-lemmas:** one per DAG edge rather than per path segment
  variant. `At/Forloop` (26 lemmas, 55.7 s) should shrink. **[forecast]**

### What it costs

- `run_dag` over `MachWP`, about 60 lines.
- The assertion structures, about 4 × 10 lines.
- `signedBound_sim`, about 30 lines.
- **What gets harder.**
  - A join where the merged values are *not* a kernel subterm. For example,
    a register merged from two unrelated computations that the kernel never
    names. Then the assertion vocabulary has no entry, and the edge falls
    back to path unfolding.
  - That case must be reported by name. It is the falsifier below.

### Theories

- **Floyd's inductive assertions at cut-points** (known): Floyd, "Assigning
  meanings to programs", 1967.
- **SSA φ-functions** (known): Cytron et al., "Efficiently computing static
  single assignment form…", *TOPLAS* 13(4), 1991. The join assertion is the
  φ's meaning.
- **Kirchhoff's nodal analysis** (known: Kirchhoff, 1845): a network is
  solved by one equation per *junction*, not per path through it. Cost is
  linear in nodes. Using it as the cost model of an arm proof is novel.
- **Coursed masonry** (novel as an analogy): each course is laid once on a
  level bed, the joint, whatever the courses below did.

### Pilot

- **Refactor FORLOOP:**
  - prefix to `e17c`;
  - two in-edges to `e194`, proving `SignedBound a5 st idx′ l le`;
  - one fork `bnez` (atom-free, since `a5` is the assertion's Bool);
  - 2 leaves.
  - With ideas 1 and 3 the hand part is the `run_dag` call. Target ≤ 6
    lines and ≤ 30k heartbeats.
- **Held-out FORPREP:**
  - the three coercion joins (idea 3);
  - `signedBound_sim lt` at `0x8001c1b8` (the same lemma as FORLOOP);
  - the zero test.
  - **Prediction:** FORPREP's float clause costs ≤ FORLOOP's refactored
    proof plus 3 view lines.
- **Held-out SUB:** one join at `0x8001e4ec`, three in-edges, one out-run.
- **Held-out LT:** joins at each chain's φ and exit **[forecast: count on
  the ELF]**.
- **Level (b):** none. But this is R5-R1-3 D on arms, so a shared
  `run_dag` could serve both.
- **Smallest thing:** `run_dag` plus `SignedBound`, on FORLOOP alone.
- **Cheap falsifier:**
  - a Python pass over `float_paths.py`'s enumeration printing, per
    field, (paths, DAG edges between cuts, joins whose merged register is
    not a kernel subterm);
  - if Σ edges ≈ Σ paths (no joins), or the unnamed-join count is large,
    the idea gives nothing.

### Seed

"cartonnage" (layers glued at seams), "masoner" (courses on mortar beds) and
"sectioplanography" (draw the thing section by section, at its cuts).

## 3. Per-operand coercion views: classes factor by operand, not by tuple

**Acts on:**
- **(c)** the per-tuple class tables of R5-R1-3 A0 (`classTab` over
  `Heads`, "6³ = 216 rows"), R5-R1-4 C (`κ.shapes`), R5-R1-5 #5
  (`class_cover`), and R5-R1-1 A5 (`κ.classify`), as applied to FORPREP and
  `opArith`.
- **(a)** R5-R1-5 #4's `NumDom.conv`: the conversion becomes a *view per
  operand macro*, not a property of the domain pair.

### The idea

`forprepK`'s float clause reads all three operands through `tonumber?`
(`Semantics.lean:499`). Each is flt, int (converted) or a numeric string.
So per-tuple classes number `27 − 3 = 24` (the 3 `[.int, _, .int]` tuples go
to the int clause), plus the coerced-limit tuples of the int clause.

The machine does **not** fork per tuple. lvm.c's `forprep` applies the
`tonumber` macro three times in sequence, and the ELF shows three
independent local forks:

| operand | test | fast side | slow side | rejoin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| limit | `lbu 24(a5)`, `== 19` | `ld 16(a5)` → `sd 40(sp)` | `luaV_tonumber_(obj, sp+40)` at `0x8001e2b0`, `bne a0,1` → `luaG_forerror` | `0x8001c13c` |
| step | `== 19` | → `sd 48(sp)` | `0x8001e2e8` (`sp+48`) | `0x8001c154` |
| init | `== 19` | → `sd 32(sp)` | `0x8001e31c` (`sp+32`) | `0x8001c16c` |

- The φ is a **stack slot**: the fast side stores the payload, and the slow
  side's callee writes the same slot through its out-pointer.
- `luaV_tonumber_` has exactly 4 call sites in `luaV_execute`: these 3 and
  `0x8001f9fc`.
- The `opArith` arms are the same pattern with `tonumberns` (no strings, no
  callee). In SUB, B's coercion lands in `s4` and C's in `a1` before the one
  call (idea 2's table).

So **the class is a product of per-operand views**:
- one lemma per lvm.c **macro** (`tonumber`, `tonumberns`; and `tointegerns`
  for the bitwise family);
- applied once per operand, joined at idea 2's cut.

The arm never enumerates tuples.

### Core rules

| law | rule | decided at a use site by |
|---|---|---|
| L5-read′ (per operand) | **`tonumber_view`**: from `VmRel`, the operand's slot `v`, and the macro's machine fragment (tag test; inline load and store to slot `o`, or the `luaV_tonumber_` call with out-pointer `sp+o`), reach the rejoin pc with `SlotNum (sp+o) v x` iff `v.tonumber? = some (.flt x _)`, else the error exit. Proved **once**. The callee is a summary whose string branch carries the `Str2dFrom` premise | syntactic: the macro is fixed per combinator operand. The rejoin pc and slot offset are generated data, checked against the image |
| L5-read′ (per operand) | **`tonumberns_view`**: the same for flt / int (`__floatdidf`, `Floatdidf.spec`) / fail (MMBIN edge) into a register | syntactic |
| combination | the class is the conjunction of views, which is idea 2's join assertion `Coerced`; the MMBIN or error exit is the view's failure leaf | none: conjunction is not a case split |
| memory φ | the out-pointer write uses `FrameMeta.memFrame_of_chain` (one `decide` on the frame's write log) and `bridgeOfSegFull` for the callee | existing tools |

**Symbolic data.** `x` is symbolic. The view says only "the slot holds
`F64` bits of `x`". The tuple of constructors is never cased at the arm.

**The honest exception: LT.** `LTnum`'s mixed case is **not** a coercion:
`ltIntFloat` runs a different algorithm (`intfitsf`, then a cast or the f2i
chain). So LT keeps a per-pair split: flt/flt, int/flt, flt/int, error.
That is 4 classes, still not a tuple table, because the order kernel reads
only 2 operands. Views apply to each class's float operand.

### What it makes free

- **FORPREP's 24 coercion tuples** become 3 view applications.
  - The machine's 2³ = 8 coercion paths times the 4 compare outcomes (32
    leaves under a tree runner) become 3 + 3 + 3 view edges plus idea 2's
    signed bound.
- **The 8 ADDI-like and 4 all-float fields:** two (or one)
  `tonumberns_view` plus one call. DIV and POW (both operands converted)
  stop being "5–9 float paths".
- **O-conv** (5/10 sampled) and **O-inv** (4/10).
- **The 3 extra `luaV_tonumber_` sites** share one view lemma.

### What it costs

- Two view lemmas, about 30 lines each.
- One `luaV_tonumber_` callee summary with an out-parameter (needed by
  FORPREP regardless).
- `SlotNum` / `Coerced` structures.
- **What gets harder.**
  - The views assume the operand fragments are **independent**: each
    fragment touches only its own slot. That is a frame fact per fragment,
    one `decide`.
  - If GCC interleaves two operands' fragments, a view's run is no longer
    contiguous and must be split at the interleave. It does not happen in
    FORPREP or SUB as read; **check the bitwise family**.

### Theories

- **Factor graphs / conditional independence** (known): Pearl,
  *Probabilistic Reasoning in Intelligent Systems*, 1988; Kschischang,
  Frey & Loeliger, "Factor graphs and the sum-product algorithm", *IEEE
  Trans. IT* 47(2), 2001. A joint over independent variables is a product of
  local factors; enumerating the joint is the anti-pattern.
- **Separation of variables for PDEs** (known, e.g. Fourier 1822): a
  solution as a product of one-variable solutions.
- **Coercive subtyping** (known): Luo, "Coercive subtyping", *J. Logic
  Comput.* 9(1), 1999. Each operand's coercion is applied where it occurs,
  not by a case analysis over all argument types.

- **Smallest thing:** `tonumberns_view` and SUB.
- **Cheap falsifier:** done for FORPREP and SUB above (read on the ELF).

## 4. Value, not position: `round_scale` plus a magnitude-guarded `round_rto`

**Acts on:**
- **(a)** the five M-odd variants: R5-R1-1 A1 `odd`, R5-R1-2 A1 `ro`,
  R5-R1-3 C `StEq`, R5-R1-4 D `Stamped`/`rto`, R5-R1-5 #3 `rto`.
- **(c)** their position bookkeeping: R5-R1-1 A1 `cancel_le_one`, R5-R1-4 D
  rule 6 (`stamp_shl`, "needs the classic cancellation lemma"), R5-R1-3 C1's
  `k + 1 ≤ target − e` side conditions per regime, and R5-R1-2 A1/A5's
  `d ≤ 3` / `4 ≤ d ≤ 56` region split.

### Same mechanism, five names; the cheapest member

| variant | form | extra layer over the core |
|---|---|---|
| R5-R1-1 A1 `odd`, R5-R1-2 A1 `ro`, R5-R1-5 #3 `rto` | a function `n / 2^k ||| [n % 2^k ≠ 0]`, rewritten | none: **cheapest** |
| R5-R1-4 D `Stamped v X k m` | a predicate wrapping `v = rto_k X` plus a margin | a wrapper with no new content |
| R5-R1-3 C `StEq k n m` | a congruence relation with closure rules | relational. It needs `round_congr` with `log2` equalities. It is more general (`scale` and `rsub` are free), but it is relation chasing where a rewrite suffices |

So the cheapest member is the **function** `rto` with directed rewrites.

### The idea

Every round-1 variant proves "rounding after the collapse equals rounding
the exact value" **at a position**: the machine's rounding bit, after its
`clz` normalisation. That forces the cancellation and margin bookkeeping.

But `F64.round` is a function of the **value** `n·2^e`, not of its
representation. Two facts follow.

1. **`round_scale`** (exact, unconditional):
   `round s (n * 2^j) (e − j) ff = round s n e ff`.
   - Proof: `target` is unchanged (`log2_shiftLeft`, `F64.lean:555`). The
     `shr` by `T − e + j` of `n·2^j` returns `n`'s triple. The mixed branch is
     exact with `q < 2^53`.
   - **Checked:** 0 failures in 200,000 random `(n < 2^70, e, j < 20)`.
   - So the machine's `clz` left shift, and *where* it puts the top bit, is
     invisible: no cancellation lemma.

2. **`round_rto`** (guarded by magnitude, not by position):
   `2^54 ≤ rto n k ∨ e + k + 2 ≤ target n e → round s (rto n k) (e + k) ff = round s n e ff`.
   - **Checked:** 0 failures on the guarded cases.
   - The unguarded statement fails often, so the guard is load-bearing.
   - The second disjunct covers the subnormal clamp.

The machine side needs **one** stage equation, L5-stage's checked `roundPack`
restated **exactly**: "normalise by `clz`, `rne3`, carry, pack of `v` at the
semiraw exponent `E`" `= round s v (E − 1078) ff`, for any `v > 0`.
- `round_scale` makes the normalisation shift disappear inside it.
- `rb`/`st` vanish, because the 3 work bits are *in* `v`.
- R5-R1-3 C0's residual absorption is then never needed for add/sub.

The add/sub core is then three rewrites and one linear fact:

```
roundPack (X ± rto Y d)                            -- machine, after srsMachine (k = d, 1 ≤ d ≤ 63)
 = round s (X ± rto Y d) (e₁ − 3) ff                -- roundPack, restated exactly
 = round s (rto (X·2^d ± Y) d) (e₁ − 3) ff          -- rto_add_even / rto_sub_even  (2 ∣ X: X = m₁ <<< 3)
 = round s (X·2^d ± Y) (e₂ − 3) ff                  -- round_rto, guard: 2^54 ≤ X − rto Y d
 = normalize (sgnApply … align … ± …) (min e₁ e₂)   -- F64.add's finite clause, after round_scale by 2^3
```

**The guard is one linear fact, the same in every regime.** With `X ≥ 2^55`
(hidden bit at 55), `Y < 2^56` and `d ≥ 2`: `rto Y d ≤ Y / 4 < 2^54`, so
`X − rto Y d > 2^54`. The cases outside it are not regimes either:
- `d = 1`: `rto Y 1 = Y / 2` exactly, since `Y` is even (`rto_exact`);
- `d = 0`: no shift;
- `d ≥ 56`: `rto Y d = 1` (`rto_big`), and the guard holds.

**Checked end to end:** 600,000 random normalised pairs, `1 ≤ d < 60`, both
add and subtract, **including** `d = 1` massive cancellation. In every
case, `round (X ± rto (Y) d) (e₁ − 3) = round (exact) e₂`, with 0 failures.
`rto_add_even` and `rto_sub_even` were checked exhaustively on `X < 64`
even, `Y < 256`, `k ≤ 5`: 0 failures. With **odd** `X`, `rto_sub_even` fails
23,632 times, so the evenness side condition is load-bearing (confirming
R5-R1-4 D's hand derivation).

### Core rules

| law | rule | decided at a use site by |
|---|---|---|
| L5-stage (SRS) | `srsMachine x k = rto x.toNat k`, `1 ≤ k ≤ 63` (all five variants have it) | syntactic match on the node; `1 ≤ d` from the dominating guard, `omega` on literals |
| (new) | **`round_scale`** | rewrite, unconditional |
| open hand-off | **`round_rto`** with the magnitude guard | rewrite. The guard is `omega` over `X ≥ 2^55`, `Y < 2^56` and `rto Y d ≤ Y / 2^d + 1`, with `Y / 2^d ≤ Y / 4` from `d ≥ 2` (one `Nat.div_le_div_left`). `2^d` stays an atom |
| L5-stage (round+pack) | **`roundPack_exact`**: `roundPack s E v = round s v (E − 1078) ff` | proved once, from L5-stage's checked identity plus `round_scale`. At the 6 round sites (5 in `__subdf3`): syntactic, at each site's node |
| core algebra | `rto_add_even`, `rto_sub_even`, `rto_exact`, `rto_big` | rewrite; `2 ∣ X` from `X = m <<< 3` |

**No rule is decided by search.** Mantissas, `d` and the exponents stay
symbolic.

### What it makes free

- **The cancellation lemma, the margin bookkeeping and the regime split.**
  They are all gone. The `clz` stage needs no semantic lemma beyond "the
  machine's normalise feeds `roundPack`". `__clzdi2`'s `log2` relation is
  absorbed by `round_scale`, since the shift amount never appears in the
  final statement.
- **`__subdf3`:** the same chain with `rto_sub_even`, at its own 5 SRS and 5
  round sites. The per-routine cost is node matching, which is a second
  instantiation, not "free" (agreeing with the coordinator's correction).
- **`__floatdidf`:** `ofInt = normalize i 0 false`; one `rto` and
  `roundPack_exact`.

### What it costs

- `round_scale`, about 25 lines (three `round` branches).
- `round_rto`, about 50 lines (`shr_rto` as in R5-R1-2 A1, plus `log2_rto`).
- The four `rto` algebra lemmas, about 60.
- `roundPack_exact` from the checked identity, about 40.
- Shared with all variants: the 14 reloads (M-spill) and the node match.
- **Cheaper than round 1:** no `cancel_le_one`, no `stamp_shl`, no
  per-regime C1 instances.

### Theories

- **Round-to-odd** (known): Boldo & Melquiond, "Emulation of FMA and
  correctly rounded sums: proved algorithms using rounding to odd", *IEEE
  Trans. Computers* 57(4), 2008.
- **Flocq's rounding as a function on ℝ, independent of representation**
  (known): Boldo & Melquiond, "Flocq", ARITH 2011. `round_scale` is
  `F64.round`'s instance of that.
- **Dimensional analysis / scale invariance** (known): Buckingham, "On
  physically similar systems", *Phys. Rev.* 4, 1914. The observable depends
  only on the dimensionless quantity, never on the unit chosen. Here the
  rounding depends on `n·2^e`, never on how the machine scaled `n`.
- **Novel as an application:** replacing a *position* margin by a
  *magnitude* guard.

### Pilot

- **Held-out `__adddf3`:**
  - the finite clause by the chain above;
  - the 8 special clauses by unpack and `decide` on `Cls`.
  - Measure: no lemma mentions `clz`, `cancel` or a `d`-regime.
- **Held-out `__subdf3`:** the same, plus the exact-zero leaf
  (`normalize … false` gives `+0`).
- **Arms:** none.
- **Smallest thing:** `round_scale` and `round_rto` in `Nat` (about 75
  lines). Go/no-go: they close under the default budget.
- **Cheap falsifier:**
  - an `#eval` of `roundPack_exact` on L5-stage's 6,070 + 1,210 vectors,
    restated without `rb`/`st`, and **with `v` not pre-normalised**;
  - R5-R1-5 #3's node invariant `pre-round node = X ± rto Y d` on
    `SpecTest`'s 2,900 pairs.

### Seed

"quiapo" (a floating plant rides at the same height whatever the depth:
scale invariance), "goldstone" (copper flecks matter by presence, not
place: the sticky as magnitude, not position), and "Burdigalian" (one
geological stage, one stage equation).

## 5. Limb stages: `umul_ppmm` and `udiv_qrnnd` over the existing soft-int batteries

**Acts on:**
- **(c)** R5-R1-1 A1 §4 ("`__muldf3`/`__divdf3`: the same `round_odd`
  applies once the product's low half is collapsed"), R5-R1-3 C ("`__muldf3`
  … one derivation"; "`__divdf3`: C0"), R5-R1-5 #3 ("`__muldf3` … R3a at
  another width [CHECK]").
- **(d)** where none of round 1 went: the double-word core.

### The idea

The ELF has no multiply or divide instructions in these routines (0
`mul`/`mulhu`/`divu`/`remu` in `__muldf3` and `__divdf3`). Instead:
- **`__muldf3`** (`0x8002e5c0–e628`) builds the 106-bit product from **four
  `__muldi3` calls** on 32-bit halves. Then it adds the cross terms with a
  carry detected by `bgeu a5,t3` (`0x8002e618`), which adds `1 << 32`. This
  is soft-fp's generic `umul_ppmm` **[recall: `longlong.h` C fallback]**.
- **`__divdf3`** makes **three rounds** of `__udivdi3`, `__muldi3`,
  `__umoddi3` (`0x8002e154–e2a8`), plus two `__clzdi2`. This is
  `udiv_qrnnd`'s C fallback (`__udiv_qrnnd_c`, Knuth's two-digit step with
  the q̂ correction) **[recall: check against `longlong.h`]**.

Round 1 priced these routines by their rounding, which `rto` handles. But
the **per-routine cost is the limb algebra**:
- "these partial products with this carry fix equal `a·b`";
- "this q̂/r̂ correction yields `⌊n/d⌋` and `n mod d`".

These are the multiply and divide analogues of the SRS stage: a fixed
idiom, inlined, with one closed-form meaning. Name them as two stages.

### Core rules

| law | rule | decided at a use site by |
|---|---|---|
| (new) `umul_ppmm` stage | **`umul_ppmm_eq`**: the 4-call block with the carry fix computes `(hi, lo)` with `hi·2^64 + lo = a·b`, for `a, b < 2^64`, from `Muldi3Spec` (existing battery) for each partial product | syntactic match of the block (a node or a cut pair from idea 2). The `Nat` identity `(a₁·2^32 + a₀)(b₁·2^32 + b₀) = …` is proved once by `ring`-free `Nat` lemmas (`Nat.mul_add`, `Nat.add_mul`) and the carry case `omega` with **literal** `2^32`, `2^64` |
| (new) `udiv_qrnnd` stage | **`udiv_qrnnd_eq`**: under the normalisation precondition (`d`'s top bit set, `n₁ < d`), the 3-round block returns `q = ⌊(n₁·2^64 + n₀)/d⌋`, `r = … mod d`, from `DivSpec`'s `__udivdi3`/`__umoddi3` facts | syntactic match. The q̂ correction is Knuth's "at most 2 corrections" lemma, proved once in `Nat` |
| L5-stage (sticky) | `__muldf3`: `lo ≠ 0` OR'd into `hi`'s low bit is `rto`. `__divdf3`: `r ≠ 0` is the sticky | idea 4's rules |

**Symbolic data.** All limbs are symbolic. Only the limb width (`2^32`) is
literal, so `omega` sees linear facts with literal coefficients.

### What it makes free

- **The cores of `__muldf3` and `__divdf3`:** MUL, MULK, DIV, DIVK, POW's
  fast path (`b == 2` is `__muldf3`), IDIV's `luai_numidiv`, and MOD's
  `fmod` path if newlib's `fmod` uses them **[unchecked]**.
- **`Muldi3Spec`/`DivSpec` reuse:** the CLAUDE.md table lists them as "here
  (WHILE addresses)", to be regenerated at Lua addresses. The 4 + 9 call
  sites become summary applications.
- **`__fixdfdi`/`__floatdidf`** need no limb stage. This is the control.

### What it costs

- `umul_ppmm_eq`, about 40 lines. `udiv_qrnnd_eq`, about 100, the hard one
  (Knuth D's correction bound).
- Regenerating the soft-int batteries at the Lua ELF's addresses.
- Level (a) for `__muldf3`/`__divdf3` is still missing (ROUND-5 §1.5), so
  the stage is matched on the ELF's blocks first.
- **What gets harder.**
  - `udiv_qrnnd`'s precondition (normalised divisor) is established by the
    preceding `clz` shift.
  - That is a position fact. `round_scale` (idea 4) does not remove it,
    because it is about the *division's* correctness, not the rounding.

### Theories

- **Multi-precision arithmetic** (known): Knuth, *TAOCP* Vol. 2, §4.3.1,
  Algorithms M and D, with the q̂ correction theorem.
- **Babylonian place-value multiplication with partial-product tables**
  (known as history: Neugebauer, *The Exact Sciences in Antiquity*, 1957).
  The limb identity is the sexagesimal schoolbook method in radix `2^32`.
- **Novel as an application:** treating the inlined limb idioms as stages
  beside SRS, `rne3` and pack.

### Pilot

- **Not in the held-out set** (level (b) is `__adddf3`/`__subdf3`, and the
  arms consume `SoftFloatSpec`). It is a cost warning for the next round.
- **Smallest thing:** `umul_ppmm_eq` stated over `Nat`, plus an `#eval`
  over a Lean transcription of `0x8002e5c0–e628` on 10^5 random `(a, b)`.
- **Cheap falsifier:** the same `#eval`. If the transcription disagrees
  with `a·b`, the block is not plain `umul_ppmm` (for example, it folds in
  the implicit-bit shifts), and the stage boundary must move.

### Seed

"butchery" (quarter the carcass: split operands into 32-bit limbs) and
"cardiotoxic" (the hidden cost inside "`__muldf3` comes by the same rule").
