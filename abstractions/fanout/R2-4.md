## Answers to the two hard questions first (they shape the five ideas)

**Q1: should `Step`/`LuaSem` be redefined as data, or should the readable inductive stay?**

I side with redefinition, for three reasons.

1. **"Proved equal once" is not once.** Keeping the inductive and proving it equal to a derived kernel is a per-rule obligation, and it recurs with every rule every later fragment adds. That is exactly the rising cost the census measures. It moves the cost; it does not remove it.
2. **Non-determinism is not the danger it looks like.** The objection "F2's `next` order is non-deterministic, so a function cannot express it" is answered by the statement shape (idea 2). Both headline theorems are *iffs* against a deterministic machine (`BcSem p out ↔ Halts c out 0`). A semantics with a genuine `choose` would make the `→` half false. So every relation must already be functional given the `Host`, and the function-shaped encodings lose nothing.
3. **The real threat to the round-1 consensus is value-dependent footprints (idea 3), not non-determinism.** Every applicative-kernel proposal (R1-3 #1, R1-4 #1, R1-5 #1, R1-6 #1) fixes the read set from `w` alone. That breaks on the first F2 table opcode. The fix is to make the kernel language monadic for heap reads and keep it applicative for register reads.

**Q2: which idea actually reduces A1, and what is the cheapest falsifier?**

- **The three held-out arms.** `ADD`, `EQI` and `FORLOOP` sit in three different families. So orbit, template and diff ideas (R1-3 #2, R1-5 #4, R1-6 #4, and my idea 4) show *nothing* on them, only on the second member of a family.
- **What reduces every arm** is the frame and representation half: the lens (R1-3 #5) or the tether (R1-5 #5), made robust to allocation and GC (my idea 5). A verified lifter (R1-2 #5, R1-1 #5, R1-4 #4) also reduces every arm, but its setup is the largest, and one arm cannot validate it.
- **Cheapest falsifiers**, all runnable before any Lean is written:
  - (i) the block-level homology statistic on the ELF (idea 4);
  - (ii) whether `OP_CONCAT`'s arm reaches `luaC_step`, and whether that writes the header of a live object (idea 5);
  - (iii) for a lifter, the number of *lifter* cases that `EQI` and `FORLOOP` add after `ADD`. If that number does not fall towards 0 by the third arm, stop.

## 1. Tombstoned kill edges: CONCAT's and CALL's garbage as erasures, not values

**Acts on:**
- **(b)** combines R1-1 #3 (X-propagating registers) with R1-6 #5 (low-equivalence as the representation relation);
- **(c)** objects to R1-5 #1, R1-6 #1/#2, and the deterministic-δ shape of R1-4 #1 on H2.

**The objection.** SUITE H2 fixes `CONCAT`'s write set as the whole range `R[A..A+B-1]`. The round-1 kernels split two ways:

- R1-5 #1 (`outs = [(pc+1, [A])]`) and R1-6 #2 (`fp = (range A B, [A])`) declare `{A}`. Then L-B1's "every register outside `wr` is unchanged" is **false**.
- R1-4 #1 and R1-3 #1 declare the range. Their `δ` must then return a `Vector Value` for every slot in it, i.e. *specific* garbage values.

`luaV_concat`'s garbage is intricate **[check against lvm.c ~640–690]**:

- `tostring` rewrites integer operands into strings *in their slots*.
- An empty operand takes a two-element pass (`isemptystr(top-1)` / `isemptystr(top-2)`), which leaves a partial result in an intermediate slot.
- Otherwise the pass collects as many operands as possible.

So the slot contents depend on the *emptiness pattern* of the operands. A deterministic kernel must either transcribe all of that, or lie. If it transcribes it, the A1 `CONCAT` arm must then *prove* those slot contents against the machine, including the in-place `lua_integer2str` results. That is a large, useless obligation, since `luac` never reads those slots.

`CALL` already has the same problem, solved differently:
- `Step.callPrint` leaves the registers above the results unchanged, which is false of the machine;
- `Fragment.keepMask`, a CALL-only special case in the analysis, and `cbcSem_iff` repair it.

Two opcodes now carry garbage, with two different repairs.

**The idea.** An edge becomes a triple `(target, defs, kills)`, and registers hold `Value⊥` (`Option Value`). On `kills` the kernel writes ⊥, a *tombstone*. The kernel stays a function, so L-B2 stays definitional. Garbage is an **erasure** in the coding-theory sense: its *position* is known and its *content* is not.

- `CONCAT A B`: defs `{A}`, kills `A+1 … A+B-1`.
- `CALL A B C`: defs `A … A+C-2` (the nil results), kills `[A+C-1, 256)`.
- Everything else: `kills = ∅`.

`keepMask` is deleted: the must-analysis sweep becomes uniform, `st[t] ∩= (st[pc] \ kills) ∪ defs`.

Every read is strict: reading ⊥ is stuck. This has to include the rules whose premises are not a positive `= .int x` match:
- `EQ` (`decide (R[A] = R[B])`: two tombstones would compare *equal*, which is non-monotone);
- `NOT`, `TEST` and `TESTSET` (`isFalse`);
- `MOVE`;
- `print`'s `args`.

R1-1 #3 needed a "positive premises" convention and a uniform tactic. Written in a kernel language whose `rd` node is strict (R1-1 #1 / R1-4 #1), strictness is structural.

**Core rules.**

- **L-B1 with kills**, one theorem over the kernel language. Suppose the two states agree on `reads w`, pc and out. Then they are both stuck, or both take the same edge `(t, D, K)`. Their successors agree on `D`, both are `none` on `K` (so they agree on `D ∪ K`), and both are unchanged outside `D ∪ K`.
- **Definite-initialisation soundness becomes one certain-answer theorem:**
  `Supported p → (BcSem⊥ p out ↔ ∀ g, BcSem_g p out)`.
  - `BcSem⊥` starts from all-⊥ registers, which is the truth at `luaV_execute`'s entry.
  - `g` ranges over completions of the tombstones.
  - `bcSemFrom_iff` and `cbcSem_iff` become instances. There is no CALL-specific lemma, because CALL is no longer special.
- **The A1 representation is a relation, not a function** (from R1-6 #5): `Rep m s :↔ ∀ r v, s.regs r = some v → slot m r encodes v`.
  - A tombstoned register is represented by *any* machine slot.
  - The `CONCAT` arm proves only slot A's value (`luaV_concat`'s contract on the result) and that `L->top` is restored.
  - The `CALL` arm proves nothing about what `print` leaves on the stack.

**What it makes free.**
- The H2 faithfulness trap. The write set is the range, and no garbage value is specified.
- `keepMask` and `cbcSem_iff`'s separate proof.
- At A1: every clause about slots above a `CALL`'s results or inside a `CONCAT` range. This clause type will recur with `VARARG`, multi-result `CALL` and `SETLIST` in F2/F3.

**What it costs.**
- `Value⊥` in `BcSem`'s state is a TCB change. The headline `BcSem` should then be *defined* as `BcSem⊥` from the all-⊥ entry state, which is more faithful than today's all-nil `State.init`.
- Kernel validations (`decide` on `f4_strlite`) now run over `Option Value` registers. Check that reduction does not regress.
- Where it breaks: a later rule that must *observe* garbage on purpose. No F1–F4 opcode does. Lua's `VARARG` copies declared values only **[check]**.

**Theories.**
- *Known, information theory.* Elias's binary erasure channel: erasures are symbols whose loss is *known at a known position*, unlike errors. A code of minimum distance d corrects d−1 erasures but only ⌊(d−1)/2⌋ errors (P. Elias, "Coding for noisy channels", IRE Convention Record 1955 **[check]**). Modelling garbage as erasures, not as unknown values, is what makes the kill set static and the proof uniform.
- *Known, distributed databases.* Tombstones in replicated stores and CRDTs: a delete writes a marker that merges deterministically instead of removing information (M. Shapiro, N. Preguiça, C. Baquero, M. Zawirski, "Conflict-free replicated data types", SSS 2011 **[check]**).
- *Known, databases.* Certain answers over incomplete databases (Imieliński and Lipski, JACM 1984), carried over from R1-1 #3.
- *Novel.* Making the kill set a *static component of the edge*, so that the dataflow analysis, L-B1 and the A1 representation all read the same field. CALL's special case disappears into it.

**Pilot.**
- **H2:** `CONCAT` with kills. Expect 0 lines for L-B1 and definite initialisation, and a clean A1 statement.
- **H1, H3, H4:** strict reads, no kills, 0 extra lines.
- **R1:** port `CALL` to kills, delete `keepMask`, and derive `cbcSem_iff` from the certain-answer theorem. Measure those lines against the incumbent.
- **Smallest build:** the edge triple, strict `rd`, and the L-B1-with-kills theorem, over `MOVE`, `EQ` (the non-monotone trap), `CALL` and `CONCAT`.
- **Falsifier:** count the rules whose premise must be *rewritten* to be strict. If one needs a positive observation of ⊥, the idea fails there.

**Seed.** `UX05`: an X beside a 0, a known-unknown next to a value. `ONO`: defined / not defined, read the same both ways.

## 2. Host-indexed determinism: every unspecified choice is a `Host` function of the semantic history

**Acts on:**
- **(c)** objects to R1-3 #3's mitigation for F2 ("`Prog` can grow a `choose` node; determinism is lost only for arms that use it"), and to the same worry in R1-6 #1;
- **(a)** extends R1-3 #3, R1-4 #2 and R1-1 #2 (the rulebook, call-tree and skeleton forms of L-A1) to F2/F3 with no new generic machinery.

**The objection.** Look at `Lua/Theorems.lean`:

- `vm_refinement_Statement` is `BcSem p out ↔ Halts c out 0`, plus "divergence gives no output".
- `compile_refinement_Statement` is `LuaSem s out ↔ BcSem p out`.

The machine is deterministic. If `BcSem` had a `choose` node, for example for `next` order, it would have outputs the machine lacks, and the `→` half would be false. So a non-deterministic node does not merely cost the determinism theorem: **it is unprovable against the headline statements.** Every relation must already be *functional given `Host`*.

The project already has the right mechanism for this. `Host.showBuiltin`, the `function: 0x…` rendering, is an implementation choice the semantics does not invent. It is a parameter that the refinement statements instantiate with the binary's layout.

**The idea.** Make it a rule that no relation ever branches non-deterministically. Every unspecified choice is a field of `Host`, a *function of the semantic history* that both layers can compute:

- `next` order: `H.nextOrder : TableHist → List Key`, where `TableHist` is the sequence of inserts and deletes of that table plus the allocation trace that fixes the addresses of pointer keys **[check: Lua 5.4 hashes table/function keys by address; `luai_makeseed` mixes a stack address, a function address and `time(NULL)`]**.
- `tostring` of a table or closure: `H.showAddr : AllocTrace → ObjId → String`.
- `math.random` without a seed, `os.time`: an *input tape* in `Host`.

Each semantic run determines its own history, so each choice is `H.f (history so far)`. In a rulebook (R1-3 #3) or call-tree (R1-4 #2) this is an ordinary Lean function application inside `rule`. It is not a new node, so `Graph_iff_run` and `Graph.det` are unchanged.

Layer A then instantiates `H` from the binary: its hash function, its seed, and its allocator. What Layer A must prove per choice is that the binary's choice *is* that function of the history. That is one callee contract (`luaH_next`) per kind of choice, not one per program.

**Core rules.**
- **L-A1, unchanged**, for every `H`: `LuaSem H c out ↔ ∃ fuel, run H fuel c = some out`. Determinism is per `H`, which is all the iff needs.
- **Layer B's statement is uniform in `H`** (∀ H). The compiled code must consume history the same way. It does, because `lcode` emits one `TFORCALL`→`next` per source iteration **[check]**, and TV sees the same table histories.
- **One new obligation per choice kind at Layer A** (a `Host` contract): the binary's `next` returns `H.nextOrder hist`.

**What it makes free.** It keeps *all* of round 1's function-shaped proposals valid through F2/F3:
- the rulebook, the call tree and the skeleton;
- the kernels, where L-B2 needs a function.

It also removes the only argument for keeping inductive relations "in case non-determinism arrives". Without it, R1-3 #3's `choose` node would be added and then found unprovable at the iff.

**What it costs.**
- `Host` grows history-indexed fields. The *history* (allocation trace, per-table edit log) becomes part of the semantic state. That is a real enlargement, and footprint (L-B1) must treat it as a read resource for exactly the opcodes that consult `Host`.
- Where it breaks: a binary choice that depends on something *not* determined by the semantic history, for example GC timing. If `next` order changes after a rehash triggered by collection-dependent sizes, or if weak tables are cleared by GC, the history must include GC pacing. Pacing is a function of allocated byte counts **[check lgc.c `luaE_setdebt` / `GCdebt`]**, so it is still deterministic, but then `Host` must model bytes per allocation.
- The honest fallback: a fragment whose observable depends on GC timing (weak tables, finalizers) needs the GC schedule as a history field.

**Theories.**
- *Known, logic.* Skolemization: `∀x ∃y φ` becomes `∀x φ(x, f(x))` (T. Skolem, 1920 **[check]**). "The semantics chooses" becomes "a function supplied from outside chooses".
- *Known, economics.* Arrow–Debreu state-contingent commodities: uncertainty is removed by indexing every good by a state of the world, after which markets are deterministic (K. Arrow, "The role of securities in the optimal allocation of risk-bearing", Rev. Econ. Stud. 1964; G. Debreu, *Theory of Value*, 1959, ch. 7 **[check]**). `Host` is the state of the world, and the history is the contingency.
- *Known, probabilistic semantics.* Kozen's first semantics runs a *deterministic* program on a random sequence supplied as input (D. Kozen, "Semantics of probabilistic programs", JCSS 22, 1981 **[check]**).
- *Known, refinement.* Prophecy variables resolve future non-determinism by an oracle fixed in advance (M. Abadi and L. Lamport, "The existence of refinement mappings", TCS 82, 1991).
- *Novel.* Deriving the ban on non-determinism from the headline statement's *shape* (an iff against a deterministic machine) instead of adopting it as a style.

**Pilot.**
- **H1–H4** make no choice: the pilot shows 0 cost, which is the point, since nothing grows.
- **Smallest build:**
  - add a `Host`-oracle call to R1-3 #3's toy rulebook (a mock `next` over a finite-map "table" with an edit log);
  - confirm that `Graph_iff_run` and `Graph.det` go through *unchanged*;
  - then write the same mock with a `choose` node and exhibit the failing `→` direction of a toy iff against a deterministic reference.
- **Falsifier (ELF, no Lean):** read `luaH_next`, `mainposition` and `luai_makeseed` in the build, and list every input to the iteration order. If any input is not a function of the per-table edit log plus the allocation trace (for example the C stack depth at the time of the call), `Host` needs that input, and the idea weakens.

**Seed.** `dy` three times (`Tdy`, `Pdy`, `sdy`): three throws of a die, each fixed by the letter before it. The dice are loaded by the history.

## 3. Two-speed footprints: static register reads, adaptive heap reads (the kernel language must be monadic where F2 needs it)

**Acts on:**
- **(c)** objects to the static-read-set kernels of R1-3 #1, R1-4 #1, R1-5 #1 and R1-6 #1 at F2/F3;
- **(b)** combines R1-5 #2 (query monad and certificate lemma) with R1-1 #1 (a continuation-style μ whose register indices come only from word fields).

**The objection.** R1-4 #1's `δ : … → List Value → …` receives `(S.rd w).map s.regs`, and `rd : Word → List Nat`. R1-3's `Kernel.reads` and R1-5's `ins` have the same shape. This works because every F1 read is a *register named by the word*. The first F2 opcode breaks it:

- `GETTABLE A B C`: `R[A] := R[B][R[C]]` reads the *heap object* whose reference is the value in `R[B]`. The read set depends on a value.
- `SETTABLE` writes such an object.
- `GETUPVAL`/`SETUPVAL` (F3) read or write an *open upvalue*, which aliases a register of another, still active frame **[check lfunc.c: open upvalues point into the stack]**. So `SETUPVAL` writes a register of a different frame, chosen by a value.
- With metatables (F4 in the ledger), `__index` chains make the read set unbounded.

R1-6 #1 noticed the value-dependent `CALL B=0` range, but none of round 1 followed it to tables. The consensus design therefore holds through F1 and H1–H4, then needs redesigning at F2, which is the next fragment after the pilot.

**The idea.** One kernel language with two kinds of read, distinguished by their *index sort*:

```lean
inductive μ
  | rdReg  (r : RegIx)  (k : Value → μ)    -- RegIx: built only from word fields (static)
  | rdObj  (o : Ref)    (k : Obj → μ)      -- Ref: any Ref-valued Value (dynamic)
  | wrReg  (r : RegIx)  (v : Value) (k : μ)
  | wrObj  (o : Ref)    (x : Obj) (k : μ)
  | alloc  (x : Obj)    (k : Ref → μ)
  | go (e : EdgeIx) | stuck
```

`RegIx` is a syntactic expression over `w.a`, `w.b`, `w.c`, `w.bx` and constants, with no `Value` in scope.

- **Register reads** keep the static property. `readsμ w`, `edgesμ w` and the kill sets of idea 1 are folds over the term that never evaluate a `Value`. So `Supported`'s mask analysis is unchanged, and it never needs heap information, because heap cells are always initialised.
- **Heap reads** are *adaptive*. Their footprint is the *log* of a run, not a static set.

**Core rules.**
- **L-B1 becomes two-speed**, one theorem by induction on μ. Take states that agree on pc, out and the static `readsμ w`, and on every heap object in the *dynamic read log* of the run from `s₁`. Then the run from `s₂` has the same log, lands on the same edge, and writes the same registers and objects.
  - This is R1-5 #2's certificate lemma, restricted to the heap sort.
  - The register half is R1-1 #1's `μ.footprint` unchanged, since register indices are static.
- **L-B2** stays definitional: `step?` is the `Option` handler of μ. Allocation is deterministic given the `Host` allocator of idea 2.
- **Frame for heap objects**, proved once: objects outside the write log are unchanged. At A1 this is what lets `GETTABLE`'s arm ignore every other table.

**What it makes free.**
- Everything the static kernels made free in F1 and H1–H4. For register-only opcodes the dynamic log is empty and the theorem degenerates to theirs.
- In F2/F3, without re-proving footprint per table opcode: `GETTABLE`, `GETI`, `GETFIELD`, `SETTABLE`, `SETI`, `SETFIELD`, `SETLIST`, `SELF`, `LEN` on tables, `TFORCALL`, `GETUPVAL`, `SETUPVAL`, `CLOSURE` and `CLOSE`, about 14 opcodes.
- The per-rule cost the census measured is the cost these opcodes would otherwise reintroduce.

**What it costs.**
- A heap in `State` (`Ref → Option Obj`) and a `Ref` sort. F2 needs these anyway.
- The dynamic footprint is weaker for the fragment check: `Supported` can say nothing about heap reads, which is fine because heap cells are never undefined.
- It is weaker still for A1: an arm's heap delta is known only per run. The A1 contract for `GETTABLE` must quantify over the log, as a callee contract for `luaH_get`.
- Readability: a monadic term is less table-like than R1-4's record. The static half can still be *printed* as the old `reads`/`edgesOf` table for review.

**Theories.**
- *Known, databases.* Item locks against predicate locks. When a transaction's read set depends on data, static locking of items misses *phantoms*, and the read set must be recorded as a predicate or at run time (K. Eswaran, J. Gray, R. Lorie, I. Traiger, "The notions of consistency and predicate locks in a database system", CACM 19(11), 1976). Register masks are item locks, and heap reads through references are the phantom case.
- *Known, databases.* Optimistic concurrency control records a *dynamic read set* during execution, then validates that nothing in it changed (H. T. Kung and J. T. Robinson, "On optimistic methods for concurrency control", ACM TODS 6(2), 1981). The two-speed L-B1 is that validation, proved once.
- *Known, build systems.* Monadic tasks have dynamic dependencies, which must be recorded by *verifying traces*. Applicative tasks' dependencies can be read statically (Mokhov, Mitchell, Peyton Jones, "Build Systems à la Carte", ICFP 2018). R1-3 #1 cited the applicative half. F2 needs the monadic half.
- *Novel.* A sort discipline (`RegIx` against `Ref`) that keeps the *dataflow-analysed* resource applicative and makes everything else monadic, so the static fragment check survives a heap.

**Pilot.**
- **H1–H4:** no heap reads in the semantics (strings are values), so this matches the static kernels: 0 proof lines. That parity is the no-regression check.
- **Smallest falsifying build** (one opcode ahead of the suite): write `GETTABLE` on an F2-style finite-map table, first in R1-4's `OpSpec` and then in two-speed μ.
  - The first must fail to type: `rd` cannot name the object.
  - The second must get L-B1 with 0 per-opcode lines.
- **Also:** `SETUPVAL` over an open upvalue that aliases an outer frame's register, to check that the frame half holds when the "heap object" *is* a register of another frame. If aliasing forces register indices to become dynamic, the static half of `Supported` is threatened, and that is the falsifier.

**Seed.** `QVUQV`: query, value, query again. The second query depends on the first value, which is an adaptive, pointer-chasing read.

## 4. Arm phylogeny: content-addressed blocks plus relational diffs along a minimum spanning tree (L-C1)

**Acts on:**
- **(a)** extends R1-5 #4 and R1-6 #4 (orbits under relabelling) to the *partial* orbits the coordinator measured;
- **(c)** objects to R1-3 #2 (templates by anti-unification) and to the exact-orbit premise of `orbit_transport` / `ArmSim.transport`.

**The objection, from the ELF facts.**
- **BAND/BOR/BXOR** and **LTI/LEI/GTI/GEI** are identical on their *opening straight-line code*, not as whole arms. The ALU operation or the comparison sense lives in a later block.
- **ADD vs SUB differ in one branch sense.** gcc inverted a test, and with it the fall-through layout.
  - The least general generalisation of the two instruction sequences therefore has a hole on a *control* instruction, and the block order differs. First-order templates with holes (R1-3 #2) cannot express "same code, flipped branch, swapped fall-through" without splitting the family.
  - `ArmSim.transport` (R1-6 #4) requires the arms to be equal up to an ALU substitution and a relocation. ADD/SUB already falls outside that.
- **MUL/MULK differ by register allocation and scheduling.** They are rr against rk, so they were never one orbit, but they show that scheduling variation is real in this binary.

So orbits exist at the level of *blocks*, and between near-identical *arms*, but rarely between whole arms up to substitution.

**The idea.** Treat the arms as a family of homologous sequences.

- **Unit 1: the basic block, content-addressed.** Normalise each block by relocating pc-relative displacements and give it a key. Blocks with equal keys share *one* block contract, proved once and instantiated at each address by the relocation lemma (R1-6 #4's one ISA theorem). The shared opening code of BAND/BOR/BXOR is then one proof.
- **Unit 2: the diff between near-identical arms.** Build the minimum spanning tree of the arms under an instruction-level edit distance, taken modulo register renaming and the commutation of independent instructions.
  - Prove one arm per tree root in full.
  - Along each tree edge `a₁ → a₂`, prove a *relational* simulation: a product program over the aligned blocks, whose cost is proportional to the unaligned instructions. It transports `ArmSim a₁ r₁` to `ArmSim a₂ r₂`.
  - ADD→SUB is then one flipped branch plus one ALU instruction.

```lean
theorem ArmSim.along (h₁ : ArmSim r₁ a₁) (hΔ : RelSim a₁ a₂ Δ) (hr : Δ.transports r₁ r₂) : ArmSim r₂ a₂
```

`RelSim` is a relational Hoare triple over aligned control-flow graphs. Identical aligned blocks discharge by reflexivity. A flipped branch discharges by one lemma, `beq/bne` with swapped successors, proved once for the ISA.

**Core rules.**
- **L-C1 becomes three rules.**
  1. **Block reuse:** a proof is keyed by content.
  2. **Transport along a relational diff** (`ArmSim.along`), proved once.
  3. **Composition** of block contracts along an arm's control-flow graph.
- **The cost law** replaces "one proof per arm": the total cost is proportional to the sum of the *root* arm sizes plus the *MST weight*.

**What it makes free.**
- The partial orbits that exact-orbit transport cannot use: shared opening blocks, flipped branches and ALU swaps.
- It degrades gracefully. A lone arm (FORLOOP) is its own root, which is the incumbent cost and no worse.

**What it costs.**
- A relational logic over Sail states: product states, and an alignment certificate per tree edge. The alignment is computed and then *checked*; the checking is the proof.
- Setup is roughly the relocation lemma plus the `RelSim` rules for aligned identical blocks, one-instruction substitution, flipped branch, and commuted independent pair.
- It does **nothing** for the three held-out arms. ADD, EQI and FORLOOP are each a root, so the measured held-out cost equals the incumbent's. Only a second family member shows the effect.
- It reduces *how many* arms get a full proof, not the per-arm frame. Stack it on idea 5 or R1-3 #5.

**Theories.**
- *Known, biology.* Homology-based annotation transfer: a function proved (characterised) for one gene is transferred to its homologs by alignment (S. Altschul, W. Gish, W. Miller, E. Myers, D. Lipman, "Basic local alignment search tool", J. Mol. Biol. 215, 1990).
- *Known, phylogenetics.* Parsimony: the minimum total change over a tree explains a family of sequences (W. Fitch, "Toward defining the course of evolution: minimum change for a specific tree topology", Systematic Zoology 20, 1971 **[check]**). Here the "change" is proof effort, and the tree is chosen to minimise it (Kruskal's MST, Proc. AMS 1956).
- *Known, verification.* Regression verification proves two *similar* programs equivalent at a cost proportional to their difference (B. Godlin and O. Strichman, "Regression verification", DAC 2009 **[check]**). Product programs: Barthe, Crespo, Kunz, FM 2011.
- *Known, systems.* Content-addressed storage: equal content, one object (R. Merkle, CRYPTO 1987 **[check]**). The same move is applied here to block proofs.
- *Novel.* Pricing an arm-simulation campaign as root size plus MST weight over *proof-relevant* edit distance, and choosing proof order by that tree.

**Pilot.**
- **Falsifier first (no Lean, an afternoon).** Extract the F1 arms from the ELF between the dispatch-table targets, split them into blocks, normalise displacements, and report:
  - (a) the number of distinct block keys over the total number of blocks;
  - (b) the MST weight over the total instruction count, under edit distance modulo renaming.

  **Abandon if (b) > 0.4.** Then relational diffs cost about as much as direct proofs.
- **Lean:** prove ADD directly, *which is the held-out arm*. Then prove SUB, BAND→BOR→BXOR, and LTI→LEI→GTI→GEI via `ArmSim.along`. Report their marginal lines separately from the held-out figure.
- **H cases** have no machine side in the suite. `LT` with strings adds a `lessthanothers → l_strcmp` tail to the existing `LT` arm, which is a small tree edge from `LT`'s own integer path.

**Seed.** `MIV…MIV`: the same trigram with different continuations, a shared prefix and a divergent tail. `B8` twice, each near a `q`: homologous copies in different contexts.

## 5. Rely-lenses: the A1 frame when GC and allocation touch the complement (and the view)

**Acts on:**
- **(c)** objects to R1-3 #5 (a lens with a *constant* complement) and to R1-5 #5 (`τ` as a separation-algebra morphism) on H2 and later fragments;
- **(a)** extends R1-1 #5's note that "GC is the hard part" into a single proved rule.

**The objection.** `OP_CONCAT` is `ProtectNT(luaV_concat(L, n)); checkGC(L, L->top)` **[check lvm.c 5.4.7]**. So the arm allocates, through `luaS_newlstr` or `luaS_createlngstrobj`, and then may run an incremental GC step. Three consequences:

1. **The complement is not constant.** Allocation changes `allgc`, the string table and `GCdebt`. R1-3 #5 conceded that with a "churn relation ≈", but gave it no rule.
2. **GC writes into the view itself.** A GC step sets the `marked` colour bits in the *headers of live objects*, including the strings that registers refer to **[check lgc.c]**. So `Config ≅ view × Rest` fails: the same bytes are in both. R1-5 #5's `τ` is not a morphism into disjoint cells for the same reason: headers are shared with the collector, not only with other registers.
3. **Interning makes "fresh" false.** `luaS_newlstr` on a short string may return an *existing* object, even one that is dead but not yet swept, which it resurrects **[check lstring.c `internshrstr`: `if (isdead(g, ts)) changewhite(ts)`]**. An allocation contract that says "the result is a new object" is wrong for exactly the H2 path that yields short strings.

**The idea.** The representation reads only **gauge-invariant** data:
- tags and payloads of register slots;
- the *contents and length* of reachable strings (later: of tables);
- the reachability graph from the roots, up to a bijection of addresses.

It never reads mark bits, list links, debt, free memory, or addresses themselves, except through the bijection. Collection and allocation become an *environment* described by one rely relation:

```
R_gc c c' :≡ reach(c') ≅ reach(c) via a bijection that is the identity on decoded content,
             no reachable object's content changes,
             and the short-string table still maps each content to the unique reachable object with it
```

Every call that the rely covers (`luaC_step`, `luaS_newlstr`'s table maintenance, `luaE_setdebt`) is treated as an `R_gc`-step. The allocator's contract is extended by "the returned object has these contents and is reachable now". That deliberately does *not* say fresh, which is what covers resurrection.

**Core rules.**
- **Stability, proved once:** `Rep c s → R_gc c c' → Rep c' s`. This is the rely-guarantee stability obligation.
- **L-C1 becomes `arm_sim_rely`**, proved once. An arm simulates rule `r` when:
  - its effect, outside calls covered by `R_gc`, is the delta of `r`'s edge on the register window, with kills per idea 1;
  - its covered calls satisfy their guarantees.
- **One guarantee per collector entry point.** `luaC_step` satisfies `R_gc`: a single, large, one-time proof about the collector, and never per arm.
  - It can be staged. First prove it for the *stopped* collector (`gcstp`/`gcstopem` set, where `luaC_step` only resets debt **[check lgc.c 5.4.7]**) and run F4 tests with GC stopped. Then prove it for the incremental collector.

**What it makes free.**
- The frame half of every allocating arm: `CONCAT` now, and `NEWTABLE`, `CLOSURE`, `SETLIST` and every `checkGC` site in F2/F3.
- The heap's churn is never mentioned per arm.
- The `EQ`-on-strings path (`eqshrstr`, pointer equality). It becomes sound *from the interning clause of `R_gc`*, not per arm: equal contents imply the same reachable object.
- Non-allocating arms (the three held-out ones) are unaffected: their rely steps are empty.

**What it costs.**
- The collector guarantee is the big item. Measure it separately. The staging above lets F4 proceed on the stopped collector without it.
- **Falsifying risk for F2.** Once tables are mutable, the *mutator* must maintain the incremental collector's tri-colour invariant through write barriers (`luaC_barrierback` in `SETTABLE`) **[check]**. Then `Rep` must include colour invariants, which are not gauge-invariant, and every heap-writing arm owes a barrier proof. Per-arm cost comes back. Strings are immutable, so H1–H4 are unaffected, but F2 is where this idea is tested.
- The bijection on reachable addresses must be carried in `Rep`, which is an existential over bijections. That is standard for GC proofs, but not free.

**Theories.**
- *Known, concurrency.* Rely-guarantee (C. B. Jones, "Tentative steps toward a development method for interfering programs", TOPLAS 5(4), 1983). Stability of assertions under the rely, as used in RGSep (V. Vafeiadis and M. Parkinson, CONCUR 2007). The collector is the interfering thread, and allocation sites are its only scheduling points.
- *Known, physics.* Gauge invariance: observables are functions invariant under gauge transformations, and physics is formulated on the quotient (standard; e.g. H. Weyl, 1929 **[check]**). Addresses, colours and list links are gauge, and `Rep` may observe only the invariants.
- *Known, databases.* Snapshot visibility under MVCC is unaffected by background vacuuming of dead versions. Readers see the same snapshot while garbage is reclaimed concurrently (H. Berenson et al., "A critique of ANSI SQL isolation levels", SIGMOD 1995, for snapshot isolation **[check]**).
- *Known, GC verification* (not distant; context). McCreight, Shao, Lin, Li, "A general framework for certifying garbage collectors and their mutators", PLDI 2007 **[check]**: an abstract mutator–collector interface.
- *Novel.* Putting string *interning* into the rely as a gauge-invariant clause, so pointer equality of short strings is justified once, and the resurrection path needs no special case.

**Pilot.**
- **A1 held-out arms (ADD, EQI, FORLOOP):** state `Rep` with the rely and prove them. Expected effect: none. The measured statistic is *no regression* against the incumbent A1 pilot's lines.
- **Falsifier (ELF, then Lean on paper).**
  1. Confirm that `OP_CONCAT` reaches `luaC_step` in the build, and that the step writes `marked` of live objects.
  2. Write `R_gc` and prove stability for a `Rep` covering the register window plus reachable short strings.
  3. If stability needs a case per object *kind* that grows with F2/F3, the "once" is per kind. That is acceptable.
  4. If it needs colour invariants, that is not acceptable, and the idea is falsified for F2.
- **H2 on paper:** the `CONCAT` arm's obligation is "slot A decodes to the concatenation, `L->top` restored, everything else an `R_gc`-step or a tombstone" (idea 1). Count the clauses against a constant-complement lens.

**Seed.** `RLR`: rely, lens, rely, a round trip that returns to where it started. `Mm`: one object under two labels (two addresses, one content), the gauge.
