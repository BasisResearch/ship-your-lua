## 0. Answers to the two hard questions

**Data `Step`, or a readable inductive plus one equivalence proof?**
For F1 through the F4 string slice, keep both. The equivalence costs little
when the data form is a kernel table (R1-3 #1, R1-4 #1, R1-5 #1, R1-6 #1),
because each old constructor unfolds to one `⟨w, rfl, …, rfl⟩` witness. I
estimate 1–2 lines per rule, about 50 lines in all. That is cheap insurance
for the TCB review, and it gets counted in R1 anyway.

The real question comes at F2/F3, and I did not see any round-1 answer price
it. Three things arrive there:

- `next` order;
- `tostring` of tables and closures (`table: 0x…`, an allocator address);
- allocation.

All of these are choices that a *function*-shaped kernel or rulebook cannot
make. Every round-1 proposal of the form "the semantics is the graph of a
function" then has to pick one of two options:

- **(i)** grow a `choose` node and lose determinism exactly where Layer B's
  `compile_refinement_of_tv` needs it (see Idea 1);
- **(ii)** move the choice into a parameter, as `Host.showBuiltin` already
  does.

I argue for (ii). With (ii), the data form stays a function through F3, and
the TCB question reduces to readability, which the derived constructor lemmas
answer.

**Which idea reduces A1, and what is the cheapest way to falsify it?**
A1's cost has two halves:

- **the frame:** everything the arm does not touch;
- **the delta:** the slots it writes and its target.

For the frame, the lens and low-equivalence ideas (R1-3 #5, R1-5 #5,
R1-6 #5) remove it. But as stated they break on stack relocation (Idea 5),
and that hazard needs `CALL` or `CONCAT`, which the pilot arms `ADD`, `EQI`
and `FORLOOP` never exercise.

For the delta there are two options:

- **Lifters** (R1-1 #5, R1-2 #5, R1-4 #4) are the only ideas that bring the
  per-arm cost near zero, but their setup costs about as much as ten
  hand-proved arms.
- **Syntactic orbits** (R1-5 #4, R1-6 #4) are cheap, but the coordinator's
  objdump check already shows they are partial.

Idea 3 sits between the two: orbits taken modulo a Lua-agnostic canonical
form. It can be falsified in an afternoon with a script over the ELF, before
any Lean is written.

## 1. Oracle-determinised semantics: every future choice is a `Host` parameter

**Acts on:**
- **R1-3 #3** (free call monad / `Graph`): (c), objection.
- **R1-4 #2** (construct trees): (c), objection.
- **R1-4 #1** (RCW), **R1-5 #1** (port-typed kernels), **R1-6 #1** (avatar
  microcode): (a), extension.

**Objection.** R1-3 #3 names its own weakness: a rulebook "can't express
non-determinism later (unspecified evaluation order, `next` order in F2)",
and it offers a `choose` node as the fix, with `Graph.det` "lost only for
arms that use it". The weakness is larger than that, for three reasons:

1. **The loss is not local.** `Graph.det` is used by
   `compile_refinement_of_tv` (in `Lua/Theorems.lean`, Layer B is stated as
   "per-program translation validation plus determinism"). If the source
   semantics is nondeterministic, the *backward* TV direction must hold for
   every resolution of the choice. A `pairs` loop anywhere in a program then
   makes every proof about that program possibilistic. The nondeterminism is
   not confined to the arm that uses `choose`.
2. **A `choose` breaks L-B2 as stated.** `Step ↔ step? = some` cannot hold
   when `Step` has two successors. Every functional-kernel proposal (R1-4 #1,
   R1-5 #1, R1-6 #1) then has to switch to `step? : State → List State`, and
   its L-B1 has to become a *possibilistic* footprint. Possibilistic
   noninterference is not closed under refinement: this is the refinement
   paradox (McLean; see Theories). So the definite-initialisation argument
   would need its hardest form.
3. **The machine is deterministic anyway.** On bare metal with a
   deterministic `dlmalloc`, the binary's `next` order and `table: 0x…`
   addresses are *functions* of the program and its input. The
   nondeterminism would be a fiction of the spec.

**Idea.** Every place where Lua is underspecified but the binary is
deterministic becomes a field of `Host`, in the same way `showBuiltin`
already is:

```lean
structure Host where
  showBuiltin : Builtin → String
  -- F2/F3 additions: pure functions of an abstract, layer-independent history
  addrOf      : AllocHist → ObjKind → Nat          -- the allocator, as the binary does it
  nextKey     : TableHist → Option Value → Option Value   -- `next` order, from a table's own history
```

`AllocHist` and `TableHist` are *abstract* histories: the sequence of
allocation requests, and a table's insert/remove history. They are defined
identically in both semantics, so the source and the bytecode consult the
same oracle on the same history. The rulebook (R1-3 #3, R1-4 #2) and the
kernels (R1-4 #1 et al.) stay functions, and `Graph.det`, L-B2 and L-A1
keep their F1 statements.

Layer A instantiates `Host` with `binaryHost`, where the addresses come from
the image. Its proof obligation is a new per-*primitive* law that sits
beside L-C1:

- **L-O1:** the machine's allocator and `luaH_next` realise
  `binaryHost.addrOf` and `binaryHost.nextKey` on the abstract histories.

**Core rules.**

- **L-A1 and L-B2 are unchanged, and stay proved once.** They are
  parametric in `H`.
- **A new law, L-O1, is proved once per oracle field**, not per construct or
  per rule. It needs two things:
  - `dlmalloc` determinism, which the Iris route presumably already has
    (the `dlmalloc` port is in the recent commits);
  - one contract for `luaH_next`.
- **Layer B picks up an oracle-agreement lemma:** `compile` preserves
  `AllocHist` up to a known morphism. **This is where the idea could fail.**
  `luac`'s bytecode allocates exactly what the source allocates, *only if*
  the source semantics counts the same allocations, for example constant
  strings interned at load time. See the pilot.

**What it makes free.**

- **Determinism for F2/F3 at no extra cost.** `Graph.det` / `holds_det` /
  `Derives.functional` are not reopened.
- **TV stays forward-only for deterministic programs.** Without the oracle,
  every `pairs` program needs backward simulation.
- **The trusted-base question gets simpler.** Redefining `Step` as data costs
  nothing extra at F2/F3, because the data stays a function.

**What it costs.**

- **Choosing the histories.** `AllocHist` must be abstract enough that the
  source and the bytecode generate the same one, yet concrete enough that
  `dlmalloc`'s real addresses are a function of it. The GC frees objects,
  and `free` changes future addresses. The history must therefore include
  collection events, and GC timing depends on `GCdebt`, a byte count. That
  pulls allocation sizes into the history. This is heavy, but it is still a
  function.
- **Programs that print addresses** become trusted only under
  `binaryHost`. That is already the case for `print(print)`.

**Theories.**

- *Known (concurrency):* oracle semantics. Hobor, Appel and Zappa Nardelli,
  "Oracle semantics for concurrent separation logic", ESOP 2008. The
  scheduler's choices are an oracle argument, so each thread's semantics is
  a sequential function.
- *Known (databases):* deterministic database systems. Thomson, Diamond,
  Weng, Ren, Shao and Abadi, "Calvin: fast distributed transactions for
  partitioned database systems", SIGMOD 2012. Nondeterminism is removed by
  agreeing the order *before* execution, so replicas are functions of the
  input log. Here `AllocHist` plays the input log, and the source and the
  bytecode are the two replicas.
- *Known (game theory / economics):* Harsanyi purification. Harsanyi, "Games
  with randomly disturbed payoffs: a new rationale for mixed-strategy
  equilibrium points", International Journal of Game Theory 2, 1973
  **[check year and volume]**. A mixed (nondeterministic) strategy is a pure
  strategy of a player with a private type drawn from a parameter. That is
  exactly moving the choice into `Host`.
- *Known (security):* the refinement paradox for possibilistic
  noninterference. J. McLean, "A general theory of composition for trace
  sets closed under selective interleaving functions", IEEE S&P 1994
  **[check the paper]**. This is why option (i) is costly.
- *Known (verification):* prophecy variables. Abadi and Lamport, "The
  existence of refinement mappings", TCS 82, 1991. The oracle is a prophecy
  fixed up front.

**Pilot.**

- **Strings (H1–H4) need no oracle.** Strings are values, not addresses, in
  both semantics. So the held-out suite does *not* measure this idea; say so.
- **The cheapest falsifier is a paper exercise plus one script.** Write out
  `AllocHist` for `local t = {} ; t.x = 1 ; for k in pairs(t) do print(k)
  end` in the source semantics and in the bytecode semantics, and check that
  they are equal. This includes `luac`'s constant table: does
  `Const.str "x"` allocate at load time or at run time? If the two histories
  cannot be made equal *by definition*, (ii) collapses into per-program
  oracle agreement, and the idea fails.
- **Refactor targets:** none change. The idea's value is that R2's
  compression survives F2.

**Seed.** *prognostication*: the choice is told in advance. *oligarchist*: a
few host parameters rule every future choice.

## 2. Poisoned writes in the kernel: CONCAT's range write and CALL's clobbering, deterministically

**Acts on:**
- **R1-5 #1**, **R1-6 #1** and **R1-6 #2**: (c), objection.
- Combines **R1-4 #1** (RCW) with **R1-1 #3** / **R1-5 #2** (X-values,
  poisoned stores): (b).

**Objection (concrete, from H2).** Three round-1 designs give `CONCAT` a
write set of `{A}`:

- R1-5 #1: `outs = [(pc+1, [A])]`;
- R1-6 #1: "a leaf writing `A ↦ concatVals`";
- R1-6 #2: `fp = (range A B, [A])`.

`SUITE.md` H2 now records, checked against `lvm.c`, that the write set is
the whole range `R[A..A+B-1]`. With a write set of `{A}`, L-B1's clause
"every register outside `wr` is unchanged" is **false**, and the generic
footprint theorem would prove a false statement about the machine the day
A1 reaches `CONCAT`. Note that the bytecode semantics itself would still be
self-consistent, so the random-state law check of `ROUND-1.md` would *not*
catch this. Only the machine can.

The designs that do get the range right (R1-3 #1, R1-4 #1) face a second
problem. Their kernel must return a `Vector Value` of length `B`: *which
values* go in `R[A+1..A+B-1]`? Faithfully, these are the residue of
`luaV_concat`'s pass structure. Each pass concatenates as many adjacent
strings as it can, and `tostring` rewrites number slots in place
(`lvm.c:649-650`, `679-682` per `SUITE.md`). Transcribing that is possible,
but it is spec text about garbage, and it is fragile to the grouping
heuristic.

The same issue already exists for `CALL`. `Fragment.lean` says a call
"clobbers every register above its results", while `Step.callPrint` writes
`nil` to its `C-1` results and nothing above them. Today the gap between
the spec and the machine is closed by the definite-initialisation analysis
together with a per-arm argument at A1.

**Idea.** Every kernel write gains one more possible value, `✗` (poison).
Registers are `Nat → Value✗`, where `Value✗ := Option Value` in the style of
R1-1 #3, and the kernel's output type becomes `Vector (Option Value) n`. A
`none` in the output means "this slot is garbage afterwards":

- `CONCAT`: `rd = range A B`, one edge writing `range A B`, with values
  `some (concat vs) :: replicate (B-1) none`;
- `CALL print`: one edge writing `range A (maxstack - A)`, with `nil` in the
  `C-1` result slots and `none` above them. The write set is static in
  `(p, w)`, because `maxstack` is a field of `p`.

Reads are strict: the single generic operand fetch
`(rd.mapM s.regs) : Option (List Value)` fails on `✗`, and a failed fetch
means stuck. This happens *before* any kernel body runs, so strictness is
not a per-rule premise. It is a property of the one fetch function. That
closes the gap R1-1 #3 admitted, where `mono` was "one tactic across
constructors" and fails on negative premises. With a strict generic fetch,
no rule can inspect `✗`.

**Core rules.**

- **L-B1 stays one theorem, `rcw_footprint`, unchanged.** A poisoned write is
  a write, so the frame clause is honest for `CONCAT` and `CALL`.
- **L-B2 stays `Iff.rfl`.** The kernel is still a function: `✗` is a value
  of the output type, not a choice.
- **Definite initialisation becomes the monotonicity theorem of R1-1 #3,
  proved structurally.** It is not "one tactic over constructors" but an
  induction on the one generic fetch. The analysis's transfer function reads
  `✗`-writes from the kernel's type, so the "kill" half of `sweep` (today
  `keepMask` for `CALL`) is derived, not tabled.
- **A1 gets a representation relation that is total on garbage:**
  `Rep m s :⇔ ∀ i, s.regs i = some v → slot m i encodes v`. This is R1-6 #5's
  low-equivalence, with the Low set now *in the state* rather than in the
  analysis. The `CONCAT` arm's proof never mentions `luaV_concat`'s passes,
  and the `CALL` arm's never mentions what `luaB_print` pushed.

**What it makes free.**

- **The H2 faithfulness debate:** the spec says ✗ for the upper range.
- **The per-arm "stale slots" clauses** at A1 for `CALL` and `CONCAT`.
- **The separate `keepMask` special case** in `Fragment.lean`'s sweep.

**What it costs.**

- **`Value✗` threads through `State`.** `BcSem`'s initial state is all-`✗`
  above the parameters, not all-`nil`. That is *more* faithful (R1-6 #5's
  "ghosts"), but `bcSemFrom_iff`-style statements must be restated. This is
  the R1 refactor.
- **`EQ` must be strict.** Today `decide (s.regs w.a = s.regs w.b)` is total,
  so comparing a garbage register would "work" in the spec. Under the
  generic fetch it is stuck. That is a behaviour change only on programs
  outside `Supported`.
- **A later fragment that deliberately reads a stale slot** (a `VARARG`
  edge case) would need an explicit declassification. R1-6 #5 names the
  same cost.

**Theories.**

- *Known (statistics):* missing-data theory and multiple imputation. D. B.
  Rubin, "Inference and missing data", Biometrika 63(3), 1976. An inference
  is valid under a missingness mechanism when it is invariant across
  imputations. `supported_certain` (R1-1 #3) is the "invariant across all
  imputations of ✗" statement, and making ✗ *explicit in the data* is
  Rubin's move from ignoring missingness to modelling it.
- *Known (compilers):* LLVM `poison` versus `undef`. Lee, Kim, Song, Hur,
  Das, Majnemer, Regehr and Lopes, "Taming undefined behavior in LLVM",
  PLDI 2017. Their key point is that `undef` is a *nondeterministic* value,
  which breaks functional reasoning, whereas `poison` is a *deterministic*
  value that is strict on observation. That is exactly the choice made here
  to keep L-B2 a function.
- *Known (domain theory):* flat lifted domains and strict functions. Scott,
  "Outline of a mathematical theory of computation", Oxford PRG-2, 1970.
  Strictness of the one fetch gives monotonicity for every kernel.

**Pilot.**

- **H2:** the `CONCAT` kernel entry with the ✗-tail. Measure that
  `rcw_footprint` applies unchanged (0 lines), and that the derived edge
  write set equals `rmask A B`. Compare with R1-5 #1's `{A}` version: the
  check is to *state* L-C1 for `CONCAT` on paper and watch it fail.
- **H1, H3, H4:** unaffected. There is no poisoned write.
- **R1:** the `callPrint` rule with a ✗-tail. The `bcSemFrom_iff` proof
  becomes `supported_certain`.
- **Smallest falsifier:** port `CALL print` only, and check whether the
  existing kernel validations (`print_print`) still reduce by `decide` with
  `Option Value` registers. If kernel evaluation slows past the budget,
  drop the idea and keep `nil` plus the analysis.

**Seed.** *besour*: to sour, to poison a cell. *wormhood*: the larval
garbage a slot holds between two lives. *unswell*: the range shrinks back to
one live register.

## 3. Orbits modulo a Lua-agnostic canonical form (dataflow DAGs up to renaming, scheduling and branch sense)

**Acts on:**
- **R1-5 #4** and **R1-6 #4** (syntactic orbits): (a), extended with the
  coordinator's objdump facts.
- **R1-3 #2** (anti-unified templates): (c), objection.
- Positioned against **R1-1 #5**, **R1-2 #5** and **R1-4 #4** (lifters).

**Where round 1 stands after the objdump check.**

- **Some arms are already in one orbit.** BAND/BOR/BXOR, LTI/LEI/GTI/GEI,
  ADDK/SUBK and LT/LE are instruction-identical on their opening straight
  line (apart from the ALU operation, as I read the note). R1-6 #4's
  transport works for them unchanged.
- **ADD/SUB differ in one branch sense.** R1-6 #4's `Relabel` (a shift plus
  instruction substitution) cannot express a flipped `bne`/`beq` with
  swapped successors.
- **MUL/MULK differ by register allocation and scheduling.** R1-6 #4 says
  "then transport fails for that arm". R1-3 #2's anti-unification gives a
  template with more holes, or splits the family. The syntactic methods
  degrade exactly where gcc's `-O2` has freedom.

The lifters are robust to all of this, but they bake Lua's data layout into
the lifter itself (`rd i` recognised from `base + 16·i`). They also need a
`Rep`, a normaliser and a Foata form: 800–1500 lines by R1-1 #5's own
estimate. That puts the break-even point at roughly ten hand arms.

**Idea.** Separate the two jobs the lifters conflate:

1. **Canonicalise, Lua-agnostically.** Define one function
   `canon : List Instr → Option Dag`. It symbolically executes a
   straight-line arm (or a small acyclic arm CFG) over RV64 *without* any
   Lua knowledge, building a dataflow DAG:
   - nodes are ALU operations and loads;
   - stores are keyed by symbolic address terms;
   - branches become `ite` nodes with *normalised sense*: `bne a b T F`
     becomes `ite (eq a b) F T`, which absorbs the ADD/SUB difference;
   - register names disappear, which absorbs register allocation;
   - instruction order disappears, except where memory dependence forces
     it, which absorbs scheduling. Stores with symbolic addresses are
     ordered only when they may alias. Within one arm, the addresses are
     `base + const`, so aliasing is decidable by comparing offsets.

   Prove one theorem, once, against Sail:
   `canon c₁ = canon c₂ → HeadToHead c₁ ≈ HeadToHead c₂`. Here `≈` is equal
   effects on memory and on the registers live at the dispatch head. This
   is value numbering with a soundness proof, not a decompiler into Lua
   terms.
2. **Orbit transport on DAGs.** Two arms are in one orbit iff
   `canon arm₁ = (canon arm₂)[op₂ ↦ op₁]`, a DAG equality after
   substituting one ALU node. R1-6 #4's `ArmSim.transport` then needs only
   the node-level lemma `Implements op f` (as R1-6 #4 already has) and the
   `canon` soundness theorem. Relocation invariance disappears, because a
   DAG has no addresses of its own.

**The design choice that matters.** `canon` knows nothing about `TValue`,
`base`, Lua or the kernel table. So it is small, it can be tested on
*any* RV64 code, and its proof is independent of A0.6's decoders. The
Lua-specific part is paid once per orbit *representative*: one full
`ArmSim`, written in the incumbent style or with Idea 5's relocatable view.

**Core rules.**

- **L-C1 becomes `ArmSim rep` for each representative, plus
  `orbit_transport`:**

  ```lean
  theorem orbit_transport (hrep : ArmSim r₁ arm₁) (hc : canon arm₂ = (canon arm₁).subst op₁ op₂)
      (hi : Implements op₂ f₂) (hr : r₂ = r₁.withOp f₂) : ArmSim r₂ arm₂
  ```

- **One soundness theorem for `canon`**, proved by induction on the
  instruction list against the Sail step, over the RV64 subset the arms use.

**What it makes free.**

- **Arms that differ only in schedule, register choice or branch sense.**
  On the coordinator's evidence, that should cover MUL/MULK and ADD/SUB,
  which the syntactic methods miss.
- **The number of hand-proved arms becomes the number of orbits modulo
  `canon`.** My forecast is about 15–20 for 54 opcodes, against R1-6 #4's
  guess of 15–20 *syntactic* orbits, which the objdump check has already
  undercut.

**What it costs.**

- **`canon` and its soundness.** It is smaller than a lifter: it has no
  `Rep`, no Lua layout and no Foata normaliser, because a DAG is already a
  canonical form for commuting independent operations. I estimate 300–500
  lines. That is roughly a third of the lifter's cost, and it buys a third
  of the lifter's benefit, since representatives are still hand-proved.
- **DAG equality must be decided in the kernel.** Structural hash-consing
  keeps it linear. `native_decide` is excluded.
- **Where it breaks:**
  - arms that call helpers (`luaV_idiv`, `luaV_mod`, `luaV_concat`,
    `luaV_objlen`): the call is an opaque node, and the callee's contract is
    separate;
  - arms with loops (none among straight-line opcodes; the callees have
    them);
  - arms where gcc cross-jumps into a shared tail: the DAG must be taken up
    to the shared tail's entry, and the tail proved once.

**Theories.**

- *Known (chemistry):* canonical labelling of molecular graphs. H. L.
  Morgan, "The generation of a unique machine description for chemical
  structures", J. Chem. Doc. 5(2), 1965. Two molecules drawn with different
  atom numberings are the same compound iff their canonical forms are
  equal. Here register allocation plays the atom numbering and the arm plays
  the molecule.
- *Known (compilers):* global value numbering. Alpern, Wegman and Zadeck,
  "Detecting equality of variables in programs", POPL 1988. Translation
  validation by symbolic evaluation to a normal form: Necula, "Translation
  validation for an optimizing compiler", PLDI 2000.
- *Known (concurrency):* Mazurkiewicz traces (as in R1-1 #5). The dataflow
  DAG *is* the dependence graph of the trace, so the Foata step R1-1 #5
  proposes is unnecessary.
- *Novel:* splitting the lifter into a Lua-agnostic canonicaliser (proved
  once, reusable for any C-compiled interpreter) and per-orbit Lua proofs.
  The saving is priced by the number of orbits modulo `canon`.

**Pilot.**

- **Cheapest falsifier, no Lean:** a Python `canon` over `objdump` of the
  54 arms, with the same normalisations. Report the orbit count.
  - If MUL/MULK and ADD/SUB do **not** merge, the idea is dead and the
    lifters are the only robust option.
  - If the count is above about 30, the idea is dead on cost.
- **A1 pilot arms:** `ADD`, `EQI` and `FORLOOP` are each a representative,
  so the held-out A1 measurement shows only setup. Add `SUB` and `MULK` as
  the "second petals", because they are exactly the members the syntactic
  methods lose, and report their marginal cost. The target is under 10
  lines each.
- **H arms:** `LEN` and `CONCAT` are singletons with callee nodes. They are
  not helped. Report this honestly.

**Seed.** *gyrocompass*: a heading independent of how the hull is turned,
that is, of register allocation. *unruddered*: the branch sense does not
steer the meaning. *ectomorphic*: strip the arm to its dataflow skeleton.

## 4. A three-valued δ: `ok | meta ev | err`, because `none` means different things in each layer

**Acts on:**
- **R1-2 #4** (one δ), **R1-4 #5** (sort-erased core) and **R1-1 #4**
  (`Prim × Addr × Cont`): (c), objection, then (a), extension.

**Objection.** All three make an operator's failure `none` ("no rule, a
runtime error"), and they share δ verbatim between the layers. That is
right for F1, but it is wrong in the bytecode from F2 onwards, and it is
already visible in the machine at A1:

- **Bytecode:** in `lvm.c` 5.4, `OP_ADD`'s fast path does `pc++` to skip
  the following `MMBIN`. When the operands are not both numbers it does
  *nothing* and falls through to `OP_MMBIN`, which calls `luaT_trybinTM`
  **[check the `op_arith` macro]**. So a non-number operand is not "no
  edge". It is the *other* edge, `pc+1`.
  - In F1 that edge leads to an error, because there is no metatable.
  - In F2, with metatables, it leads to a call of Lua code.
  - `MMBIN` reads the *previous* instruction's `RA`, which is another
    cross-instruction dependency.
- **Source:** the same failure is "call the `__add` metamethod", a
  re-entrant call into the interpreter.
- **Machine at A1, today:** the `ADD` arm has two exits, the fast path to
  the dispatch head at `pc+2`, and the fall-through to `MMBIN` at `pc+1`.
  F1's `Step` makes the non-integer case stuck, so `stuck_sim` must show
  the binary does not exit 0, which goes *through the `MMBIN` arm*. R1-1 #4
  gives `Cont` a single `store+skipMMBIN`. R1-2 #4 gives `strictSet` a
  single `pc + skip o`. Both hide this second exit, so the A1 pilot arm
  `ADD` will meet it unmodelled.

**Idea.** δ returns a three-valued result, shared verbatim by both layers:

```lean
inductive Outcome | ok (v : Value) | meta (ev : TMEvent) (args : List Value) | err
def δ : Prim → List Value → Outcome
```

- `meta ev` names the tag-method event (`TM_ADD`, `TM_LT`, `TM_CONCAT`,
  `TM_LEN`).
- `err` is a genuine error, for example integer division by zero, where
  `luaV_idiv` raises directly.

Each layer interprets `meta` through its **own `Cont`**:

- in the bytecode, `meta` selects the `pc+1` edge (towards `MMBIN*`), or,
  for `LT`/`LE`, `Protect(luaT_callorderTM)` inside the arm;
- in the source, `meta` becomes a `call` node in the rulebook (R1-3 #3 or
  R1-4 #2).

In F1–F4, with no metatables, both interpretations make `meta` stuck, so
nothing changes in the relations yet. What changes is that the *reason* is
carried along.

**Core rules.**

- **L-B1/L-B2 per `Cont`, as in R1-1 #4, with three continuations per
  shape** (`ok`, `meta`, `err`) instead of one. That is still
  `|Addr| + |Cont|` cases, not per operator.
- **Layer-B operator agreement (R1-4 #5's L-X1) is stated on `Outcome`.**
  Source `a + b` and bytecode `ADD; MMBIN` agree on `ok` by `rfl`. They agree
  on `meta` by one lemma per *addressing shape*: `MMBIN`/`MMBINK`/`MMBINI`
  reconstruct the operands, and `MMBINK`/`MMBINI` pass a `flip` bit. That is
  3 lemmas, not one per operator.
- **At A1, `stuck_sim` gets a rule.** Every `err` and every
  `meta`-without-metatable leads the machine to `luaG_*error` →
  `luaD_throw` → a non-zero exit. This is proved once per *error entry
  point* (a handful), and the δ result names the entry point.

**What it makes free.**

- **The F2 metamethod extension of every operator:** 0 lines per operator,
  and one `Cont.meta` interpretation per layer.
- **The `ADD` arm's second exit at A1** is a named edge with a generic
  proof, not a surprise.
- **H2/H3/H4:** `concat`, `len` and string order are `δ` cases, as in round
  1, but `δ .lt [.str a, .int b] = meta TM_LT …` *records* the case that
  `luaG_ordererror` handles today. In F1–F4 this is still stuck.

**What it costs.**

- **More constructors in the three δ-based designs.** The `Outcome` type
  and one more `Cont` branch per addressing shape add about 40 lines.
- **Getting the `meta`/`err` split right needs checks against `lvm.c`.**
  Which failures raise directly (`luaV_idiv` by zero, `luaG_forerror` in
  `FORPREP`) and which go through tag methods (**[check]**
  `luaV_concat` → `luaT_tryconcatTM`)? The split is per primitive, but it is
  faithfulness-critical.

**Theories.**

- *Known (economics / contract theory):* incomplete contracts and residual
  control rights. S. Grossman and O. Hart, "The costs and benefits of
  ownership: a theory of vertical and lateral integration", J. Political
  Economy 94(4), 1986. A contract (δ) fixes outcomes where it can. In the
  gap (`meta`) the holder of residual rights decides: the metatable in the
  source, and the `MMBIN` instruction in the bytecode. Sharing the contract
  across two firms works only if the gap is *named* in the contract and not
  left as silence (`none`).
- *Known (PL):* handlers of algebraic effects. Plotkin and Pretnar,
  "Handlers of algebraic effects", ESOP 2009. `meta ev` is an operation, and
  each layer is a different handler for it. L-X1 on `ok` is handler
  independence.
- *Known (Lua):* Ierusalimschy, de Figueiredo and Celes, "The implementation
  of Lua 5.0", J. Universal Computer Science 11(7), 2005, for the
  register-VM design. The 5.4 `MMBIN` split is newer than that paper
  **[check the 5.4 change notes]**.

**Pilot.**

- **H1–H4:** implement δ with `Outcome`, and measure against the two-valued
  δ. The prediction is a small constant overhead (the extra constructor),
  and 0 metatheory lines per case in both.
- **The falsifier is on the A1 `ADD` arm.** Count the `ADD` arm's exits in
  the ELF. If gcc has *not* compiled a separate fall-through to `MMBIN`
  (for example, if it merged the non-number path into a shared tail that
  raises directly under F1's build flags), the `meta` edge is not a machine
  fact and the objection is moot for A1. It still stands for F2.

**Seed.** *uncinatum*: a hook, which here is a tag method. *facioplegia*:
one side of the face does not move, so the fast path fails and the other
side takes over. *contraflow*: traffic sent the other way, to `MMBIN`.

## 5. A relocatable view: registers named relative to the stack, because GC and `CALL` can move the whole window

**Acts on:**
- **R1-3 #5** (lens with constant complement): (c), objection.
- **R1-5 #5** (tethered separation, `R[i] ↦ v` at `base + 16·i`): (c),
  objection.
- **R1-1 #5** / **R1-4 #4** (lifters that recognise `base + 16·i`): (c),
  objection.
- **R1-6 #5** (low-equivalence): (a), extension.

**Objection.** Each of these fixes the machine location of `R[i]` as
`base + 16·i`, with `base` in a machine register at the dispatch head.

- **R1-3 #5 is closest to the problem.** Its list of hard parts says
  `luaV_execute` caches `base`, `pc` and `k`. But its complement
  preservation (`comp c' ≈ comp c`) and GetPut still assume that the slots
  stay where they were.
- **The slots do not always stay where they were.** In Lua 5.4 the stack is
  a `realloc`ed vector:
  - `luaD_precall` for a C function (our `print`) runs
    `checkstackGCp(L, LUA_MINSTACK, func)`, which can *grow* the stack.
  - `OP_CONCAT` ends with `checkGC(L, L->top)`, and a GC step's
    `traversethread` can call `luaD_shrinkstack` on the running thread when
    not in an emergency collection.
  - Either one reallocates and moves **every register**. `luaV_execute`
    then re-derives `base` (`updatebase` after `Protect`).
  - Lua 5.4.6+ itself converts stack pointers to offsets around a
    reallocation (`relstack`/`correctstack`).
  - All of this is **[check against 5.4.7's `ldo.c`/`lgc.c`]**.
- **What breaks.** After such a move:
  - the lens's GetPut is *false* at the memory level;
  - "disjoint cells to disjoint memory" (R1-5 #5) holds, but with a
    different morphism before and after;
  - a lifter keyed on the entry value of `base` misreads every register
    access after the `Protect`.
- **Why the pilot misses it.** The A1 pilot arms `ADD`, `EQI` and `FORLOOP`
  never call or collect. The first arm that meets the move is `CALL`
  (already in F1) or `CONCAT` (H2).

**Idea.** Take Lua's own discipline as the representation. The view decodes
registers *relative to `L->stack`*, as offsets (`savestack`/`restorestack`),
never as absolute addresses. The frame, "every other register unchanged", is
stated on the *decoded view*, never on memory:

```lean
def viewRegs (m : Mach) : Nat → Value✗ :=
  fun i => decode m (m.stackBase + 16 * (m.ciFuncOffset + 1 + i))   -- offsets, re-read at each head
theorem view_relocate : Relocates m m' (the stack vector was memmoved, offsets preserved) →
  viewRegs m' = viewRegs m
```

`Relocates` is a single relation, proved once for the `realloc` contract
(`dlmalloc`'s: the bytes are copied, and the old block is freed). Every arm
that may move the stack (`CALL`, `CONCAT`, and later `NEWTABLE` and
`CLOSURE` through GC) then splits into two parts:

- its semantic delta, proved as if nothing moved;
- one instance of `view_relocate` at the `Protect` point, discharged by the
  generic lemma.

Combined with Idea 2's ✗ cells and R1-6 #5's low-equivalence, the
representation becomes
`Rep m s :⇔ ∀ i v, s.regs i = some v → viewRegs m i = some v`. It is
invariant under relocation by `view_relocate`, and it says nothing about
garbage.

**Core rules.**

- **L-C1's frame half becomes `view_relocate`, plus the frame on the
  decoded view.** The constant complement of R1-3 #5 is taken *up to
  relocation*: `comp c' ≈_reloc comp c`. The lens laws hold on the view
  after the view is re-read at the head. They never hold on raw memory.
- **A new law to check, L-R1 (relocation invariance):** at every dispatch
  head, the decoded view is a function of the offsets alone. It can be
  tested on the Sail trace by forcing a stack reallocation, for example a
  `print` at maximal register pressure: decode before and after, and
  compare.

**What it makes free.**

- **Stack moves under GC and calls**, in every arm and for every fragment,
  as one theorem instead of an argument inside each arm proof.
- **In F3, open upvalues** (`UpVal.v` points into the stack and is fixed up
  by `correctstack` **[check]**). They are decoded by offset too, so
  closures inherit the same lemma.

**What it costs.**

- **`Relocates`** needs the `realloc` contract from the `dlmalloc` Iris
  route, and a statement that no *other* pointer into the old stack is live
  at the head. In 5.4 that means `ci->func`, `ci->top`, `L->top`,
  `L->tbclist` and open upvalues, all rewritten by `correctstack`. This is
  `correctstack`'s correctness, proved once.
- **The view must be re-read at each head,** not carried across the arm.
  A lifter (R1-1 #5, R1-4 #4) must model `updatebase` as a re-read,
  which adds one idiom.

**Theories.**

- *Known (logic):* nominal sets and equivariance. A. Pitts, *Nominal Sets:
  Names and Symmetry in Computer Science*, CUP 2013. Addresses are atoms,
  and a relocation is a permutation of atoms. `Rep` must be equivariant,
  which is exactly `view_relocate`. Stating `Rep` only through offsets makes
  equivariance hold by construction.
- *Known (physics):* gauge invariance, with the physical content independent
  of the choice of coordinates. The standard move is to state laws on
  gauge-invariant quantities. Here the gauge is the stack's base address,
  and the invariant quantity is the offset. (Analogy; any GR or gauge-theory
  text.)
- *Known (verified GC):* moving collectors and mutator views. McCreight,
  Shao, Lin and Li, "A general framework for certifying garbage collectors
  and their mutators", PLDI 2007 **[check the author list]**. The
  mutator's abstract heap is stated up to the collector's relocation.
- *Novel:* mirroring the interpreter's own relocation discipline
  (`savestack`/`restorestack`) in the refinement's representation, so that
  the proof follows the same invariant the C code relies on.

**Pilot.**

- **Cheapest falsifier, no Lean:** run `f4_strlite.lua` (or any F1 program
  with `print` at high register pressure) on Sail. Log `L->stack` at every
  dispatch head.
  - If the address **never changes** for any F1–F4 program in the suite,
    the objection is theoretical for now. Record that, and defer the idea
    to F2, where table allocation makes GC routine.
  - If it **does change**, the lens and SL proposals as written cannot
    prove the `CALL` arm, and this idea is required.
- **A1 arm:** add `CALL print` to the A1 pilot set as the fourth arm. It is
  already in F1, and it is the first arm where this matters.
- **Smallest build:** `viewRegs` by offsets, `Relocates`, and
  `view_relocate` for a `memmove`d vector. The `dlmalloc` side comes from
  the existing port.

**Seed.** *disband*: the stack is disbanded and regrouped elsewhere.
*unfellable*: the view survives the move. *cardiology*: the dispatch head is
the heartbeat at which the view is re-read. *irrigator*: the contents flow
to a new channel, and their order is kept.
