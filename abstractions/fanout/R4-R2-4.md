## Three facts round 1 missed (they drive ideas 1, 2 and 4)

1. **Offset +16 of a `TString` belongs to two different fields.** `lobject.h` declares `union { size_t lnglen; struct TString *hnext; } u;` at +16.
   - For a **long** string, +16 holds `lnglen`. It is immutable, `TStringRepr.long` reads it (`rd64 m (ts + tstringLnglenOff)`), and so do `luaS_eqlngstr` (`a->u.lnglen`) and `l_strcmp` (`ld s2,16(a0)` at `0x8001a730`).
   - For a **short** string, +16 holds `hnext`, which the runtime rewrites in *live* strings. `tablerehash` does so in `lstring.c:72`, and `luaS_remove` (called from GC's `freeobj`) does `*p = (*p)->u.hnext` in `lstring.c:170`, writing the **predecessor's** +16.
   - Long strings also have +10 (`extra`) and +12 (`hash`) written lazily by `luaS_hashlongstr` (`lstring.c:55–56`). That function is called from `ltable.c:167` on every table access with a long-string key, which is F2.
2. **`TStringRepr.long` does not say `shrlen = 0xFF`, and `l_strcmp` branches on it.** At `0x8001a720` there is `lbu s2,11(a0); li a5,255; bne s2,a5,…`, and the same for `a1` at `0x8001a734`. `lstring.c:160` sets `ts->shrlen = 0xFF` for every long string. `TStringRepr.long` (Repr.lean) states the header tag, `lnglen`, the bytes and the NUL, but **not** byte +11. So no transfer of `TStringRepr` to machine memory lets the LT/LE path decide which branch `l_strcmp` takes on a long string. The relation is missing a fact, and moving facts around cannot supply it.
3. **FORPREP keeps a caller-saved register alive through memory, not through a callee-saved register.**
   - Around `luaV_tointeger`: `sd a5,16(sp)` at `0x8001e1e0` (also at `0x8001e2ac`, `0x8001f844`, `0x8001f9d0`), the call `jal luaV_tointeger` at `0x8001e1fc`, then `ld a5,16(sp)` at `0x8001e200`.
   - Around `__udivdi3`: `sd a5,16(sp)` at `0x8001f868` and `0x8001fa88`, the call, then `ld a5,16(sp)` at `0x8001f870` and `0x8001fa90`.
   - `a5` is `ra`'s address, which the post-call stores need (`sd a4,16(a5)`, `sb a4,24(a5)`).
   - The out-parameter `sp+40` is in the same C frame `[sp, sp+176)`, so it is in `Win`, **not** a separate "cframe outside" chart.

## 1. Variant-read footprints: `Repr` predicates written in read atoms, with the support computed

**Acts on:**
- (c) **R4-R1-3 #5** (holed complement). It makes +16 a hole "of every object". That deletes `lnglen`, which both `luaS_eqlngstr` and `l_strcmp` read, so EQ-long becomes *unprovable*.
- (c) **R4-R1-5 #2** and **R4-R1-1 #5**. Both use a contiguous `fp (.str s) = [x, x+24+len]`. That range covers +10 and +12 (written by `luaS_hashlongstr` in F2) and, for short strings, +16 (`hnext`, written by `luaS_remove`/`tablerehash` under GC). Their own caveat ("exclude +0, +9, +16") contradicts their `fp` for long strings, where +16 is `lnglen`.
- (a) **R4-R1-4 #1** (`Sealed`). It has the right footprint shape (it excludes +0, +9, +12), but includes +16 unconditionally, and its `Sealed` instance is hand-proved per `Repr`.
- (c) all five M-str variants, through fact 2: transferring facts cannot discharge LT-str's premise, because the needed fact is missing from `TStringRepr`.

### The idea

Underdistinction is the bug. A footprint is not a property of "a string". It is a property of **the reads a `Repr` constructor makes**, and those depend on the variant: offset +16 means `lnglen` in one constructor and `hnext` in the other. So stop writing footprints by hand.
- Write the constructors of `TStringRepr` (and later `TableRepr`, `UdataRepr`) in a tiny **read-atom language**: `Atom := rdEq w off v | bytesEq off bs`. A constructor is a list of atoms relative to `ts`.
- The support is computed: `supp atoms ts = ⋃ [ts+off, ts+off+w)`.
- `Sealed` (R4-R1-4 #1) is proved **once, for the language**, not per predicate.

Widen `long` by one atom, `rdEq 1 11 0xFF`. This is the only relation edit, and the boot witness checks it (`check_tstring` already reads every string). With it, `l_strcmp`'s branch at `0x8001a72c` is decided.

The footprint obligation in `ValRepr.str` (R4-R1-2 #1 (a), R4-R1-4 #1: an index, not a new `Core` field) becomes `supp (atoms v) ts ∩ Win = ∅`, which is variant-exact:
- short: {+8, +11, contents, NUL};
- long: {+8, +11, +16..23, contents, NUL}.

GC's +16 writes on short strings, and the F2 hash writes at +10/+12, miss the support *by computation*.

### Core rules

```lean
inductive Atom | rdEq (w off v : Nat) | bytesEq (off : Nat) (bs : List UInt8)
def Atom.holds (m : Mem) (ts : Nat) : Atom → Prop          -- total reads (bytesT*), getD 0
def supp (as : List Atom) (ts : Nat) (a : Nat) : Prop       -- computed
theorem atoms_sealed (as) (ts) : Sealed (fun m => as.All (·.holds m ts)) (supp as ts)   -- ONCE
def strAtoms : List UInt8 → List Atom                      -- variant chosen by length (strTag)
theorem TStringRepr.toAtoms : TStringRepr m ts s → (strAtoms s).All (·.holds m ts)       -- once
theorem Core.str_atoms : Core p c s w → s.regs j = some (.str t) → slot j holds ts →
    (strAtoms t).All (·.holds c.σ.mem ts)                 -- L4-str, via Core.frame + atoms_sealed
```

L4-str′ along a run, `Sealed.along` (R4-R1-4 #1), needs only `supp ∩ writes = ∅`. When the run's writes are a region-tagged log (M-log), that is one `decide`.

### What it makes free

- **LT/LE on strings with no premise**, which no round-1 M-str variant achieves alone: `shrlen = 0xFF` is now a relation fact.
- EQ/EQK-long's `lnglen` reads, kept *against* the holed-complement design.
- **F2/GC robustness for free.** The `luaS_remove`, `tablerehash` and `luaS_hashlongstr` writes miss the computed support. No per-arm footprint lemma is needed at CALL, CONCAT or GETTABLE.
- **Every future `Repr`** (a table's `array`/`node` fields, which its `realloc` changes) gets `Sealed` by construction.

### What it costs

- Restating `TStringRepr` over atoms, or proving `toAtoms` against today's inductive: about 60 lines.
- `atoms_sealed`: about 40 lines.
- One boot-witness check (+11 = 0xFF for every long string).
- The `ValRepr.str` index, as in R4-R1-4 #1.
- What gets harder: presence-based `BytesAt` users (`TStringRepr.inj`) must go through `toAtoms`'s converse, or keep both forms. Long-string `extra`/`hash` are deliberately outside the support, so nothing may ever rely on a long string's hash. Only F2 does, and it recomputes the hash.

### Theories

- **Dynamic frames** (Kassios, FM 2006). Known **(recalled; check)**. The read clause becomes *computed*.
- **Why-provenance of a query** (Buneman, Khanna, Tan, ICDT 2001) **(recalled; check)**, from databases. Known. The support is the lineage of the predicate's truth.
- **Tagged-union footprints**, from linguistics: homographs (one spelling, two words, disambiguated by context). Known as linguistics. Using it as "the footprint is indexed by the discriminant" is **novel** here.
- **Measurability w.r.t. a generated σ-algebra**: `atoms_sealed` says the predicate is σ(supp)-measurable. Known (standard).

### Pilot

- **Smallest build:** `Atom`, `supp`, `atoms_sealed`, `strAtoms`, `TStringRepr.toAtoms` with the widened `long`, and the boot check.
- **Zero-build falsifier first:** try to prove `TStringRepr.long … → rd8 m (ts+11) = some 0xFF`. It fails, which confirms fact 2 in minutes.
- **EQ long:** `Core.str_atoms` ×2, then `luaS_eqlngstr` (lengths at +16), then `memcmp` (idea 5's sign quotient and round-1's scan).
- **LT strings:** the same, plus the +11 atom deciding `0x8001a72c`.
- **FORPREP, IDIV, LOADNIL, MODK:** not affected.

### Seed

*Underdistinction* (two fields share one offset), *chamberlet* (the 8-byte room at +16 with two tenants), *unaisled* (a contiguous `[x, x+24+len]` footprint with no internal partition: the bug).

## 2. Spill-promoted call frames: the frame is registers *and* the caller's spill words

**Acts on:**
- (c) **R4-R1-1 #2** (∀ρ over callee-saved registers) and **R4-R1-4 #5** (`call_poly`, frame = pins minus the clobber set). Fact 3 is a counterexample to both. At FORPREP's two calls, the live value `a5` is caller-saved, so both rules drop it. It survives only as the word `[sp+16]`, which neither rule's frame contains.
- (a) **R4-R1-5 #5**. Its argument-parametric footprint `Fp f (cellsOf args)` is exactly what the memory half needs: `luaV_tointeger` writes `*a1 = [sp+40]` and its own frame below `sp`, and *not* `[sp+16]`.
- (c) **R4-R1-2 #2** and **R4-R1-5 #5**. They place `sp+40` in a chart `cframe` (R4-R1-5: "cframe words above 0(sp)"). It is in `luaV_execute`'s C frame `[sp, sp+176)`, which is in `Win` (`Rel.lean`, `Win`). So the out-parameter does not need its own chart or a `bleachF` variant. It is a `Win` write that the ordinary `bleach` already allows. Only `0(sp)` (`kptr`) needs protection.

### The idea

At a call site, treat each caller-frame word that is stored before the `jal` and loaded after the return as a **virtual register**. Then the frame rule has one shape:
- `ρ := pins.filter calleeSaved ++ spills.filter (∉ Fp f args)`;
- `spills` is the list of `(sp+o, v)` words written by the prefix segments. It is read off the segment log as data (the generator's liveness pass already knows the reload at the return address).
- `Fp f args` is R4-R1-5 #5's derived footprint.

Reloading `ld a5,16(sp)` is then a read of a frame-carried word, which `SegSt` treats like a pin.

Summaries also become **tag-split**. FORPREP's kernel exists only for an integer limit (`forprepK` matches `[.int i, .int l, .int st]`). So `luaV_tointeger`'s summary is needed only on its tag-3 path (`0x8001ae30`: `ld a5,0(a5); sd a5,0(s1)`). That path has no `luaO_str2num` call (`0x8001ae64`) and therefore no `l_str2int` loops. Summary = ⋃ over argument tags, and a call site instantiates the one tag its kernel admits.

### Core rules

```lean
structure CallSite where pins : List Pin; spills : List (Nat × BitVec 64); args : List (BitVec 64)
theorem call_spill (S : HelperSum f) (hfp : ∀ o ∈ cs.spills.map (·.1), o ∉ S.fp cs.args := by decide) :
    Triple (SegSt jal cs.pins (ArmPay m o))
      (fun c => ∃ r m', S.post cs.args r ∧ SegSt ret (S.res r ++ cs.pins.filter calleeSaved)
                  (ArmPay m' o) c ∧ ∀ x ∈ cs.spills, bytesT8 m' x.1 = x.2)
-- tag-split summary: one HelperSum per argument tag, selected by the kernel's match
structure HelperSumByTag (f) where at : Nat → Option (HelperSum f)
```

L4-call becomes `call_spill`, proved once from `FrameMeta.memFrame_of_chain` (the memory side) and `abiFrame_of_wrChain` (the register side).

### What it makes free

- **FORPREP's four call sites** (two up/down × `luaV_tointeger`/`__udivdi3`): no frame statement, and no reliance on `a5` being re-derivable.
- IDIV keeps `s3`/`s4`/`s10`/`s6` in callee-saved registers, so it is the R4-R1-1 #2 case and costs nothing new. Its conditional second call (`__moddi3` at `0x8001f964`, only when `xor s3,s4 < 0`) is a second `call_spill`.
- **`bleachF` disappears.** `sp+40` is a `Win` write. The close only needs "no write to `[sp, sp+8)`", which is one entry of the derived footprint.
- **Future calls with spills.** `luaT_adjustvarargs` spills `t3, a4, a7, a0` around `luaD_growstack` (`0x800198c0–0x800198e8`). That is the same rule *inside a callee*.

### What it costs

- A `spills` field in the generator's call-site data: about 30 Python lines. It emits data, not proofs.
- `call_spill`: about 60 lines. `HelperSumByTag`: about 20.
- What gets harder: a spill whose offset collides with the out-parameter must be rejected by the `decide`, which is the right failure. Recursive spills (`luaD_call`) are later work.

### Theories

- **Register allocation with spill slots as stack-allocated virtual registers** (Chaitin 1982; Poletto and Sarkar's linear scan, TOPLAS 1999) **(recalled; check)**. Known.
- **Frame rule with ownership transfer of stack cells**: separation logic for assembly (Myreen and Gordon's decompilation into logic, FMCAD 2008; Jensen, Benton and Kennedy, *High-level separation logic for low-level code*, POPL 2013) **(recalled; check)**. Known.
- **Left-luggage lockers** at a railway forecourt, from logistics: an item survives a journey it does not take because it is deposited in a locker the traveller never opens. This is an analogy. Using it to state "the callee's footprint excludes the locker" is **novel** here.

### Pilot

- **FORPREP:** `call_spill` at `0x8001e1fc` and `0x8001f86c` (step > 0), with `HelperSumByTag luaV_tointeger` at tag 3. Success means **0 frame lines** and no `FpMem`/`fpFrame`.
- **IDIV:** both helpers with no frame lines.
- **MODK refactor:** `modk_call` goes from 6 lines to 1, since `divFrame` is now `pins.filter calleeSaved`.
- **Smallest build:** `call_spill` plus the `spills` data for FORPREP's step > 0 path.

### Seed

*Forecourt* (the frame area in front of `sp`), *cede* (`luaV_tointeger` is ceded `sp+40`, and only that), *chamberlet* (`[sp+16]`, a small room the callee never enters).

## 3. Filtrate memory: resolve reads at the store, then forget the store (the budget carrier if M-log fails)

**Acts on:**
- (d) Round 1 resolves every read **at the load**: R4-R1-1 #3 `rd_log`, R4-R1-2 #5 `rd_apply`, R4-R1-3 #3 `rd_through`, R4-R1-4 #4 `rd8_wr8_other`, R4-R1-5 #4 `fwd`. Each keeps the written memory as a term in `ArmPay m` for the rest of the path.
- This idea goes the other way. **At each store segment, resolve the finitely many later reads once, then generalise the memory away.** Read-over-write then costs one step per (store, future load) pair, and no later goal contains a store term.
- (b) It also combines with **R4-R1-4 #5** (`Ckpt`). The boundary state becomes "pins + a read set". That is *data* the generator can print, which makes a cut's statement free without `@[irreducible]` folding.

### The idea

The rest of the path needs only a **sufficient statistic** of memory: the values at the addresses it will still read. These addresses are known statically from the generated segments (the `lbu`/`ld` addresses after this point, as `RelPtrs` terms). The generator emits that read set `R_k` per boundary as data.

One lemma, `forget`, turns `SegSt pc pins (ArmPay (writeMap8 m a v) o)` into `∃ m', SegSt pc pins (ArmPay m' o) ∧ Reads m' R_k vals_k ∧ FrameEq m' m (Win)`:
- each `vals_k` entry is discharged once, at this store;
- `FrameEq` is the close's exactness fact (`Core.frame`'s form), carried as one hypothesis.

After `forget`, memory is a free variable. A guarded load in a later segment is `Reads.get` (a list lookup by index, `rfl`), and whnf never meets a store chain.

If M-log's measurement shows that `omega` was *not* the cost, this idea still removes the other suspects: `timeout at whnf` on `writeMap8` towers, and the 557k fallback unification against them.
- No goal ever contains a store chain after `forget`.
- Each separation fact that is still needed is proved once at the store, and with literal offsets.

### Core rules

```lean
structure Reads (m : Mem) (R : List (Nat × Nat)) (vs : List Nat) : Prop where  -- (addr, width) ↦ value
  get : ∀ i (h : i < R.length), rdW m R[i] = vs[i]!
theorem forget (hseg : SegSt pc pins (ArmPay m₁ o) c) (hR : Reads m₁ R vs) (hF : FrameEq m₁ m₀ W) :
    ∃ m', SegSt pc pins (ArmPay m' o) c ∧ Reads m' R vs ∧ FrameEq m' m₀ W      -- trivial: m' := m₁, then
-- the proof term is closed with `m₁` abstracted (generalize), so later elaboration sees `m'` only
theorem Reads.store (hR : Reads m R vs) (hsep : ∀ i, disjoint R[i] (a, w) ∨ R[i] = (a,w)) :
    Reads (writeMap w m a v) R (vs.update_at_hits v)                        -- once; hsep by one decide/omega per pair
```

- **L4-split′** (the budget). A boundary is `(pins, R_k, vs_k)`, all data. A split costs one `forget`.
- **L4-call′** (memory frame). A callee's havoc of `cstack` updates no `R_k` entry (cstack ∉ read set), by one check per pair.

### What it makes free

- **The per-load toll.** Each of FORPREP's six reads after `savestate` (`R[A]`, `R[A+1]`, `R[A+2]` tag and payload) is checked against `savestate`'s two stores once, at `savestate`, before any load. That is 12 pairs with ground offsets.
- **The close.** `FrameEq m' m₀ W` is `Core.frame` already, so `bleach` is the identity on it.
- **The budget even if M-log's heartbeat claim is false.** Goal size is constant per segment no matter how many stores precede it.

### What it costs

- The generator emits `R_k` per boundary, about 40 Python lines (data). `Reads`, `forget`, `Reads.store` and `FrameEq`: about 100 lines.
- What gets harder:
  - Reads whose address depends on a *loaded* value (`K[C]` through `ld a4,0(sp)`, or a string payload pointer) must be in `R_k` as symbolic addresses. Their separation from the stores is then not ground, and needs M-log's labels or `Ranges`.
  - A long path has a large read set early on. The pair count is |stores| × |later reads|, about 30 for FORPREP.

### Theories

- **Memory SSA and mem2reg**: promoting memory to SSA values at defs (Chow, Chan, Liu, Lo and Streich, CC 1996; LLVM's `mem2reg`) **(recalled; check)**. Known.
- **Sufficient statistics** (Fisher 1922), from statistics: the read set is a statistic of memory that is sufficient for the rest of the path's behaviour. Known in statistics. As a proof-state compression this is **novel**.
- **Projection pushdown** in query optimisation (Selinger et al., SIGMOD 1979) **(recalled; check)**, from databases: drop columns no later operator reads. Known.

### Pilot

- **The cheap falsifier:** in MODK seg2, `forget` after `savestate` with `R = [K[C].tag, K[C].val, R[B].tag, R[B].val]`, then time the `K[C]` tag read. If it stays above 10k with the store chain gone, the cost is pin arithmetic, not memory, and only M-cut (ideas in **R4-R1-4 #5**/**R4-R1-3 #4**) carries the budget.
- **FORPREP:** one declaration per exit, with `forget` at `savestate` and at each return.
- **IDIV, MODK:** the same pattern.
- **Smallest build:** `Reads`, `forget`, `Reads.store`, and a hand-written `R` for MODK seg2.

### Seed

*Filtrate* (keep only what passes into the rest of the road), *kilogram* and *jow* (a unit toll per load, paid once at the store instead), *cede* (cede the memory term and keep the receipts).

## 4. Comprehension log entries: a store loop's closed form is a quantified write-log entry

**Acts on:**
- (c) **R4-R1-3 #2** (`Cell` with `writes : List (Int × Src)` relative to one pointer `p₀ + s·i`), **R4-R1-2 #4** (`iterate_affine`, one pointer `x ↦ x + c`) and **R4-R1-4 #3** (`slotCopy`). Counterexample: `luaT_adjustvarargs`'s copy loop (`0x80019874–0x80019894`) does four stores per pass over **three bases**:
  - `sd a5,16(a6)`: `L->top`, the *same* address every pass, last writer wins;
  - `sd a2,-32(a5)` and `sb a2,-24(a5)`: the destination lattice;
  - `sb zero,-16(a4)`: a *tag-only* nil on the source lattice.

  `a4` and `a5` both have stride 16, but their offset is `L->top − ci->func`, a run-time value, not an `Int` literal. A one-pointer cell cannot state this loop.
- (c) **R4-R1-3 #2**'s close `bleach_tile` ("a tile whose lattice lies in `Slots`") and **R4-R1-2 #4**'s held-out claim "RETURN0/1 at 0 new lemmas". `OP_RETURN0` (`lvm.c:1749–1751`) sets `L->top = base − 1` and nils `ci->nresults` slots. That lattice starts at `func`, **outside** `Slots`, and its length is the caller's count, not `maxstacksize`. `L->ci = ci->previous` also changes `w`. The iterate lemma is reusable, but the close is not, so that pilot would measure the wrong thing.
- (b) It combines M-loop with **R4-R1-1 #3** / **R4-R1-5 #4** (region-tagged log). The closed form is a log entry, so the separation `decide` and the close (`bleach_log`) consume it unchanged, whatever region the lattice lies in.

### The idea

Extend the M-log entry type with two quantified forms:
- `lattice ⟨rgn, base, stride, off, width, n, val : Nat → Term⟩`: writes at `base + stride·i + off` for `i < n`;
- `last ⟨addr, val⟩` (last-writer).

A store loop's closed form is computed **from its body's log**:
- each body entry whose address is affine in the iteration counter becomes a `lattice`;
- each entry at a loop-invariant address becomes `last` with the final pass's value.

Each lattice keeps its own base, so two lattices at a symbolic distance are two entries. Their disjointness is a label fact ("old frame slots" vs "above `L->top`") or one `Ranges`-style premise. It is never an `Int` offset inside one cell.

### Core rules

```lean
inductive Ent | pt (r : Rgn) (a w : Nat) (v : Term) | lattice (r : Rgn) (b s off w n : Nat) (v : Nat → Term)
              | last (r : Rgn) (a w : Nat) (v : Term)
theorem loop_log (body : ∀ i < n, LogTriple (S i) bodyLog[i] (S (i+1)))     -- body log affine in i: by decide
    (haff : bodyLog.affineIn i = true) (hRAW : bodyLog.noCrossIterRAW = true) :
    LogTriple (S 0) (closeLog bodyLog n) (S n)                               -- ONCE (on the L4-loop′ rule)
theorem fwd_lattice : fwd (lattice r b s off w n v) a = (if a ∈ lat … then hit (v ((a-b-off)/s)) else miss)  -- once
```

- `noCrossIterRAW` (no pass reads what an earlier pass wrote) is the side condition that makes the closed form sound. For `adjustvarargs` that means the source reads `func+16i` are never written by the destination lattice, which comes from `top ≥ func + 16·(nfix+1)`. It is decided on labels.
- **L4-loop′** is `loop_log`: the per-loop content is the body log, which the generator already has.

### What it makes free

- LOADNIL: `sb zero,-8(a5)`, stride 16, one lattice entry, and `bleach_log`'s `decide` puts it in `slots`. `loadnil_loop` becomes 0 lines and `sim_LOADNIL` is a kit run plus `loop_log`.
- RETURN0/1, `luaD_precall`, `luaD_poscall`: the same lattice in a different region. Their closes belong to the cross-frame relation, which is honest about the work.
- `luaT_adjustvarargs` (VARARGPREP's relocation): two lattices plus one `last`, with no new class theorem.
- Tag-only nils (`setnilvalue` writes 1 byte) versus full-slot copies are just entry widths.

### What it costs

- Two entry constructors in M-log, plus `fwd_lattice` (an `omega` on `mod`, once) and `closeLog`: about 120 lines.
- `affineIn` and `noCrossIterRAW` checkers: about 60 lines.
- It requires M-log. Without it, this is R4-R1-3 #2's `tile` with a list of bases.
- What gets harder: lattices with a *symbolic* count `n` in `fwd`'s hit test need `a < b + s·n`. That is one `omega` with no disjunctions.

### Theories

- **Polyhedral array dataflow** (Feautrier, IJPP 1991) **(recalled; check)**. Known. The quantified entry is a write's iteration domain.

## 5. Observation-quotiented summaries: a helper is specified only up to what its caller observes

**Acts on:**
- (c) **R4-R1-4 #2**: "the exit post must state the sign as the byte difference at `firstStop`". **R4-R1-2 #1**: the resolver "returns `g (t₁ j*) (t₂ j*)` at the least `j*`". Counterexample: `strcmp`'s word-mismatch resolver (`0x8003b9a0–0x8003b9d8`) compares **16-bit halfwords**.
  - The code is `slli a4,a2,0x30` … `srli a4,a4,0x30; sub a0,a4,a5; zext.b a1,a0; bnez a1,…`.
  - When the low bytes agree, it returns `a0 = a4 − a5`, which is `256·(b − b′)`, **not** a byte difference (`ret` at `0x8003b9d8` and `0x8003b9f0`).
  - So no byte-level `g` matches the machine result, and a summary stated that way is false.
- (a) **R4-R1-1 #1**'s `lexLt_splitOn` already works on signs. This idea makes the sign quotient the rule rather than a convenience.

### The idea

A helper's result is consumed through a fixed **observation**, which the caller's code makes visible right after the `jal`:
- `srliw a0,a0,31` (LT, `0x8001e260`): the sign;
- `slti a0,a0,1` (LE, `0x8001e29c`): ≤ 0;
- `bnez a5` (`l_strcmp`'s `strcoll`, `0x8001a75c`): ≠ 0;
- `memcmp(...) == 0` (`luaS_eqlngstr`): = 0;
- `bnez a0` (`luaV_tointeger`, `0x8001e204`): success.

So a summary need only determine the **observation class** of `a0`: `Ordering` for comparisons, `Bool` for tests.

The rule is "observation-quotiented call". The generator reads the observation from the instructions after the return (data: one of five shapes). The summary is stated in the quotient, `obs a0 = spec args`, and the caller's next segment consumes `obs a0`. Inside the callee, each exit only has to prove its exit value lies in the right class. For `strcmp`'s halfword exits, that is `sign (256·d) = sign d` plus the lane facts: one lemma per *shape* of exit (byte, halfword), not per exit.

### Core rules

```lean
inductive Obs | sign | isZero | leZero | nonZero
def Obs.cls : Obs → BitVec 64 → Ordering ⊕ Bool
structure QSum (f) (o : Obs) where spec : List (BitVec 64) → Ordering ⊕ Bool
                                     ok : ∀ args c, … → o.cls a0 = spec args
theorem call_obs (S : QSum f o) (hobs : nextSegConsumes ret o = true := by decide) : …   -- once
theorem sign_shift (d : Int) (k) : sign (2^k * d) = sign d                               -- once; covers 6 strcmp exits
```

**L4-call** is the value half of the summary contract. The contract is *weakened to what the caller observes*, so it is never stated per helper beyond its `spec`.

### What it makes free

- `strcmp`'s 6 word-loop exits and 2 byte exits. Each one proves `sign` only, so the 6 per-exit lane lemmas of R4-R1-2 #1 collapse to `sign_shift` plus the shared lane split.
- `memcmp` and `luaS_eqlngstr` only need `isZero` (the `subw` byte difference at `0x800361d4` never matters).
- `luaV_tointeger`'s success flag. In time, the soft-float comparisons (`__gedf2`, `__ledf2`, 71 call sites in `luaV_execute`) are all consumed by `bltz`/`bgtz`/`blez`, which is a sign observation.

### What it costs

- `Obs`, `QSum`, `call_obs`, `sign_shift`: about 70 lines.
- The generator classifies the post-return instruction: about 20 Python lines (data).
- What gets harder: a helper whose result is used *both* arithmetically and as a test, such as `__moddi3`'s value then `beqz`, needs `Obs.exact`. That is the current summary, so nothing is lost.

### Theories

- **Abstract interpretation with Galois connections** (Cousot and Cousot, POPL 1977). Known. The summary lives in the abstract domain that the context's observation induces.
- **Contextual / observational equivalence** (Morris 1968) **(recalled; check)**. Known. Two results are equal if no observing context tells them apart.
- **Lumpability of Markov chains** (Kemeny and Snell, *Finite Markov Chains*, 1960) **(recalled; check)**, from probability. A chain observed through a partition is still a chain iff the partition is lumpable, and the observation here is lumpable by construction. Known. The application is **novel**.

### Pilot

- **LT strings:** `l_strcmp` as `QSum sign` over `strcoll = strcmp` (`QSum nonZero` inside the loop) and `strlen` (`exact`).
- **EQ long:** `memcmp` as `QSum isZero`.
- **Smallest build:** `call_obs`, `sign_shift`, and `strcmp_sum` in the quotient, with the halfword exit at `0x8003b9d8` as the test case that the byte-difference variants get wrong.

### Seed

*Illusionistic* (the magnitude is an illusion), *dullity* (only the coarse class is real), *jow* (a bell tolls one bit), *cruentation* (the caller's next instruction gives away what it wants).
