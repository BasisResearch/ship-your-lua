## I1. Kernel-mirror zip: no f2i summary, no feasibility filter; split on the guard's Bool and enumerate atoms only at the leaves

**Acts on:**
- (c) **R5-R1-3 #5** (E: Galois connection + order closure), **R5-R1-1 #4** (A4: layout-quotient certificates for f2i), **R5-R1-2 #3** (`f2i_canon`), **R5-R1-4 #1** (`ChainShape`), and **R5-R1-5 #1**'s claim that "L5-chain's fold is a must";
- (c) the feasibility machinery of **R5-R1-1 #3** (`gen_compare`, decided by search), **R5-R1-3 #1** (Φ) and **R5-R1-4 #2** (`feas`);
- (c) the "case on the atom, 4 children" step of **R5-R1-3 #1** (A2 `at_run_fork`) and **R5-R1-4 #2**;
- (b) it combines **R5-R1-1 #3** (AnswerTree) with **R5-R1-5 #2** (`relOf`).

### The idea

The kernel is lvm.c-literal, all the way into the f2i chain.
`Lua.Num.flttointeger` (`Arith.lean:89`) consists of:
- `floor n`;
- `!(beq n f)`;
- for ceil, `add f 1`;
- `numberToInteger f` = `le minIntF f && lt f minIntF.neg`, then `toInt64`.

These are exactly the atoms the machine chain queries:
- `floor`;
- `__eqdf2(n, f)`;
- `__adddf3(f, 1.0)`;
- `__gedf2(f, −2^63)`;
- `__ledf2(f, 2^63)`;
- `__fixdfdi`.

`ltIntFloat` etc. (`Arith.lean:151–177`) mirror `LTintfloat` down to the
`f > 0` fallback. So once atoms are identified (I2), the machine tree and
the kernel tree query the **same** atoms. Their agreement under a *free*
valuation is pointwise. On an infeasible valuation both sides compute the
same Boolean function, so they agree there too.

Consequences:
- The f2i chain needs no summary, no Galois lemmas, no order closure and no
  layout certificate. Its forks are just more forks of the arm's tree.
- The 6 layouts differ only in each fork's idiom polarity, which `relOf`
  (R5-R1-5 #2) absorbs at the fork.
- No feasibility filter is needed anywhere on the arm side.
- R5-R1-5 #1 already proved that soundness needs no feasibility. Its claim
  that `norm` must *fold* the chain is wrong: the kernel is not folded, so
  folding the machine side would *create* a mismatch.

**Objection to "4 children per atom".** `cases oᵢ` at a fork gives 4
children, but only 2 machine continuations. FORLOOP's first fork,
`__gedf2(step, 0)` + `blez` (`0x8001e178–e17c`), sends `{lt, eq, none}` to
`0x8001edf0`. So `cases o₁` runs that side's suffix (`__ledf2`, `slti`,
the φ at `e194`, the tail) **3 times**. That is the 8.3k replay
reintroduced one level down.

The fix is to split on the guard's **Bool**:
- `by_cases h : relOf Ge blez o₁`, which gives 2 children;
- keep `o₁` symbolic, with `h` as a hypothesis;
- enumerate the atoms only at the leaf, where the check is closed.

### Core rules

| rule | statement | decided at a use site by |
|---|---|---|
| `fork_bool` | at a branch whose guard is `ι.eval (R.S x y)`: by `R.spec` and `idiom_fork` (R5-R1-5 #2), the guard is `relOf R ι (F64.compare x y)`. FORLOOP: 16 points. LT int×flt branch: 4⁴ compare atoms × 2 Bool atoms (`intfitsf`, `i < fi`) = 1024 points, closed `Fin`/`Bool` data. **No search.** |

**Symbolic data.**

### Theories
- **Stemmatics / collation of manuscript witnesses** (Lachmann's method;
  P. Maas, *Textkritik*, 1927; known). Two copies of one text are collated

  Here lvm.c is the archetype, and the ELF and `Semantics.lean` are two
  witnesses. **Novel** use: the collation unit is the macro, and polarity
  flips are "orthographic".
- **Translation validation** (Pnueli, Siegel & Singerman, TACAS 1998;
  Necula, PLDI 2000; known): a per-instance check against a fixed checker.
- **Ordered decision diagrams over a shared variable set** (Bryant 1986;
  known): equal functions over the same variables need no feasibility
  reasoning.

## I2. Atom keys by congruence: identify atoms before enumerating them, which subsumes the symmetry group

**Acts on:**
- (c) the symmetry group of **R5-R1-1 #2**, **R5-R1-3 #2** and **R5-R1-4 #1**: tables, `Site` descriptors and `canon` are more than needed;
- (a) it extends the `generalize`/`atomize` step of **R5-R1-1 #3**, **R5-R1-2 #2** and **R5-R1-3 #1**, which all leave out *what* is generalised;
- (b) it combines that step with **R5-R1-5 #1**'s `norm`, applied to atom *arguments*, not to trees.

### The idea

`generalize` makes one variable per **syntactically distinct** term. Take
FORLOOP's bound:
- the machine compares `limit` with `Adddf3.S st i` (`__adddf3(step, idx)`
  at `0x8001e164–e168`, then `__gedf2(limit, idx′)` at `0x8001e180–e188`);
- the kernel tests `Float.Model.le (Model.add i st) l`.

Generalised as they stand, these are **two** atoms, and the 16-point
`decide` becomes 64 points with mismatches. So `decide` returns `false`.
Every answer-tree proposal relies on this step and none specifies it.

The step is a **fixed, confluent normaliser on atom arguments**, run once
before `generalize`:

| rewrite | from | example site |
|---|---|---|
| bridge in | `F64.op (ofModel x) (ofModel y) → ofModel (Model.op x y)` (the existing `*_eq_model`) | everywhere |
| commutative orientation | `Model.add x y → Model.add y x` when `y ≺ x` in a fixed atom order (register index) | FORLOOP's `__adddf3(step, idx)` |
| compare orientation | `compare x y` with `y ≺ x` `→ (compare y x).swap`, with the idiom's `relOf` composed with `swap` | GTI's `__gedf2(x, imm)` against the kernel's `lt imm x`; FORLOOP's `__gedf2(step, 0)` against `lt 0 st` |
| literal fold | `ofModel (ofNat 1)`, `ofModel minIntF`, `ofModel zero` → closed bit literals | f2i chain, FORLOOP, FORPREP |
| integer guards to trichotomy atoms | the machine's `blt a b` / `blt b a` / `bge` on symbolic `BitVec 64` → `Ord.compare a.toInt b.toInt ∈ S`; the unsigned `intfitsf` test `(2^53 + i) ≤ᵤ 2^54` → `intfitsf i` | LT's `i < fi`; `intfitsf` (`Arith.lean:149`) |

So the group `⟨σ, ν, β, τ⟩` collapses:
- `σ` is the compare/commutative orientation;
- `ν` (the `2`/`−2` unordered codes) and `β` (branch polarity) are absorbed by
  `relOf` over `code R` on 4 points (R5-R1-5 #2);
- `τ` (the sign flip for sub) is spec-only, the one lemma `F64.sub = add ∘ neg`.

No `Fin 8`/`Fin 16` table, no `canon_spec` and no site descriptor recognised
from instruction words are needed.

### Core rules

| rule | decided by |
|---|---|
| `AtomNF`: a `simp only` set of the rewrites above, terminating by the atom order. Soundness is each rewrite's own lemma (existing bridges, `add_comm`, `compare_swap`, two closed literal facts, `BitVec.slt_iff_toInt_lt`, one `intfitsf_machine` lemma). | **syntactic** rewriting, no search. The atom order is a fixed key (register index, then constant index) emitted with the arm. |
| `atomize`: after `AtomNF`, `generalize` every `compare _ _`, every integer trichotomy and every `intfitsf` | syntactic |
| failure report | if `leaf_zip` (I1) fails, an `#eval` lists the atom pairs that differ only by a rewrite missing from `AtomNF`. That names the missing rule. | computation (a debugging tool, not part of the proof) |

**Symbolic data** stays inside the atom arguments, and the normaliser
rewrites them as terms. Two semantically equal terms that `AtomNF` fails to
identify cause a loud `decide` failure, never an unsound proof.

### What it makes free
- **O-ord everywhere**: GTI/GEI's swapped immediates, FORLOOP's
  `model_add_comm` line, FORPREP's `lt 0 st`.
- **The 17 idioms × 3 routines**: no descriptors.
- The same normaliser is the **level-(b) control plane's atom step** (I4).
  One mechanism serves both levels, which is what R5-R1-4 #5 asked for
  ("`Mesh.valid` and `plane_eq` … should be one definition").

### What it costs
- About 40 lines: the `simp` set, the atom order, `intfitsf_machine`.
- **Harder**: confluence. The orientation rules need a total order on atom
  arguments that is stable under bridging. Registers by index, constants by
  K-index, literals last is enough for the arms in the pilot. A nested data
  atom (`floor (add i st)`) is ordered by its head first.

### Theories
- **Congruence closure** (Nelson & Oppen, JACM 1980; Downey, Sethi &
  Tarjan, JACM 1980; known) and **hash-consing** (Ershov 1958; known):
  identify terms before counting them.
- **Taxonomic synonymy** (the ICZN principle of priority; known in
  biology). Species are counted only after synonyms are resolved to one
  valid name, or the census double-counts. **Novel** as a proof-engineering
  rule: "resolve synonym atoms before enumerating valuations".

### Pilot
- **FORLOOP**: `AtomNF` maps `compare limit (add st i)` and the kernel's
  `le (add i st) l` to one atom. Measure: does the 16-point `decide` pass
  with `AtomNF` and fail without it? That is the cheapest falsifier, one
  `#eval`.
- **LT**: the integer atoms `i < fi` and `intfitsf i` through the
  trichotomy rule.
- **SUB**: the control. Only the bridge rewrite fires (`Subdf3.S x y` with
  `ofI i` partners).
- **FORPREP**: `lt 0 fs` orientation, and `beq fs 0` against `__eqdf2`.
- **Smallest thing**: the `simp` set with comm orientation + compare
  orientation + bridge, on FORLOOP.

### Seed
`Zx` twice, separated by `4Caez7Lg`: one term in two contexts that must be
recognised as one. The `54` right after is `intfitsf`'s `2^54`, the
integer-guard atom.

## I3. One sticky law under five names: pick the `F64.shr` form, and fix what it does not cover (checked numerically)

**Acts on:**
- (b) it merges the five variants: **R5-R1-1 #1** (`odd`), **R5-R1-2 #1** (`ro`), **R5-R1-3 #3** (`StEq`), **R5-R1-4 #4** (`Stamped`/`rto`) and **R5-R1-5 #3** (R3a–f). It names the cheapest one;
- (c) it objects to their "free for `__muldf3`/`__divdf3`" claims;
- (a) it extends **R5-R1-1 #1**'s `cancel_le_one` and **R5-R1-4 #4**'s rule 6 into the one margin lemma that add and sub both use.

### Same mechanism, cheapest member

All five are Boldo–Melquiond's innocuous double rounding over `Nat`.
- `StEq` (R5-R1-3 #3) is the *relational* closure: more general, but its
  `round_congr` must re-derive `target` agreement from scratch.
- `odd`/`ro`/`rto` are the same function under three names.

The cheapest member is **R5-R1-2 #1's form**: `ro` plus `shr_ro`, stated
against the **existing** `F64.shr` (`F64.lean:101`). The reason is that
`F64.round` (`F64.lean:121`) already consumes `shr`'s `(q, rb, st)` triple.
The hand-off is then:

```
shr_ro : 2 ≤ s → F64.shr (ro n k) false false s = F64.shr n false false (s + k)
target_ro : 1 ≤ n / 2^k → target (ro n k) (e + k) = target n e
⇒ round_ro : k + 2 ≤ (target n e − e) → round neg (ro n k) (e + k) false false = round neg n e false false
```

plus `ro_add_even` and `ro_sub_even` for the aligned add and subtract.

### Law check (Python transcription of `F64.target`/`shr`/`rne`/`round`, 200k random cases)

| law | failures | note |
|---|---|---|
| `ro (A·2^k + Y) k = A + ro Y k`, `A` even | 0 | |
| the same with `A` odd | 195,140 / 200,000 | the parity side condition is load-bearing |
| `A − ro Y k = ro (A·2^k − Y) k`, `A` even, `Y ≤ A·2^k`, `ro Y k ≤ A` | 0 | R5-R1-4 #4's own derivation holds |
| `shr (ro n k) s = shr n (s + k)` as triples, `s ≥ 2` | 0 | |
| `round` invariance, margin `≥ k + 2`, exponents down to −1200 (subnormal targets included) | 0 / 103,225 | |
| the same with margin `= k + 1` | 949 failures | the "+2" is exact |

So the M-odd core is safe to build. The check is not a proof.
- File: `scratchpad/laws.py`, about 40 lines, re-runnable.
- The matching Lean pre-check is `#eval` on the same transcription.

### Objection: mul and div are not "free" (R5-R1-1 #1 §4, R5-R1-2 #1, R5-R1-3 #3, R5-R1-4 #4, R5-R1-5 #3)

On rv64i, the cores of `__muldf3` and `__divdf3` are **callee compositions**:
- `__muldf3` calls `__muldi3` 4× (`0x8002e5d8`, `e5e8`, `e5f8`, `e608`), the
  128-bit product from 32-bit halves, and `__clzdi2` 2×;
- `__divdf3` calls `__udivdi3` 3×, `__umoddi3` 3× and `__muldi3` 3×
  (`0x8002e154–e2a8`): `udiv_qrnnd` by halves with an estimate-and-correct
  step **[recall: longlong.h `__udiv_qrnnd_c`]**.

`round_ro` applies only **after** an exact-core lemma, and none of the five
proposals supplies one:
- for mul: the four partial products reassemble the 106-bit product, with
  carries;
- for div: the quotient and remainder are exact after the correction.

Neither is a rounding fact. They are where mul and div cost lives, and they
want the `Muldi3Spec`/`DivSpec` batteries plus a two-limb arithmetic lemma.

Add, sub and `__floatdidf` call `__clzdi2` once each (`0x8002d838`,
`0x8002eb0c`, `0x8002f228`). Their normalise stage therefore needs
`clz64 x = 63 − log2 x` for `x ≠ 0`, as a callee-spec fact. It is small,
but it is not in `SoftFloatSpec`.

### Core rules and decision

| rule | decided by |
|---|---|
| `srs_ro` (the one variable-`BitVec`-shift lemma, `1 ≤ k ≤ 63`) | rewrite at the SRS node; the side condition is the dominating guard's hypothesis (`omega` on literals) |
| `ro_add_even`, `ro_sub_even` | rewrite; `2 ∣ A` from `A = f <<< 3` (`slli …,3` at `0x8002d7d4`/`d7d8`), by `omega` |
| `margin`: after the aligned op, `target − e ≥ k + 2`. It merges `cancel_le_one` (exponent difference ≥ 2 ⇒ at most 1 bit of cancellation) and "exponent difference ≤ 1 ⇒ `ro` exact, since the low 3 bits are 0". One lemma, by cases on the exponent difference `d`. | linear after `generalize n.log2 = L` with the `log2` bounds lemma; `omega` |
| `round_ro` | rewrite; its side condition is `margin` |

Symbolic data: the mantissas, `d` and `L` stay atoms. `2^k` appears only
inside rule hypotheses that match syntactically.

### What it makes free
The finite×finite clause of `F64.add`/`F64.sub` (`F64.lean:144,159`). Its
`normalize (sgnApply … ± sgnApply …) (min e₁ e₂) false` *is* the exact
value that `round_ro` returns. The six inlined round sites and five SRS
sites of add (5/5 of sub) are all the same rewrites.

### What it costs
- About 150 lines of `Nat`. `target_ro` needs `Nat.log2` of `n / 2^k ||| b`,
  the one awkward lemma.
- **Not covered:**
  - matching the lifted DAG's nodes to these stages: the generator cut
    (R5-R1-2 #5's chambers, or R5-R1-3 #4's node assertions);
  - the exact-zero sign (`normalize … false`) on `x + (−x)`.

### Theories
- **Round to odd** (Boldo & Melquiond, *IEEE Trans. Computers* 57(4), 2008;
  known).
- **Materiality in financial auditing** (known: e.g. ISA 320). Below a
  threshold an item is recorded only as "nonzero, immaterial". An audit at
  a coarser granularity reaches the same opinion as long as the threshold
  sits at least two levels below the reporting unit. **Novel** analogy, and
  the margin check confirms "two levels" is exact.

### Pilot
- **`__adddf3` (held out)**: `srs_ro` at the SRS node `N_8002dcd0` (R5-R1-1
  #1 names it), `ro_add_even` at `0x8002dce8`, `margin`, `round_ro`. Region:
  finite×finite, both signs.
- **`__subdf3` (held out)**: the same rules. The new lines are only the
  unpack node's sign flip and NaN test (`0x8002e9e4–e9f0`: `snez`/`seqz`/`or`
  on `b`'s exponent and fraction, before the flip).
- **Arms**: none.
- **Smallest thing**: `Lua/Num/RoundOdd.lean` with `ro`, `shr_ro`,
  `target_ro`, `round_ro`, `ro_add_even`, `ro_sub_even`, `margin`. Go/no-go:
  `round_ro` under 200k without `BitVec`.

### Seed

bits zero, the even anchor. The `06` right after the first: the `k = 0`

## I4. Compare routines by trichotomy atoms and a raw-lex bridge, with no machine conjugacy

**Acts on:**

### Evidence

`__gedf2` (`0x8002e3c0`, 36 instructions) and `__ledf2` (`0x8002e460`, 37)
share their first 11 instructions (the unpack). After that they diverge in
block order and polarity:
- `__gedf2`: `bnez a7; bnez a6 → li a0,-2`;
- `__ledf2`: `bnez a7; li a0,2; beqz a6; ret`.

A `difflib` alignment of the mnemonic sequences matches 79.5% (74% with
operands). `__adddf3` against `__subdf3` matches 54% by mnemonic and 14.6%
with operands, and their longest common run is 39 instructions. So a
machine-side conjugacy certificate would be mostly non-trivial node pairs.
Spend the effort on proving each routine directly.

**Objection to free Bool atoms (R5-R1-4 #5).** The machine decides the
order with *two* tests per field:
- exponents: `blt a2,a3` then `blt a3,a2` (`0x8002e434`/`e438`;
  `0x8002e4e4`/`e4e8` in `__ledf2`);
- fractions: `bltu a5,a6` then `bgeu a6,a5` (`0x8002e43c`/`e444`).

A free valuation of these as Bool atoms includes `a < b ∧ b < a`. On that
point the machine and `F64.compare` disagree, so the plane `decide` is
**false** unless it gets a feasibility filter.

I2's trichotomy rule removes the problem: each pair is one atom
`Ord.compare a b`, with 3 values.

### Core rules

| rule | decided by |
|---|---|
| lens (R5-R1-2 #5): `(x >>> 52) &&& 0x7ff = bexp x`, `x &&& (2^52−1) = frac x`, `x >>> 63 = sign x`; literal shifts only | rewrite; `BitVec.toNat` lemmas with literals |
| trichotomy atoms (I2): each `blt`/`bltu` pair on the same two fields becomes `Ord.compare` of the fields | syntactic |
| **`raw_lex`** (`compare_key` in lexicographic form): for finite nonzero `x`, `y` of the same sign, `(Ord.compare e₁ e₂).then (Ord.compare m₁ m₂) = (Ord.compare (bexp x) (bexp y)).then (Ord.compare (frac x) (frac y))`, where `e`, `m` come from `cls` (`F64.lean:60`), so they include the hidden bit and the subnormal `e = −1074` | proved once; R5-R1-4 #5's "real hazard" goes away because `m` carries the hidden bit, so `e = −1074` with `m < 2^52` sorts below `m ≥ 2^52` |
| class atoms: `bexp = 2047`, `frac = 0`, `bexp ∣ frac = 0`, each with 2 values | syntactic, via the lens |
| the plane check | closed `decide` over sign² × class atoms × 3 × 3 ≈ 2·2·4·4·3·3 = 576 points, both sides as closed functions |

Symbolic data: the field values never enter. Only their trichotomy and class
atoms do.

### What it makes free
`SoftFloatSpec.eqdf2`, `.ledf2` and `.gedf2`, each proved directly with no
conjugacy. They are not held out, but they are the cheapest proof that the
I2 normaliser works at level (b). The same rules give `__fixdfdi`'s range
tests and `__unorddf2`.

### What it costs
- `raw_lex`: about 40 lines.
- Lens: about 30 lines, shared with I3.
- The plane form of each lifted compare `F`: these have no frame and no
  variable shift (census), so `split` on `F`'s `if`s with the atoms
  generalised should fit the budget. That is unchecked, since I may not
  read `Lua/Vm/Fn/Ledf2.lean`.

### Theories
- **Order-preserving key encodings in database indexes** (memcmp-comparable
  keys; G. Graefe, "Modern B-tree techniques", *FnT Databases* 2011; known).
  A composite key compared field by field is the lexicographic order. IEEE's
  bit layout is such a key **[recall: Kahan's design rationale; check]**.
- **Trichotomy and ordinal measurement** (Stevens, *Science* 1946; known):
  each pair of strict tests carries one ordinal datum, not two Booleans.

### Pilot
- `__ledf2` first, as a warm-up. Held-out evidence: `__gedf2` with the same
  rules and **no** new lemma; its only difference is the `−2` leaf.
- Arms: none directly.
- **Cheap falsifier**: `#eval` the 576-point table for `__ledf2` with the
  two exponent tests as free Bools. It should show disagreeing points. With
  trichotomy atoms it should show none.

### Seed
`MM` doubled, then `Mn` twice: near-twins (`IpMnz` / `tMnm`) that share a
prefix and diverge, like `__ledf2`/`__gedf2`'s common 11-instruction prefix.

## I5. Macro observations: one machine summary per lvm.c macro (`tonumber`, `tointeger`), and class views at macro granularity

**Acts on:**
- (c) the head-constructor tables of **R5-R1-3 #1** (A0, 6³ = 216 rows), **R5-R1-4 #3** (shape tables with a `str` column) and **R5-R1-5 #5** (`class_cover` over constructors), as applied to FORPREP;
- (c) **R5-R1-1 #4**'s "check whether `forlimit` is inlined": it is not (below);
- (d) none of round 1 summarised the out-of-line `tonumber`/`tointeger` macros against the kernel's own `Value.tonumber?`/`tointegerns`.

### Evidence

FORPREP's float path does not fork on int/str/other. Per register the
machine:
1. tests `ttisfloat` inline;
2. otherwise calls `luaV_tonumber_` out of line (`jal` at `0x8001e2b0`,
   `e2e8`, `e31c`);
3. tests `li a4,1; bne a0,a4 → luaG_forerror`.

Inside the callee, ints go to `__floatdidf` (`0x8001ab90`) and strings to
`luaO_str2num` (`0x8001abc0`). The coerced-limit path calls `luaV_tointeger`
(2 `jal` sites in `luaV_execute`). The f2i chain sits *inside* that callee
(`luaV_flttointeger` at `0x8001ac18`), not inlined.

So the machine's split per register is **2-way**, not 3–6-way. The kernel
matches it: `Value.tonumber?` (`Semantics.lean:102`) is
`.flt` ↦ itself, everything else ↦ `tonum?.map toFloat`. Head tables split
FORPREP into 3³ − 3 = 24 shape classes that the machine never
distinguishes. Each class then needs its own walk through the same callee
summary.

The error exits (`luaG_forerror`) are not obligations: the kernel gives
`none`, so there is no `Step`.

### The idea

The kernel is built from lvm.c macro combinators (CLAUDE.md, `opKernel`).
Make the **macro** the unit of machine summary:

| macro | machine realisation | kernel | summary |
|---|---|---|---|
| `tonumber(o, &n)` | inline flt test, else callee `luaV_tonumber_` | `Value.tonumber?` | `tonumber_obs`: one lemma for the inline+callee shape; the callee spec is stated against `Value.tonumber?` (a named premise with `Str2dFrom` for strings, until proved) |
| `luaV_tointeger(o, &i, mode)` | callee | `lim.tonum?.bind (tointegerns mode)` (`forLimit`, `Semantics.lean:365`) | callee summary proved **by I1 inside the callee**: `luaV_flttointeger`'s fork tree against `Arith.flttointeger` |
| `luai_numlt`/`le`/`eq` | call + idiom | `Float.Model.lt`/… | `relOf` (R5-R1-5 #2) |
| `cast_num` | `__floatdidf` | `ofI` | `Floatdidf.spec` |
| `luaV_flttointeger` inlined | the chain | `flttointeger` | **none**: I1's zip |

A **class view** (M-view) then has one field per macro observation, not per
constructor. FORPREP's float class becomes: three `tonumber` observations
(each `some (.flt x n)`, from inline or callee), then the kernel's float
clause over three float payloads. That is the same shape as FORLOOP's view.

### Core rules

| rule | decided by |
|---|---|
| `tonumber_obs`: from the inline tag test and the callee's spec, the slot yields `Value.tonumber? v = some (.flt x n)` with `x`, `n` read from the callee's out-parameter (`addi a1,sp,40` etc.) or from the slot | syntactic: the two shapes (inline hit, callee) are the two `fork_bool` sides (I1) on the tag test |
| `luaV_tointeger.spec` against `forLimit`'s inner `bind` | I1 inside the callee (its `FnSummary` from `gen_fn.py`, plus `leaf_zip`) |
| `forprep_view`: `ForCoerce` + kernel `some` → `ForView` with 3 `tonumber` observations, plus the step-zero and sign atoms | one theorem for `forprepK`, `cases` on its 4 edges |

Symbolic data: the payloads come out of the callee's spec as terms. The
compare atoms (`beq fs 0`, `lt 0 fs`, the bound) are I2-normalised.

### What it makes free
- **FORPREP**'s float class:
  - 8 machine paths (2³ per-register sides) instead of 24 shape classes;
  - 3 compare atoms, so I1's leaf check is 64 points.
- **FORPREP**'s coerced-limit class: one callee summary, reusable wherever
  `luaV_tointeger` is called.
- **Others**: any future arm that uses `tonumber` (`luaV_tonumber_` has 6
  call sites in the ELF, 4 of them in `luaV_execute`).

### What it costs
- `tonumber_obs` (≈30 lines).
- The `luaV_tonumber_` spec as a named premise (it depends on
  `luaO_str2num`, i.e. `Str2dFrom`; FLOAT-DESIGN already names it).
- `luaV_tointeger`'s summary through I1 (≈1 arm's worth).
- **Harder**: the out-parameter is on the caller's stack (`sp+32/40/48`).
  Reading the result back needs the callee's memory frame. That is
  `FrameMeta.memFrame_of_chain`, one `decide`, not new machinery.

### Theories
- **Isozymes in biochemistry** (known: Markert & Møller, *PNAS* 45, 1959).
  Different proteins catalyse the same reaction, and the reaction is the
  unit of classification. Here the inline fast path and the out-of-line
  callee are two isozymes of the `tonumber` reaction. **Novel** as a proof
  unit.
- **Per-construct simulation in compiler verification** (Leroy, CompCert,
  *CACM* 2009; known): prove the simulation once per source construct, here
  per lvm.c macro, and compose.

### Pilot
- **FORPREP** (held out): `forprep_view` + 3 `tonumber_obs` + I1/I2 over 64
  points for the float loop; the coerced limit via `luaV_tointeger.spec`.
  Target ≤ 10 lines, and no per-constructor case.
- **LT, SUB**: `luai_numlt` and `cast_num` are macro observations already
  (`relOf`, `Floatdidf.spec`). I5 adds nothing there, so they are the
  control.
- **FORLOOP**: its view (3 float payloads, R5-R1-5 #5) is I5's
  `forloop_view` with no `tonumber`.
- **Smallest thing**: `tonumber_obs` with the callee spec as a premise, on
  one FORPREP register. Count the classes against R5-R1-3 #1's table.

### Seed
The string starts `1h1` and ends `Z1`. `1` is the boundary code, i.e.
`luaV_tonumber_`'s success return tested by `li a4,1; bne` at each call.
`977s7` is three equal digits behind one gate: the three identical FORPREP
call sites behind one inline tag test each.
