## 0. Positions on the two hard questions (they recur below)

**(Q1) Redefine `Step`/`LuaSem` as data, or keep the inductive and prove it equal once?**

"Prove equal once" is not once. The equivalence `Step ↔ kernelStep` is a
per-rule proof. Every new opcode or construct adds an arm to it, forever.
That arm costs about what a `step?_sound` + `step?_complete` pair costs today,
because it *is* that pair with the stepper renamed. So keeping the readable
inductive keeps the census's per-case slope in its cheapest form (about 2
lines per rule, if a generic tactic closes most arms), but not at zero.
Redefinition gives zero.

My position is to **redefine**, and to buy back readability with two derived
artefacts, each proved once:

- one-line constructor lemmas (R1-5 #1's "pedomorphism");
- **one derived inversion principle**
  `Step s s' → ∃ o, KernelCase o s s'`, which `simp [spec]` splits.

The one refinement: the redefinition must be **stable under F2/F3**, or the
bake-off winner has to be torn up again at F2. Ideas 2 and 3 below are about
exactly that.

**(Q2) Which idea actually reduces A1, and what is its cheapest falsifier?**

Only two kinds of round-1 idea shrink the A1 *count or per-arm size*:

- **Count reducers:** R1-5 #4, R1-6 #4 (orbits) and R1-3 #2 (LZ templates).
- **Per-arm-to-computation reducers:** R1-1 #5, R1-2 #5 and R1-4 #4 (lifters and parsers).

The others shrink only the frame half of each arm:

- R1-3 #5 (lens);
- R1-5 #5 (tether);
- R1-6 #5 (low-equivalence).

The coordinator's arm data already refutes the pure group-action form of the
orbit ideas (idea 4). The cheapest falsifiers, in order of cost:

1. the clade measurement of idea 4: a text exercise on the ELF, hours, no Lean;
2. the tag-alphabet count of idea 5: the same disassembly, plus reading the branch instructions;
3. lifting the ADD arm alone (R1-1 #5, R1-2 #5, R1-4 #4): weeks.

Run 1 and 2 before anyone builds a lifter.

## 1. Clobber-above edges: kernels whose edges carry a havoc boundary

**Acts on.**
- **(c)** R1-5 #1, R1-2 #1, R1-2 #4 and R1-6 #2 all give CONCAT the write set `[A]`. SUITE H2 already corrects that to the range. I argue that even the range may be too small.
- **(b)** It combines the converged kernel (R1-4 #1 RCW / R1-6 #1 Micro) with R1-6 #5 (`havoc_ni`) and R1-1 #3 (X registers). The result is one edge shape covering CALL, CONCAT and every later `checkGC` opcode.

**The idea.** An edge is a triple, not a pair:

```lean
structure Edge where
  tgt  : Nat
  wr   : List Nat    -- registers the kernel writes, with values it computes
  top  : Option Nat  -- havoc boundary: registers ≥ top are unspecified afterwards
```

The kernel computes values only for `wr`. Registers at or above `top` are
don't-care: the machine may leave anything there, and the program may never
read them before writing them. Everything else is the frame.

The concrete counterexample to a write set of `{A}`, or even of
`A..A+B-1`, is `OP_CONCAT` in 5.4.7 **[check]**:

```c
L->top = ra + n;  ProtectNT(luaV_concat(L, n));  checkGC(L, L->top);
```

- `luaV_concat` leaves `L->top = ra + 1`.
- `checkGC` may run a GC step with that top.
- In the atomic phase, `traversethread` marks the stack only below `top` and
  then **sets every slot from `top` to `stack_last` to nil**
  ("clear dead stack slice", `lgc.c` **[check]**).

So after a CONCAT, every register `≥ A+1` may be nil'd, *including registers
above `A+B-1`*. Whether that happens depends on the GC phase, which is not in
the VM state. From the semantics' point of view it is a havoc of `[A+1, ∞)`,
exactly like CALL's clobber (`Fragment.lean` already says "a `CALL` clobbers
every register from its base up"). SUITE H2 checked `lvm.c:649-650` and
`679-682`, but not the `checkGC` that follows the concat.

**Core rules.**

- **L-B1 with havoc**, proved once by the kernel argument (R1-4 #1's
  `List.map_congr` on the read vector), plus one clause:
  ```lean
  theorem footprintH : agree (reads w) s t → s.pc = t.pc → s.out = t.out →
    (Step s s₁ → ∃ t₁ e, Step t t₁ ∧ e ∈ edges ∧ s₁.pc = e.tgt ∧ s₁.out = t₁.out ∧
       agree e.wr s₁ t₁ ∧ ∀ r ∉ e.wr, r < e.top.getD ∞ → s₁.regs r = s.regs r)
  ```
- **The semantics stays deterministic.** `Step` writes a fixed reference
  value above `top`: "unchanged", which is today's choice for CALL. A
  separate one-line relation `s ≈[<top] s'` says the machine only needs to
  match below `top`.
- **`keepMask` disappears as a special case.** The must-analysis becomes
  ```lean
  st[t] ∩= (st[pc] ∩ rmask 0 (e.top.getD 256)) ∪ e.wr
  ```
  for every edge, not only CALL's. This is the Kildall lemma of R1-6 #5,
  proved once, over edges.
- **`cbcSem_iff` and its CONCAT twin are one theorem**, `havoc_certain`:
  ```lean
  Supported p → (BcSem p out ↔ ∀ havoc choices, BcSemH p out)
  ```
  This is R1-1 #3's certain answer, but with havoc only on declared tops. It
  needs no `Option Value` threaded through every register, and so no X
  arithmetic anywhere.
- **At A1** the representation relation is low-equivalence below the
  current *must-mask* (R1-6 #5). CONCAT's arm is not asked what GC did to
  `R[A+1..]`.

**What it makes free.**
- Every clobbering opcode, now and later:
  - CALL;
  - CONCAT (H2);
  - F2's NEWTABLE and SETLIST, and F3's CLOSURE and VARARG, all of which
    `checkGC` **[check the list]**.

  Each costs one `top` field and no proof. Today CALL alone needs
  `keepMask` and `cbcSem_iff`, and every new clobberer would need its own
  copy.
- The H2 write-set question stops mattering: whatever `luaV_concat` leaves in
  `A+1..A+B-1` is under `top = A+1`.
- The kernel no longer has to transcribe `luaV_concat`'s intermediate
  values, which would otherwise put the "as many strings as possible per
  pass" loop into the trusted text.

**What it costs.**
- About 40 lines on top of whichever kernel wins: the `top` field, the extra
  clause in `footprint`, the generalised `sweep`, and `havoc_certain`.
- A trust point. `top` is new trusted content: a `top` set too low would
  let the spec ignore a clobber the machine really does. It is checked
  twice:
  - by the kernel-validated runs (a program reading above `top` is not
    `Supported`);
  - at A1, where the arm proof would fail on any register below `top` that
    the machine changes.
- Stuckness is unchanged, so progress-sensitive NI is not needed here.

**Theories.**
- *Known, logic synthesis.* Don't-care sets. A specification gives an on-set
  and a don't-care set, and any implementation that agrees on the care set
  is correct: Brayton, Hachtel, McMullen, Sangiovanni-Vincentelli, *Logic
  Minimization Algorithms for VLSI Synthesis*, Kluwer 1984. `top` declares
  the don't-care registers of an edge, and `havoc_certain` is the "agrees on
  the care set" theorem.
- *Known, memory management.* Garbage collection as a semantic rule that
  deletes unreachable bindings, proved unobservable once: Morrisett,
  Felleisen, Harper, "Abstract models of memory management", FPCA 1995. The
  dead-stack clearing is such a rule applied to the register window, and
  `top` is where "unreachable" begins.
- *Known, databases.* Codd's "unknown" null, where a query must give the same
  answer under every value: Codd, "Extending the database relational model
  to capture more meaning", ACM TODS 4(4), 1979 **[check]**.
- *Novel:* making the GC's root boundary a first-class field of a
  control-flow edge, so the dataflow analysis learns clobbers from the same
  place as writes.

**Pilot.**
- **Falsify first, in Lean, cheaply.** Take whichever kernel wins round 2 and
  add the `top` field. State `footprintH` and prove it for MOVE, CALL and
  CONCAT only. Then re-prove `cbcSem_iff` as `havoc_certain` restricted to
  those three opcodes.
  - *Falsified if* `havoc_certain`'s proof needs a per-opcode arm, or if
    `sweep`'s generalisation changes `Supported` on any existing kernel-checked
    program (`whileProto` and the difftest corpus).
- **H2:** `CONCAT A B` gets `reads = rmask A B`, one edge
  `⟨pc+1, [A], some (A+1)⟩`, and a kernel that folds right to left. 0 proof
  lines. Report whether the `checkGC` clearing is confirmed in `lgc.c`. If it
  is not, `top = A + B` and the same machinery applies.
- **H1, H3, H4:** `top = none`, unchanged from the plain kernel.
- **R1:** delete `keepMask` and fold `cbcSem_iff` into `havoc_certain`.
  Measure against the `bcSemFrom_iff`/`cbcSem_iff` lines.

**Seed.** `dXOO`: an unknown (X), then two cleared slots (O, O). And `Sz00`:
two zeros after a boundary letter. Registers past a boundary become X or
nil.

## 2. Query-tree kernels: stratified footprints that survive the heap

**Acts on.**
- **(c)** It objects to the *applicative* kernels of R1-3 #1 (`List Value →
  …`), R1-4 #1 (RCW `δ : … → List Value → …`), R1-5 #1 (port-typed) and
  R1-6 #1 (Micro). Their types fix every read address from `w` alone. That is
  exactly what F2 and F3 break. R1-6 #1 saw this for `CALL B=0`, but it is far
  wider.
- **(b)** It combines them with R1-5 #2's query monad, restricted so that the
  must-analysis still works.

**The counterexamples, per fragment.**

| fragment | opcode | what breaks in an applicative kernel |
|---|---|---|
| F2 | `GETFIELD A B C` | reads `R[B]` (static), then the heap cell `(table R[B], key K[C])`. That address is a *value* read in step 1. It also reads the metatable chain if the key is absent. The read set is `{B} ∪ cells reachable from R[B]`, so no `List Nat` from `w` can state it. |
| F2 | `SETFIELD`, `SETTABLE` | the write set is a heap cell chosen by a read value, plus rehash, which rewrites *every* node of the table. |
| F3 | `CALL A 0 C`, `RETURN A 0`, `SETLIST A 0 C`, `VARARG A 0` | the register range is `A..top`, where `top` was set by the previous instruction. The read set depends on state that is not in `w`. |
| F2 | `TFORCALL`/`next` | the successor key is not a function of the visible state (see idea 3). `Option` is the wrong codomain. |

**The idea.** A kernel is a **query tree** whose nodes come in two kinds:

```lean
inductive QT (α) where
  | reg  (r : Nat)              (k : Value → QT α)   -- r is a function of (p, pc, w) ONLY: static
  | cell (ℓ : Value → Loc)      (k : Value → QT α)   -- address computed from an earlier answer: dynamic
  | top                         (k : Nat → QT α)     -- the variable-results boundary (F3)
  | pick (o : OracleId)         (k : Nat → QT α)     -- a Host-oracle answer (idea 3)
  | leaf (a : α)
```

`Step` is the graph of `run : QT Out → State → Option State`, and L-B2 is
definitional, as in the converged idea.

The **stratification** is typed. Register queries have static addresses, so
`labels_reg (T w)` is a projection and the `Supported` must-analysis keeps
working unchanged. Heap queries may have dynamic addresses, but they are
never needed by the must-analysis, because the register window is its whole
domain.

**Core rules.**
- **L-B1 in its general form is the certificate lemma**, proved once by
  induction on `QT`:
  ```lean
  run T s = r ∧ log T s = Q → agreeOn Q s t → run T t = r ∧ log T t = Q
  ```
  - The log `Q` is the *dynamic* footprint: registers and heap cells.
  - The register part of `Q` is contained in `labels_reg (T w)`, the static
    one.
  - For F1 and H1–H4, `Q` is exactly the static set, so this *is* today's
    L-B1.
- **Frame for writes.** Leaves write registers (static) and cells whose
  locations were read earlier in the same path. "Writes only what it has
  addressed" is one lemma: every written `Loc` occurs in the log. This is the
  dynamic-frames discipline.
- **L-B2 under choice.** With `pick` nodes, `Step` is no longer a function.
  The law becomes
  ```lean
  Step s s' ↔ ∃ ω, runω T s ω = some s'
  ```
  or, with a fixed oracle (idea 3), `Step_H s s' ↔ run_H T s = some s'`. Either
  is one generic theorem.

**What it makes free.**
- Everything the applicative kernels make free for F1 and H1–H4, at the same
  cost. Register queries in a fixed order are an applicative kernel written
  in continuation style.
- It keeps that at F2 and F3. The bake-off winner does not have to be
  replaced when tables arrive. That is the hidden cost of the applicative
  kernels: they win H1–H4, then need a second abstraction for GETFIELD.
- The per-rule definite-initialisation argument for `top`-relative opcodes
  (`CALL B=0`). The `top` query is a static register-range query relative to
  a *tracked* boundary. The must-analysis tracks `top` as one extra abstract
  value, as `lcode.c` does with its `freereg`/`MULTRET` convention **[check]**.

**What it costs.**
- About 60 more lines than an applicative kernel: continuation style, the
  log, and the certificate lemma.
- The kernel table reads less like a record. `reg b fun x => reg c fun y =>
  match x, y with …` is closer to `lvm.c`'s `vRB`/`vRC`, so arguably no worse.
- The `Σ e : Fin n, Vector …` typing of the RCW/port ideas (edge index,
  exact write arity) is lost. Edges become leaves, as in R1-6 #1, and the
  static edge list is `leaves (T w)`. That is fine for F1. For heap-writing
  leaves the "edge" carries only the register part.

**Theories.**
- *Known, program logic.* Dynamic frames: a footprint is a state-dependent
  set computed by the program itself, and framing is stated against that
  set. Kassios, "Dynamic frames: support for framing, dependencies and
  sharing without restrictions", FM 2006. The `cell` node is a dynamic frame
  and the `reg` node a static one.
- *Known, databases.* Query answering under binding patterns: an access
  method can be called only once its input attributes are bound, so plans are
  *adaptive* chains. Rajaraman, Sagiv, Ullman, "Answering queries using
  templates with binding patterns", PODS 1995 **[check]**. `cell (ℓ : Value →
  Loc)` is an access with a bound input, and the stratification says register
  accesses have no inputs.
- *Known, statistics.* Adaptive group testing, where later tests are chosen
  from earlier results: Dorfman, "The detection of defective members of large
  populations", Ann. Math. Statist. 14, 1943. The log of an adaptive
  procedure is its certificate. That is the same content as R1-5 #2's
  query-complexity certificate, but it adds the *adaptive* reading that
  heap reads need.
- *Novel:* the two-stratum typing, static register queries and dynamic heap
  queries, chosen so that the fragment's dataflow check never sees the heap.

**Pilot.**
- **Falsifier, cheap, before the bake-off:** write *one* F2 opcode,
  `GETFIELD`, against a 30-line toy heap (`Loc := Nat × Key`, no metatables,
  absent key gives nil) in both an applicative kernel and `QT`.
  - *Falsified if* the applicative kernel can state GETFIELD's L-B1 with a
    static read list plus one generic heap lemma. If it can, the applicative
    kernels are fine and this idea is overhead.
  - My prediction is that it cannot without adding a second, heap-specific
    footprint theorem.
- **H1–H4:** identical to the applicative kernel. Only `reg` nodes, 0 proof
  lines. Measure the setup delta, about 60 lines, as the price of
  F2-stability.
- **R1:** as for the converged kernel.

**Seed.** `Vx…Vx` twice (`qVxQTNxT`, `NvVxst9sX`): the same opening read,
then different continuations that depend on what was read.

## 3. Nature moves first: oracle-determinised semantics, so L-A1 survives F2/F3

**Acts on.**
- **(a)** It extends R1-3 #3 and R1-4 #2 (rulebook, call trees) and the
  LL(1)/format family (R1-2 #3, R1-5 #3, R1-6 #3).
- **(c)** It objects to R1-3 #3's own mitigation, a `choose` node in `Prog`.
  That mitigation loses `Graph.det` exactly where F2 needs it, and it pushes
  the F2 `os-trace` facts back into the per-construct cost.

**The problem.** Every round-1 L-A1 idea proves "`LuaSem` is the graph of a
function". F2 and F3 bring behaviour the manual leaves open:

- the order of `next`/`pairs`;
- the text of `tostring` on tables and closures (`table: 0x…`);
- later, `collectgarbage("count")` and `os.time`.

The project already met this once and solved it the right way, twice:

- `Host.showBuiltin` makes the address in `function: 0x…` a *parameter*;
- the assignment store order is fixed to `restassign`'s.

**The idea.** Make that convention the rule for all unspecified behaviour:
**every choice is an argument, supplied before the run** (Harsanyi's
"Nature moves first").

```lean
structure Host where
  showBuiltin : Builtin → String
  showRef     : RefKind → Addr → String                 -- F2/F3: table/closure rendering
  nextOrd     : TableHistory → List Key                 -- F2: iteration order as a function of
                                                        --     the table's operation history
  wf          : NextOrdValid nextOrd                    -- enumerates live keys exactly once, stable under
                                                        -- assignment to existing fields (manual §6.1, `next`)
```

The semantics stays a function *of `(H, c)`*, so determinism, graph and
interpreter soundness remain one generic theorem. Allocation addresses need no
oracle. The source semantics is stated **up to address renaming**: tables are
atoms of a nominal set, and `LuaSem` is equivariant. Only `showRef`, the one
observation of an address, goes through `H`.

**Core rules.**
- **L-A1 relative to a host**, the same generic `Graph_iff_run`/`holds_det` as
  R1-3 #3 and R1-4 #2, with `H` a fixed parameter. It needs no `choose` node
  and no per-arm `choose`-freedom check.
- **Validity is one predicate, checked once.** `NextOrdValid` is the manual's
  contract for `next`. Every program-level fact that needs "each key once"
  uses it instead of re-deriving it per trace. This is the `os-trace`
  cluster's facts about `next`.
- **The headline theorems change quantifier, once.** Layer A/B become
  ```lean
  ∃ H, H.wf ∧ HaltsWith elf out ∧ LuaSem H c out
  ```
  "The ELF's output is one the manual allows". Or, if the ELF's own order is
  wanted, `∀` over the host *the layout induces*. The existing
  `endToEnd_of_layers` composition is unchanged, because `H` is shared by both
  layers, as it already is for `showBuiltin`.
- **Equivariance** (one theorem, by induction on the rulebook, since rule
  bodies never inspect atoms except by equality): `π • ρ` evaluates as `ρ`
  does, with outputs related by `π`.

**What it makes free.**
- L-A1's generic proof keeps working through F2 and F3. Without this, the
  first `pairs` loop either breaks determinism (a `choose` node, and back to
  per-construct inversion around it) or forces the semantics to model
  `luaH`'s hash layout, which puts `lua_Hash` seeding and node order into the
  trusted text.
- The bytecode side gets the same oracle for `TFORCALL` (idea 2's `pick`).
  Layer B's translation validation then compares two *deterministic*
  semantics under the same `H`, which is the easy case.

**What it costs.**
- `TableHistory` must be part of the source state: one list per table,
  append-only. That is roughly R1-5 #5's monotone output resource, but per
  table.
- Stating `NextOrdValid` faithfully is the real work. It must include
  "assigning `nil` to an existing field during traversal is allowed"
  (manual §6.1 **[check wording]**).
- The `∃ H` form is weaker than "the ELF's order is this function". Anyone
  wanting that must supply the layout-induced host: `luaH_next` over node
  order as a Lean function. That is the cost this idea *defers*, not
  removes.
- **On the TCB question (Q1):** this is what makes redefinition safe. A
  redefined, data-shaped `LuaSem` is tied to determinism. Without an
  oracle discipline, F2 would force it back to a relation.

**Theories.**
- *Known, economics and game theory.* The Harsanyi transformation: a game of
  incomplete information becomes a game of imperfect information with one
  initial move by Nature, after which play is determined by strategies.
  Harsanyi, "Games with incomplete information played by 'Bayesian'
  players, I–III", Management Science 14, 1967–68. `H` is Nature's move.
- *Known, probability.* Kolmogorov's sample space: randomness is a
  deterministic function of an outcome `ω` drawn once. Kolmogorov,
  *Grundbegriffe der Wahrscheinlichkeitsrechnung*, 1933.
- *Known, refinement.* Prophecy variables resolve future nondeterminism in
  advance so that a refinement mapping exists: Abadi and Lamport, "The
  existence of refinement mappings", TCS 82(2), 1991. `nextOrd` is a
  prophecy of every future traversal.
- *Known, PL and logic.* Nominal sets and equivariance: Gabbay and Pitts, "A
  new approach to abstract syntax with variable binding", FAOC 13, 2002;
  Pitts, *Nominal Sets*, CUP 2013. Allocation is determinate up to
  permutation of atoms.
- *Novel:* making the history-indexed oracle, not the table's layout, the
  trusted semantics of `next`, with the manual's traversal contract as its
  single validity predicate.

**Pilot.**
- **H1–H4:** no oracle is needed. `H` is unchanged, and the pilot measures the
  rulebook (R1-3 #3 / R1-4 #2) as in round 1. This idea is **not measured by
  H**. Say so.
- **Cheapest falsifier (paper plus 50 lines of Lean):** add a toy `pairs`
  over an association-list table to the rulebook. Prove that `holds_det`
  still goes through with `H` fixed and no per-construct lines.
  - *Falsified if* any construct's arm needs to inspect `H.wf` to stay
    deterministic. Determinism must come from `H` being a function, not
    from its validity.
- Then check `NextOrdValid` against the `os-trace` cluster: can its 18 facts
  about `next` be restated as corollaries of `wf`?

**Seed.** `cnnnF`, the only triple: `next`, `next`, `next`. A traversal
whose order is fixed before it starts.

## 4. Arm clades: shared-stem proofs over a phylogeny of arm CFGs, not orbits

**Acts on.**
- **(c)** It objects to R1-5 #4 and R1-6 #4 (orbits under a group action),
  using the coordinator's measurements.
- **(b)** It combines what survives of them with R1-3 #2 (anti-unification)
  and R1-4 #4 (idiom patterns as DAGs).

**The objection.** A group action needs *whole-arm* isomorphism up to one
substitution. The measured data shows something else:

- **BAND/BOR/BXOR, LTI/LEI/GTI/GEI, ADDK/SUBK, LT/LE** are
  *instruction-identical on the opening straight-line code*. They share a
  **stem** and diverge later: at the ALU instruction, or at a shared tail
  reached by different jumps.
- **ADD vs SUB differ in one branch sense.** That is block layout: gcc
  inverted a conditional and swapped fall-through. It is not a kernel
  substitution. `Relabel.apply` of R1-6 #4 (a positional substitution plus a
  shift) does not map one to the other.
- **MUL vs MULK differ by register allocation and scheduling.** They are
  outside any orbit of R1-5 #4's action, which renames only registers dead at
  the head. A scheduling change is not a renaming.

So the similarity is a *tree*: common stems and divergent tails. It is not a
*quotient*. Proof reuse should follow the tree.

**The idea.**
1. Represent each arm as its **CFG from the dispatch head to the dispatch
   head, modulo layout.** Blocks are nodes, branch polarity is normalised
   (the "taken" successor is the one whose condition holds), and fall-through
   is an explicit edge.
2. Build the **clade tree** of all arms by maximal shared rooted sub-CFG,
   modulo α-renaming of dead machine registers. This is hash-consing on
   rooted CFGs: the internal node of a clade is its stem.
3. **A stem is proved once**, as a triple with a *symbolic* postcondition.
   The postcondition names symbolic machine values (for example
   `x0 = payload(R[B])`, tag checks passed) and does not yet say which spec
   rule applies:
   `{Rep s ∗ head} stem {∃ σ, Rep s ∗ Sym σ}`.
4. **A tail** (the ALU op plus the store and dispatch, a few instructions)
   is proved per opcode, starting from `Sym σ`.

**Core rules.**
- **L-C1 by clade composition**, proved once:
  ```lean
  theorem clade_sim : StemSpec c → (∀ o ∈ leaves c, TailSpec o c) → ∀ o ∈ leaves c, ArmSim o
  ```
  Composition is sequential composition of triples at the stem's exit
  points.
- **Layout invariance**, proved once over Sail's branch semantics: the CFG
  modulo polarity and block order determines the head-to-head behaviour.
  Normalisation needs a *relocation* lemma only for the one branch per block,
  not R1-6 #4's global relocation invariance of the decoder.
- **Membership is a decidable check, not a proof.** "Arm `o`'s CFG is the
  stem `c` followed by tail `t_o`" is a comparison on normalised CFGs.

**What it makes free.** Every stem obligation below the first member of a
clade. With the measured families:

| stem family | members |
|---|---|
| bitwise rr | BAND, BOR, BXOR (SHL/SHR probably separate: they call `luaV_shiftl` **[check]**) |
| order immediate | LTI, LEI, GTI, GEI |
| order rr | LT, LE |
| arith K | ADDK, SUBK |

- ADD and SUB also join one clade: branch polarity is normalised away.
- MUL and MULK stay separate, which is honest.

The tails are small, and for the ALU-substitution pairs a tail is "one ALU
instruction refines one `BitVec` op". This keeps R1-6 #4's product-program
insight at the scale where it actually holds, a few instructions.

**What it costs.**
- A CFG extractor for the arms, plus the normaliser (polarity, order,
  dead-register α). The extractor exists in any lifter design; the
  normaliser is about 150 lines of Lean if the check is to be kernel-checked.
- Symbolic postconditions are the design risk. A stem's post must be strong
  enough for all its tails and not mention any of them. If the stems of a
  family end *after* the ALU instruction (gcc cross-jumped the store), the
  stem contains the only differing instruction. Then the clade is a
  one-instruction substitution, and R1-6 #4's transport is the right tool
  after all. The clade tree tells you which case you are in.
- It does not touch the metatheory clusters.

**Theories.**
- *Known, biology.* Cladistics: classify by *shared derived characters*
  (synapomorphies), and put a character's explanation at the clade node
  where it arose, once. Hennig, *Phylogenetic Systematics*, University of
  Illinois Press, 1966. The stem is the synapomorphy, and the tail is the
  autapomorphy.
- *Known, compilers.* Hash-consing and maximal sharing: Ershov, "On
  programming of arithmetic operations", CACM 1(8), 1958 (value numbering);
  Filliâtre and Conchon, "Type-safe modular hash-consing", ML Workshop 2006.
  Also the inverse of what gcc did: code factoring and cross-jumping,
  Debray, Evans, Muth, De Sutter, "Compiler techniques for code compaction",
  TOPLAS 22(2), 2000 **[check]**. The clade tree recovers the sharing that
  the macro expansion had and gcc partly kept.
- *Known, logic.* Anti-unification (Plotkin 1970), as in R1-3 #2. Here it is
  applied per rooted sub-CFG rather than per whole arm, which is what the
  measured partial orbits call for.
- *Novel:* a proof campaign indexed by a phylogeny of compiled code, where
  the cost is the *tree length* (the sum of stem and tail sizes), not the
  arm count and not the orbit count.

**Pilot.**
- **Falsifier (hours, no Lean, same inputs the coordinator already used):**
  extract the CFGs of all 54 F1 arms, normalise, build the clade tree, and
  report the **sharing ratio**, Σ unshared blocks / Σ blocks.
  - *Kill the idea* if the ratio is above 0.6.
  - *Prefer R1-6 #4's transport* if most stems end after the ALU op.
- **A1 arms ADD, EQI, FORLOOP.** Each is a clade representative, so the
  held-out set shows only overhead: the stem triple plus the tail. Add
  **SUB** (normalised with ADD) and **LEI** (with EQI's order-immediate
  family) as second members, and report their *marginal* cost. Target: under
  10 lines each.
- **H cases on the machine side** (not measured): `LT`/`LE` with strings adds
  a *new tail* to the order-rr stem (`lessthanothers`, then the `l_strcmp`
  call). That is the clade way to extend a family.

**Seed.** `Hjl9` and `F3jl3`: the stem `jl`, then two different digits. A
shared prefix with divergent tails is a clade, not an orbit.

## 5. Tag-symbolic arms: partition the dispatch section by a finite alphabet, and let the kernel check path coverage

**Acts on.**
- **(a)** It extends R1-2 #5: the Poincaré section partitioned by opcode.
  Partition it further, by **opcode × tag vector × guard bits**, a finite
  Markov partition.
- **(b)** It combines that with R1-4 #5 (sort-erased core). The core
  functions' sort dispatch becomes the spec side of the same finite
  alphabet.
- **(d)** Round 1 did not separate an arm's *finite control* from its
  *data*. The predecessor's per-site row explosion was, I think, mostly
  control: one row per path per site.

**The idea.** In `luaV_execute`, an arm's branch instructions test three
kinds of thing:

1. **tag bytes** (`ttisinteger`, `ttisstring`, `ttisnil`), which are finitely many;
2. **the instruction's `k` bit**, a field of `w`;
3. **a few data predicates** (`count > 0` in FORLOOP, the result of a compare
   in EQI and LT).

Define the arm's **symbol** as the vector of branch outcomes along a path.
Every head-to-head path is then named by a word over a finite alphabet. On
the spec side, a kernel's `match` on value constructors (`| [.int x, .int y]
=> …`), together with its `if` on a guard, induces a partition of the read
vector by the *same* kinds of symbol: constructor tags and guard bits.

L-C1 splits into two parts:

- **Control conformance.** It is decidable because both sides are finite
  automata over the alphabet. For every symbol, the arm's path and the
  spec's branch agree on:
  - which edge or target they take;
  - which read registers' tags they required;
  - whether they reach the head at all, or go to a slow path.

  A slow path is `luaT_trybinTM` or `luaG_*error`, and it must be matched to
  a *stuck* spec branch, whose "never exits 0" is already `stuck_sim`'s job.
  This part is closed by `decide` on the product.
- **Data conformance per path.** Straight-line: for the path's symbol, the
  written payloads equal the kernel's values. That is a `BitVec` identity
  such as `add x y = x + y`, and there is one per *reachable conforming*
  path, not one per site.

**Core rules.**
- **L-C1 from `control_conforms ∧ ∀ path, data_conforms`**, proved once:
  ```lean
  theorem arm_sim_of_conformance :
    ArmAut o ≅ SpecAut o →                               -- product check, by `decide`
    (∀ σ ∈ acceptingPaths (ArmAut o), DataEq o σ) → ArmSim o
  ```
- **The alphabet is fixed by `Rep`.** `Rep` maps `Value` sorts to tags, so
  the arm's tag tests range only over the image of `Rep`. F1 has no floats,
  so the float branch of `EQI` or `ADD` is an *unreachable* symbol, and the
  product check prunes it by `Rep`, not by a per-arm lemma. When Float
  arrives, the pruned symbols become live and need `DataEq` entries. That
  cost is visible and bounded by the alphabet.
- **A guard bit must be explained.** Every data-predicate branch in the arm
  must map to a guard in the kernel (`n ≠ 0`, `f x y`). An unexplained guard
  bit fails the product check, which is how a real mismatch surfaces.

**What it makes free.**
- The *coverage* half of every arm simulation: which tag combinations reach
  which edge, that every spec branch is realised, and that every
  non-realised arm path is a slow path that the spec leaves stuck. That is
  the part that grows with the number of sorts, and it is where F4 strings
  and Float will multiply A1's cost. This makes it a `decide`.
- H4 at A1 is a *new live symbol* in LT/LE's automaton (`str × str`), which
  goes to the `l_strcmp` call. Its coverage is automatic, and its data part
  is the `l_strcmp` contract.

**What it costs.**
- Extracting the automaton needs the same CFG extractor as idea 4 and the
  lifters. Classifying each branch as tag, `k`, or data needs a symbolic
  evaluator for the few instructions before each branch: load byte at
  `base + 16·r + 8`, compare with an immediate. That is small, but it is
  another verified function.
- `decide` over the product must fit in the kernel. The alphabet is small
  (say, 3 read tags × about 5 sorts in the `Rep` image, plus 1 to 3 bits),
  so a few hundred symbols per arm at worst.
- It does not help straight-line data proofs. Those stay per path.

**Theories.**
- *Known, dynamical systems.* Markov partitions and symbolic dynamics: a
  partition of phase space such that the dynamics becomes a shift on words
  over a finite alphabet. Adler, "Symbolic dynamics and Markov partitions",
  Bull. AMS 35(1), 1998; Morse and Hedlund, "Symbolic dynamics", Amer. J.
  Math. 60, 1938. The first-return map of R1-2 #5, coarse-grained by tags
  and guard bits, is such a shift, and conformance is a conjugacy *of the
  symbolic systems*.
- *Known, automata.* Equivalence of finite automata by a product
  construction, decidable in near-linear time: Hopcroft and Karp, "A linear
  algorithm for testing equivalence of finite automata", Cornell TR 71-114,
  1971 **[check the number]**.
- *Known, algebra.* Order-sorted algebra, as in R1-4 #5: overloading
  resolution is a finite table from argument sorts to operations. Goguen and
  Meseguer, TCS 105, 1992. That table *is* the spec automaton.
- *Novel:* using the representation relation's *sort image* as the alphabet
  restriction, so that adding a sort (strings, floats) adds symbols and not
  proofs.

**Pilot.**
- **Falsifier (a day, mostly disassembly reading):** for ADD, EQI and
  FORLOOP, list every conditional branch between head and head, and classify
  each as tag, `k`, or data.
  - *Falsified if* any arm has a data branch that no kernel guard explains.
    A candidate is an overflow or `l_castS2U` check in FORLOOP's unsigned
    count **[check `forloop` in lvm.c 5.4.7]**.
  - *Also falsified if* any arm has more than about 12 symbol bits. Then
    the "finite control" is not small, and this degenerates to path
    enumeration.
- **Then** build the product check for EQI alone: tag of `R[A]` ∈ {int, other},
  `k`, and the compare bit. Prove `arm_sim_of_conformance` with one `DataEq`
  (none, for EQI: it writes nothing).
- **H4 on paper:** show that LT with `str × str` adds exactly one live symbol
  and one `DataEq`, the `l_strcmp` contract.

**Seed.** `HZ`, `GHZ`, `THz`: a frequency three times. Sample the machine at
the dispatch clock and read its path as a word over a finite alphabet.
