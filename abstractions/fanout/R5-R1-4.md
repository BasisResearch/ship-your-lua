## A. Twin-law quotient

### Idea

In crystallography a *twin law* is a symmetry operation (a reflection or a
rotation) that maps one individual of a twinned crystal onto the other. You
describe the twin once, by its individual and its law, not twice.

The float census is full of twins:
- **Comparison idioms.** 17 idioms over 3 routines at 93 sites. They differ by
  three operations:
  - `σ_swap`: the argument swap, with `Ledf2.S a b = −Gedf2.S b a` up to the
    unordered code;
  - `σ_pol`: flipping a branch's polarity (`beqz`↔`bnez`, `bgez`↔`bltz`), which
    exchanges the taken and fall-through leaves;
  - `σ_not`: `not` before `srli 63`, the sign test of `−a0 − 1`.
- **f2i chains.** The 6 layouts of the inlined `luaV_flttointeger` differ only
  by `σ_pol` per fork and by `j` placement.
- **Add and sub.**
  - `__subdf3` is `σ_neg · __adddf3`, with `σ_neg` the sign-bit flip of the
    second argument (L5-comm, bit for bit with NaNs).
  - The two pack joins are the same function with permuted parameters (a
    permutation twin).
- **Swapped immediates.** GTI and GEI read `[imm, R[A]]`, LTI and LEI read
  `[R[A], imm]`. The ELF calls `__gedf2(x, imm)`: another `σ_swap`.

The abstraction makes these symmetries first-class:
- a **finite descriptor type** for each twinned family;
- a **group of laws** acting on it;
- **one soundness theorem per family**, proved over the whole descriptor type
  at once, by a closed `decide`, because each routine's specification factors
  through the 4-valued `F64.compare`.

A site then contributes data (its descriptor, as a closed term), not a lemma.

### Core rules

1. **`Idiom` and `idiom_sound`** (from L5-idiom, extended by L5-comm).
   ```
   inductive Rt | le | ge | eq                    -- __ledf2/__ltdf2, __gedf2/__gtdf2, __eqdf2
   inductive Test | bltz | bgez | blez | bgtz | beqz | bnez | slti1 | sgtz | seqz | srli63 | notSrli63
   structure Idiom where (rt : Rt) (test : Test) (swap : Bool)
   def Rt.code : Rt → Option Ordering → BitVec 64       -- Ledf2.S/Gedf2.S/Eqdf2.S as a function of compare
   def Test.eval : Test → BitVec 64 → Bool               -- the instruction's meaning on a0
   def Idiom.meaning (ι) : Option Ordering → Bool := fun o => ι.test.eval (ι.rt.code (if ι.swap then o.map .swap else o))
   theorem Rt.S_factor : ∀ r a b, r.S a b = r.code (F64.compare a b)             -- 3 proofs, by unfolding (rfl/cases)
   theorem idiom_sound : ∀ ι a b, ι.test.eval (ι.rt.S (if ι.swap then b else a) (if ι.swap then a else b))
                                 = ι.meaning (F64.compare a b)                    -- S_factor, compare_swap, then decide
   ```
   - **How it is decided.** The core is `∀ ι o, … = …` over
     `Idiom × Option Ordering`, which is 3·11·2·4 = 264 closed cases. That is
     one `decide` (not `+kernel`) on a `Fintype`-free enumeration (a `List`
     of all descriptors plus `List.all`).
   - **At a site.** The site's descriptor is a closed literal, emitted from the
     ELF as data next to the existing `f<k>_eq`. `idiom_sound ι` instantiates
     by syntactic match, and `ι.meaning` reduces by `rfl` on the literal `ι`
     to a `match` on `F64.compare a b`.
   - **Symbolic data.** `a` and `b` stay symbolic, and only the compare
     result is cased (4 cases), never their bits.
2. **`subdf3_twin`** (from L5-comm). One theorem:
   `Subdf3.S a b = Adddf3.S a (b ^^^ 2^63)`, that is, `F64.sub a b = F64.add a (F64.flipSign b)`.
   - On `F64`: case on `b.cls`, using `cls_neg` (already proved, the
     `Cls.negate` lemma). It is decided by `cases`, plus `rfl` per `Cls`
     constructor (5 shapes).
   - The machine side, `Subdf3.F m s r a b = Adddf3.F m s r a (b ^^^ 2^63)`, is
     *not* proved by this abstraction. Proving it would be a per-routine
     equality. See "What it costs" for how the twin is still used.
3. **`ChainShape` and `chain_sound`** (from L5-chain, with L5-idiom's
   polarity). A descriptor
   `ChainShape := { mode : F2Imod, pols : Fin 4 → Bool, eqPresent : Bool, ceilAdd : Bool }`
   with `pols` from the ELF. Statement:

   > For every shape `sh` consistent with its mode, and every outcome
   > valuation `v : Valn` of the chain's call atoms (`floor x`, then
   > `compare (floor x) x`, `compare (floor x) 2^63`, `compare (floor x) −2^63`;
   > each an `Option Ordering`), the machine's leaf `sh.leaf v` and
   > `F64`'s `tointegerns mode x` agree on whether the result is `some`.
   > On `some`, the value is `Fixdfdi.S (floor x)`.

   It is decided in two parts:
   - **Control.** Closed `decide` over `ChainShape × Valn`: 3 modes ·
     2^4 polarities · 4^3 outcomes ≈ 3k cases. **Check this count**: if
     `decide` is slow, split it by mode, giving three `decide`s.
   - **Data.** Once per mode: the relation of `floor`'s specification and
     `Fixdfdi.S` to `Lua.Num.flttointeger` (`F2Ieq` needs
     `floor x = x ⇔ compare (floor x) x = some .eq`). This sits behind the new
     named premise `FloorSpec` that L5-chain already asks for.

**Search.** None. Every rule fires by syntactic match on a closed descriptor
literal. What is computed is the descriptor's `meaning` or `leaf`.

### Theories

  **[recalled]**).
- **Symmetry reduction by orbit representatives** (known): Burnside and Pólya
  counting, and in verification Emerson & Sistla, *Symmetry and model
  checking*, FMSD 9 (1996) **[recalled]**. We use only the idea of "prove
  per orbit", not a model checker.
- **Factorisation through a finite quotient** (known): the routine's answer
  depends on its inputs only through `compare`. This is a *characteristic
  function* argument, as in the universal property of a quotient.

## B. Rack-and-pinion mesh: the machine fork tree meshed with the kernel's class tree

### Idea

A cog railway's pinion engages a fixed rack tooth by tooth. The engagement
is decided by geometry, not by trying teeth.

FORLOOP has two trees:
- **the kernel's outcome tree.** After L5-read′, the classes are the leaves
  and the kernel guards are the forks;
- **the machine's fork tree.** A shared prefix, then forks at `e17c` (`blez`
  on `__gedf2(step, 0)`) and `e194` (`bnez` on the bound idiom).

The proof today splits on the kernel first and then replays the machine 4×
(8.3k redundant), choosing polarity by trial (10.3k).

The mesh makes the **machine's fork tree the proof's skeleton**:
- **the rack.** A closed data term `ForkTree`, read off the ELF:
  - prefix segment ids;
  - at each fork, the guard as a descriptor over *call atoms*;
  - the leaves.
- **the pinion.** The kernel's class tree as a function of the same atoms.
- **the mesh.** A map from machine leaves to kernel classes (many-to-one
  allowed, for f2i). Its validity is one closed `decide`.

One theorem, `sim_of_mesh`, turns a valid mesh plus the per-leaf segment runs
(already generated) into `SimArmOn o Q`. The run is built along the tree:
- the prefix is executed once;
- at a fork the proof cases on the *atom* (an `Option Ordering`, 4 cases);
- the guard then reduces to a literal `Bool` by `decide`, so the right branch
  lemma is chosen by computation and no closer is tried.

### Core rules

1. **`ForkTree` and its denotation** (from L5-fork).
   ```
   inductive Atom | cmp (k : Nat) | cls (k : Nat) -- call k's compare answer, or a tag/class read
   inductive ForkTree
     | leaf (id : Nat)
     | seg (segId : Nat) (t : ForkTree)                -- a generated SegSt→SegSt segment
     | call (site : Nat) (t : ForkTree)                -- a call node with its f<k>_eq summary
     | fork (g : Idiom × Atom) (taken notTaken : ForkTree)
   def ForkTree.leafOf : ForkTree → (Atom → Option Ordering) → Nat   -- which leaf a valuation reaches
   ```
   Denotation: `ForkTree.wp (Wp : MachWP M) T Φ`, by structural recursion,
   using `wp_segW` for `seg` and `wp_callW` for `call` (both exist in
   `VsaIris/MachWP.lean`), and for `fork` `if g.meaning (val atom) then wp
   taken else wp notTaken`.
   - **Decided by** structural recursion on a closed tree. Its unfolding is
     `simp only [ForkTree.wp]` on a literal, a syntactic match.
2. **`KClass` and `classOf`** (from L5-read′). Per kernel combinator (not per
   arm), a function `classOf : (Atom → Option Ordering) → Class` such that
   `κ.body vs = some (outcomeOf (classOf val))` under the register view (C).
   - Proved once per combinator: `opArith`, `docondjump`, `forloopK`,
     `forprepK`, unary `setR`. That is 5 theorems, by unfolding the
     combinator and using `lt_eq_model`/`le_eq_model`/`compare_eq_model`
     (proved).
   - **Symbolic data.** Float payloads stay symbolic. Only their compare
     answers are atoms.
3. **`Mesh.valid`** (the mesh law). For a closed `T`, a closed class function
   `κc`, and a closed `μ : Nat → Class`:
   `∀ val : Valn T, μ (T.leafOf val) = κc val`.
   - The atoms are finitely many and each has 4 values, so this is a closed
     `decide` over `4^|atoms|`. That is 16 for FORLOOP, and **≤ 4^6 for
     LT/LE: check the atom count; if 4^k > ~10^4, decide per subtree**.
   - **Inconsistent valuations.** A valuation that no input realises (say
     `compare a b = lt` together with `compare b a = lt`) need not be meshed
     correctly. Atoms are therefore chosen *independent* where possible (one
     atom per call, not per idiom). Where two atoms are dependent (the f2i
     chain's `compare (floor x) x` and the range tests), the mesh may map the
     infeasible combination arbitrarily, *but then validity is false on it*.
     So `Mesh.valid` takes a closed **feasibility filter** `feas : Valn → Bool`,
     proved sound once per atom family. Example: `compare_swap` makes
     `(cmp a b, cmp b a)` pairs feasible only if swapped.
4. **`sim_of_mesh`** (the one bridge).
   ```
   theorem sim_of_mesh (T : ForkTree) (κc) (μ) (hμ : Mesh.valid T κc μ feas = true)
     (hfeas : FeasSound T feas) (hpre : EntersTree o Q T)          -- dispatch to T's root; from `dispatch` + C's view
     (hleaf : ∀ ℓ ∈ T.leaves, LeafCloses ℓ (μ ℓ))                  -- store/jump tail per class: generated segments
     : SimArmOn o Q
   ```
   - **Decided by**: `hμ` by `decide`, `hfeas` by a lemma per atom family,
     `hleaf` by the generated tail segments (`at_close` today) per *class*,
     not per leaf when several leaves share a tail.

**Search.** `hleaf`, *if* a leaf's tail is closed by `at_close` as today: that
is search over closers. But the class, and so the tail's target and stores,
is now a literal, so `at_close` has one candidate. **Flagged**: it is still
the existing tactic, with the polarity search eliminated.

### What it makes free

- **Heartbeats.**
  - The prefix replay (8.3k for FORLOOP) and the polarity trial (10.3k) go
    for every arm. That is the bounded 18.6k of 59.1k (31%), measured.
  - For the 16 FORLOOP-like fields the replay share grows with `fp`. The
    forecast budget breach for SHL/SHR (22 paths) and LT/LE (24) is where B
    pays. The run cost becomes *O(tree nodes)* instead of
    *O(paths × prefix)*.
- **Lines.** FORLOOP's "kernel outcomes" (4 lines) and "jump target"
  (4 lines) go. The jump target becomes part of the class's leaf data,
  `jumpTo` evaluated once inside the combinator theorem.
- **The many-to-one leaves of f2i** (L5-fork's untested refinement). These
  are exactly what `μ` being non-injective expresses.
- **C5-handoff.** `at_fcond` and `at_farith` (and the ≈10 predicted
  hand-offs) become instances: one `classOf` per combinator, and the rest is
  the generated tree.

### What it costs

- **Setup.**
  - `ForkTree` and its `wp` denotation over `MachWP`: about 80 lines.
  - `Mesh.valid` and its soundness: about 40 lines.
  - Five `classOf` theorems: about 15 lines each.
  - A generator *of data* that reads `float_paths.py`'s path enumeration and
    emits `T` per arm. This is not a proof, and the brief allows data
    generation. **Check it against "generator scripts that emit per-arm
    proofs"**: `T` is a closed term, and the proof is `sim_of_mesh` applied
    to it.
- **What gets harder.**
  - The tree is acyclic only up to the dispatch tail (`c240`), which every
    float path ends at. A leaf is "reached the dispatch tail with the class's
    stores done".
  - Arms whose float semantics sit inside a callee (EQ, EQK through
    `luaV_equalobj`) need that callee's tree as a `call` node with its own
    summary, built by the same means, one level down.
  - Atoms must be chosen so that feasibility is cheap. Badly chosen atoms
    make `feas` a real proof obligation.

### Theories

- **Rack-and-pinion gearing** (novel as an analogy): the engagement is
  determined by pitch geometry. The fork guard is likewise determined by the
  atom value, never tried.
- **Stuttering simulation and bisimulation** (known: Namjoshi, *A simple
  characterization of stuttering bisimulation*, FSTTCS 1997 **[recalled]**):
  the mesh is a simulation whose machine side stutters (prefix, calls) and
  whose kernel side moves once at the leaf. `Mesh.valid` is the finite
  "matching step" check.
- **Shared decision diagrams** (known: Bryant, *Graph-based algorithms for
  Boolean function manipulation*, IEEE TC 1986): the proof object is the
  shared tree, not the path set, which is why the cost is linear in nodes.
- **Partial evaluation's "The Trick"** (known: Jones, Gomard & Sestoft 1993
  **[recalled]**): when a value is dynamic but has finitely many possible
  values, case on it at the point of use and continue statically.

### Pilot

- **FORLOOP refactor.**
  - `T` has a prefix (`c1ec` → `e158` loads → `call __adddf3` → `call
    __gedf2(step, 0)`), then `fork(blez, cmp1)`. Each side has a `call
    __gedf2` and a `fork` (the bound idiom), then the 4 leaves.
  - `κc` is `forloopK`'s float class, a function of `cmp1 = compare st 0` and
    `cmp2 = compare l idx` or `compare idx l`, with A's swap. 16 valuations.
  - The arm is then `sim_of_mesh T κc μ (by decide) feas_ok (enter_forloop) leaves`.
  - Expected: about 4 lines (with C's view). Heartbeats: one prefix (≈3k),
    plus 4 tails (≈11k, the measured stores and tail), plus `decide`, plus
    the reductions. Forecast ≤ 25k against 59.1k.
- **LT (FltAB).** The largest tree (24 paths). This is the real test of
  "linear in nodes" and of feasibility over the f2i atoms.
- **SUB (FltBC).** Three classes (`flt` partner, `int` partner through
  `__floatdidf`, MMBIN). A small tree: a test of `classOf opArith`.
- **FORPREP (ForCoerce).** It has string coercion (`luaV_tonumber_` →
  `strtod`, a call node under the `Str2dFrom` premise), so the tree has a
  callee-summary `call` node. This tests the extension.
- **Level (b).** Not targeted. But the same `ForkTree`, over a lifted
  function's guards instead of machine forks, is E's control plane.
- **Smallest thing to build.** `ForkTree` with only `seg`/`call`/`fork`/`leaf`,
  `wp`, `Mesh.valid`, and FORLOOP's tree written by hand as a literal (data,

## C. Xenophoran class view: registers carried by the kernel class, not read by the machine

### Idea

*Xenophora* snails cement foreign shells and stones onto their own shell and
carry them everywhere. Gastromelus is a body with extra limbs attached.

In FORLOOP the machine tests one tag (R[A+2]). The other two registers' float
types are *foreign*: they come from the kernel's clause, not from any machine
read. 48% of FORLOOP's lines read them by hand (`kit_reg` ×3, `cases v0 <;>
cases v1`) and bundle their payload and model (`flt_parts` ×3).

The abstraction is a **class view**: a named-field structure produced by one
theorem per kernel combinator. Given:
- `VmRelAt p c s w`;
- the kernel's answer `κ.body (reads ↦ regs) = some out`;
- the operand predicate `Q`,

it yields the class (L5-read′) and, for every register the class names, the
attached facts:
- the constructor;
- the tag byte at `w.slot j`;
- the payload word;
- the `Float.Model` equal to `F64.toModel` of the payload;
- the NaN sign bit.

The machine never re-derives them, because they ride on the class.

### Core rules

1. **Shape skeletons.** `VShape := nil | bool | int | flt | str | fn` with
   `Value.shape`. A kernel combinator's *shape table*,
   `κ.shapes : List (List VShape × ClassTag)`, is a closed list computed
   from the combinator's match arms. For `forloopK`:
   `[([_, int, int], intLoop), ([flt, flt, flt], fltLoop)]`. For `opArith`
   with `FltBC`: `[([flt, flt], val), ([flt, int], val), ([int, flt], val), ([flt, ns], mmbin), …]`.
2. **`shape_inv`, one theorem per combinator** (the L5-read′ rule).
   `κ.body vs = some out → Q → (vs.map shape, tagOf out) ∈ κ.shapes.filter (Q-compatible)`.
   - Proved by unfolding the combinator once and `cases` on the `Value`
     constructors in the combinator's own match. That is once for `forloopK`,
     `forprepK`, `opArith` (over `fastArith`'s clauses), `docondjump ∘ δ`
     (order and eq), and unary `setR`.
   - **At a use site**: by syntactic match. The arm instantiates its
     combinator's `shape_inv`, and the table filter by the arm's `Q` reduces
     by `decide` on the closed table (`Q` gives one constructor as a literal).
   - **Symbolic data.** Only constructors are cased. Payloads stay variables.
3. **`ClassView`, a named structure, never a ∧ tower** (CLAUDE.md: new
   predicate).
   ```
   structure FltReg (w : RelPtrs) (m : Mem) (j : Nat) (x : Float.Model) (n : Bool) : Prop where
     tag   : slotTag m (w.slot j) = BitVec.ofNat 8 vNumFlt
     pay   : F64.toModel (slotVal m (w.slot j)) = x
     sign  : (slotVal m (w.slot j)).msb = n
   structure ClassView (κ) (p c s w) (cls : ClassTag) : Prop where
     regs : ∀ k ∈ κ.flts cls, ∃ x n, s.reg (κ.reads[k]) = .flt x n ∧ FltReg w c.σ.mem (κ.reads[k]) x n
     ints : ∀ k ∈ κ.ints cls, …  -- likewise, int payload
   theorem classView_of (κ) : VmRelAt p c s w → κ.body (readRegs s κ.reads) = some out → Q … → ∃ cls ∈ κ.classes Q, ClassView κ p c s w cls
   ```
   - The `∀ k ∈ list` form is **generic over `κ.reads`**: proved by
     induction on the read list using `Core`'s slot clause
     (`ValRepr … (slotTag …) (slotVal …) v`) and `ValRepr`'s `flt`
     constructor (inversion: tag 19 iff `.flt`).
   - One proof for all combinators. Only the shape table is per combinator.
4. **`FltReg` feeds the call atoms (bridge to B).** The payload in `FltReg`
   is the argument word of the soft-float call. Its `toModel` is the kernel's
   float. So `__adddf3(slotVal …, slotVal …) = F64.add …` meets
   `Float.Model.add x st` by `add_eq_model` (proved) and `FltReg.pay`. This
   is a rewrite, not a bundle.

**Search.** None. The class is chosen by `decide` on the filtered closed
table. If more than one class survives (SUB: partner `flt` or `int`), the
arm gets one goal per class, and those classes are exactly B's mesh leaves.

### What it makes free

- **FORLOOP's 14 lines** (register reads, non-float refutation and payload
  bundles): 48% of the measured 29.
- **O-pay** in 9/10 of the sample and **O-inv** in 4/10. By §1.3's
  extrapolation that is every member with a register or K partner: 12 of
  the 33.
- **`at_farith`/`at_fcond`'s one-register reads**, which are the same
  theorem at one register.
- **ADD/SUB/MUL `int` partners.** The class `[flt, int]` names the int
  register's payload, which feeds `__floatdidf`'s call atom directly.

### What it costs

- **Setup.**
  - `VShape`, the shape tables (5 combinators, closed lists), 5 `shape_inv`
    proofs (≈10 lines each), and `ClassView` with its generic proof
    (≈40 lines).
- **What gets harder.**
  - `δ`'s numeric clauses fork on `int`/`flt` *pairs*. The order kernel's
    table for FltAB has the int/float mixes plus the order-error class (a
    non-number partner gives `none`, `stuck`).
  - The `none` classes are not this arm's obligation (`SimArmOn` needs a
    `Step`). `shape_inv`'s hypothesis `body = some out` removes them, so
    the table lists only `some` classes. Good, but the order kernel's error
    class must be shown to give `none` *in `δ`*, which is the combinator's
    proof.
  - **FORPREP** reads through `tonumber?`, which *coerces strings*. The shape
    `str` maps to `flt` only after `str2number`. So `forprepK`'s table has
    a `str` column whose class depends on a parse. Its view carries a
    `Str2dFrom` result as the attachment, not a `FltReg`. This is a sixth
    attachment kind.

### Theories

- **Xenophora's foreign attachments** (novel as an analogy): the facts a
  carrier holds need not have been produced by the carrier.
- **Views** (known: Wadler, *Views: a way for pattern matching to cohabit
  with data abstraction*, POPL 1987): the arm pattern-matches on the class
  view, not on the representation.
- **Inversion lemmas, generated once per definition** (known: McBride,
  *Inverting inductively defined relations in LEGO*, TYPES 1996
  **[recalled]**). `shape_inv` is an inversion principle for a kernel
  combinator, not for an inductive.
- **Shape analysis as a finite abstract domain** (known: abstract
  interpretation, Cousot & Cousot 1977): `VShape` is the abstraction, and the
  combinator is exact on it up to the guard.

### Pilot

- **FORLOOP refactor.** `classView_of forloopK` with `Q = FltStep`. The
  table filtered to R[A+2] = `flt` leaves the one class `fltLoop`. Three
  `FltReg` facts follow. Expected: 14 lines → 1. Combined with B, FORLOOP is
  about 4 lines.
- **SUB (FltBC).** `opArith .sub`'s table under FltBC gives `{[flt, flt],
  [flt, int], [int, flt]}` (value) and `{[flt, ns], [ns, flt]}` (MMBIN).
  The views carry the `__floatdidf` input on the int side.
- **LT (FltAB).** The order table gives the bool classes with int/flt mixes,
  and the error class removed. Its classes are B's mesh targets.
- **FORPREP (ForCoerce).** This is the stress case for the `str` attachment.
  Report whether the attachment kind generalises or forces a per-arm
  special case. That is a falsification test.
- **Smallest thing to build.** `FltReg`, `ClassView` for `forloopK` only,
  `shape_inv forloopK`. Then re-prove FORLOOP's first 14 lines with it.

## D. Odd hallmark: stamped residues for level (b)

### Idea

This is the answer to the open hand-off: rounding after the sticky collapse
equals rounding the exact value, per routine. The aim is to make it **one
family of stamp-preservation rules**, so that no routine needs its own proof.

Silver gets a **hallmark**: a stamp certifying purity that survives later
working of the metal. What survives below the line (*supralineal*: what sits
above the line is kept, what sits below is the residue) is not the residue's
bits but its *class*.

The class is round-to-odd (RTO), "jamming" in hardware terms:
- the machine's SRS idiom (`srl`, `sll` by `64 − k`, `snez`, `or`) computes
  `rto_k(x) := x / 2^k ||| [x mod 2^k ≠ 0]`, the truncation with its last
  bit forced odd when inexact (L5-stage: 18,900/18,900 for `1 ≤ k ≤ 63`);
- the known theorem (Boldo–Melquiond): **RTO at `p + m` bits with `m ≥ 2`,
  followed by RNE at `p` bits, equals RNE of the exact value at `p` bits**;
- soft-fp keeps 3 work bits (`_FP_WORKBITS = 3` **[recalled, check
  `op-common.h`/`double.h`]**). So the jam happens with margin 3.

The stamp is the predicate
`Stamped (v : Nat) (X : Nat) (k m : Nat) := v = rto_k X ∧ m ≥ 2`, read as
"`v` at scale `2^k` hallmarks the exact `X`, with margin `m` below the final
rounding position". It is carried through the routine's core by rules that
preserve it. At round time, `rne_of_stamp` swaps the machine's `rne3` of `v`
for `F64.round` of the exact `X`. That is the spec's `normalize`/`roundExact`
of the exact aligned sum.

### Core rules

All are Nat lemmas with `2^k` symbolic. Side conditions are linear in the
shift atoms (`k`, `d`, `j`), so `omega` closes them with `2^k` as an
uninterpreted atom. **No variable `BitVec` shift appears in any rule's
conclusion.**

1. **`srs_rto`** (L5-stage, checked): for `1 ≤ k ≤ 63` and `x < 2^64`,
   `srsMachine x k = rto_k x` (BitVec to Nat).
   - The one place a variable shift is unfolded. It is proved once over
     symbolic `k`, using `BitVec.toNat_ushiftRight` and
     `toNat_shiftLeft` (core lemmas) plus `Nat.mod_two_pow` facts.
   - At a site: syntactic match on the node body, which A's descriptor-free
     matching gives. **The node body must be in the canonical idiom form.**
     L5-stage found the join nodes are permuted, so the match is up to the
     argument permutation (A's permutation twin).
2. **`rto_big`**: `X < 2^k → rto_k X = [X ≠ 0]`. This covers the `d ≥ 56`
   ("`f ≠ 0` as pure sticky") branch and makes it one rule. The `k = 0`
   counterexample cannot occur here because this branch is guarded by
   `d ≥ 56`.
3. **`rto_add_even`**: `2 ∣ a → a + rto_k Y = rto_k (a·2^k + Y)`.
4. **`rto_sub_even`**: `2 ∣ a → Y ≤ a·2^k → a − rto_k Y = rto_k (a·2^k − Y)`.
   - The evenness is what makes subtraction commute with the jam. I worked
     the four parity cases by hand: if `a` is odd and `⌊Y/2^k⌋` even, the two
     sides differ by 1. So the side condition is load-bearing.
   - It holds in soft-fp because the larger operand was shifted left by 3
     (`slli …, 3` at `8002d7d4`/`d7d8`), so `a ≡ 0 mod 8`.
   - **This is my own derivation. Check it by evaluation** on the
     `LawChecks` vectors before relying on it.
5. **`rto_rto`**: `rto_j (rto_k X) = rto_{j+k} X`. This is the renormalising
   right shift by 1 after a carry out of bit 56, itself an SRS by 1 (known
   for RTO **[recalled]**; it follows from `Nat.div_div_eq_div_mul` and the
   sticky disjunction).
6. **`stamp_shl`**: `Stamped v X k m → j ≤ m − 2 → Stamped (v <<< j) X (k − j)… `.
   Stated carefully: after cancellation the machine shifts left by `j` (clz).
   The jam bit moves up `j`, the margin drops to `m − j`, and the stamp
   survives iff `m − j ≥ 2`.
   - For `__adddf3`, cancellation of more than 1 bit happens only when
     `d ≤ 1`, and then no jam happened (`k ≤ 1`, so the value is exact: case
     8).
   - So the rule's side condition is discharged by the branch guard
     `d ≥ 2 ⇒ j ≤ 1`. That is a linear fact, but **it needs the classic
     cancellation lemma: `d ≥ 2 ⇒` the difference has at most 1 leading
     zero. One Nat lemma, proved once.**
7. **`rne_of_stamp`** (the hand-off law):
   `Stamped v X k m → roundPack s E v = F64.round s X (E' ) false false`
   (with `E' = E − 1075 − k` per L5-stage's scaling).
   - It composes `roundPack = F64.round … rb st` (checked, 7,280 cases, to be
     proved once as a stage lemma) with **`round_rto`**:
     `F64.round s (rto_k X / 2^m') … = F64.round s X …` when `m ≥ 2`.
   - `round_rto` is the Boldo–Melquiond lemma specialised to `F64.round`'s
     `target`/`shr`/`rne`. It is proved once from `shr`'s closed form: `shr`
     of `rto_k X` by `s ≥ 2` gives the same `(q, rb, st)` as `shr` of `X` by
     `s + k`. Through `Nat.div_div_eq_div_mul` and a mod case split, it is
     one lemma.
   - **Subnormals.** `target`'s `max … (−1074)` changes the rounding
     position. The margin condition must hold at the *subnormal* position
     too. It does: the subnormal position is coarser, so the margin only
     grows.
8. **`exact_is_stamp`**: `Stamped (X·2^0) X 0 m` for any `m`. Exact values
   (`d ≤ 1`, multiplications' exact products before their own jam) enter the
   same pipeline.

**How it is decided at a use site.**
- **Rewriting.** The path's pre-round expression is rewritten by rules 1–6
  from the leaves (operands unpacked) towards the root.
- **Side conditions** are linear in the shift atoms and parities. Each is
  `omega` with `2^k` as an atom, plus `Nat.dvd` from the `<<< 3`.
- **Spec side.** `F64.add`'s finite clause is
  `normalize (sgnApply a (align m₁ …) + sgnApply b (align m₂ …)) (min e₁ e₂) false`.
  Its `align` *is* the exact `a·2^d` above.
- **Symbolic data.** Mantissas and `d` stay symbolic. Nothing enumerates
  values.

**Search.** Choosing which of rules 3–6 applies to a node is syntactic, by
the head symbol of the node's expression. No search, *provided* the node
boundaries line up with stage boundaries (L5-stage's "generator must cut at
stages"). Where they do not (rounding tail-duplicated into 6 sites), E's
plane split supplies the cut. **Flagged**: without E this is a search over
where to apply rule 1 inside an opaque node body.

### What it makes free

- **The per-routine "core" of C5-spec.** For `__adddf3` this is the
  finite/finite clause, and all of its `d`-branches (0, 1, 2–55, ≥56; same
  sign or not) become one rewrite chain, parameterised by the branch's guard
  facts.
- **The 5 sticky sites and 6 round sites of `__adddf3`** (and 5/5 of
  `__subdf3`) are the same rules, not 11 proofs.
- **`__subdf3`.** Exactly the same chain with the sign of `b` flipped
  (A's twin), so its proof is a re-instantiation.
- **`__muldf3` (1 sticky + 3 round sites), `__divdf3`, `__floatdidf` (1/1).**
  Their jams are RTO too. `__floatdidf`'s jam (int to 53 bits) is `rto_k` of
  the integer, and `F64.ofInt = normalize i 0` is `roundExact`.
  `rne_of_stamp` closes it.
- **Variable shifts.** The 42 in `__adddf3` meet `omega` only as `2^k`
  atoms. `srs_rto` is the single place where a variable `BitVec` shift is
  unfolded.

### What it costs

- **Setup.**
  - `rto` and rules 1–8: about 150–250 lines of Nat lemmas.
  - `round_rto` is the hard one. It needs `target`'s behaviour when `X` and
    `rto_k X / …` have the same `log2`. True, because RTO never changes the
    leading bit position when `k ≥ 1` and the value has ≥ 2 bits above `k`.
    One lemma.
- **What gets harder.**
  - Rules apply only if the lifted `F`'s nodes expose the stage expressions.
    This is the generator-cut problem (L5-stage refinement); see E.
  - The sign handling (same sign: add magnitudes; different signs:
    subtract the smaller from the larger, with a swap when `a < b`) is
    *control*, not data. That swap produces two mirror paths (A's `σ_swap`
    again).
  - Exact zero results (`x − x`) take the "zero sign is the caller's" rule
    in `normalize` (`zneg := false`, round-to-nearest gives `+0`). That is a
    separate control leaf, handled in E.

### Theories

- **Round-to-odd** (known: S. Boldo & G. Melquiond, *Emulation of FMA and
  correctly rounded sums: proved algorithms using rounding to odd*, IEEE
  Trans. Computers 57(4), 2008; also *When double rounding is odd*, 2005
  **[recalled, check venue and year]**). Formalised in Coq/Flocq. The "≥ 2
  extra bits" condition is theirs.
- **Ultrametric balls in the 2-adic integers** (known, e.g. Koblitz, *p-adic
  Numbers, p-adic Analysis, and Zeta-Functions*, GTM 58 **[recalled]**):
  the residues that RTO identifies are unions of 2-adic balls of radius
  `2^-k` (no sticky) or their complement. The evenness condition in rule 4
  is "the ball is invariant under translation by `a·2^k` iff `a` is in the
  radius-`2^{-(k+1)}` ball of 0".
- **Complete abstract interpretation** (known: Giacobazzi, Ranzato &
  Scozzari, *Making abstract interpretations complete*, JACM 47(2), 2000):
  `rto_k` is an abstraction for which `rne` (beyond margin 2) is *backward
  complete*. Rounding the abstraction loses nothing. That is exactly
  `rne_of_stamp`.
- **Hallmarking of precious metals** (novel as an analogy): a stamp applied
  once certifies a property that survives subsequent working (shift left
  within margin, adding an even multiple), and is checked only at sale
  (pack).

### Pilot

- **Level (b), `__adddf3`.** Prove the finite/finite equation for one sign
  pattern (same sign, `2 ≤ d ≤ 55`) with rules 1, 3, 7 only. Then extend to
  different signs (rule 4, rule 6), then `d ≥ 56` (rule 2), then `d ≤ 1`
  (rule 8).
- **Level (b), `__subdf3`.** Re-instantiate with `b`'s sign flipped. Report
  how many lines are *new*. The target is ≤ 10% of `__adddf3`'s.
- **The arms (LT/SUB/FORPREP).** Not targeted. They consume
  `SoftFloatSpec`, unchanged.
- **Smallest thing to build.** `rto`, `rto_rto`, `rto_add_even`,
  `rto_sub_even`, and `round_rto`, all in Nat, with no machine. Check rule 4's
  parity condition by evaluation on 10^4 random `(a, Y, k)` first. Then prove
  `round_rto`. If `round_rto` takes more than ~150 lines, the abstraction is
  in trouble, and that is the go/no-go point.

### Seed

"hallmarked" gave the stamp that survives working. "supralineal" (above the
line) gave the split between kept bits and a residue known only by class.
"vitasti" (a fixed span of a hand) gave the margin: a fixed span of 2 work
bits that must stay between the jam and the rounding position.

## E. Biplane: a lifted routine split into a control plane (closed `decide`) and a data plane (stage rewrites)

### Idea

A biplane has two wings of different construction, braced together.
Siliceofelspathic rock is two minerals that are one stone.

The lifted `Adddf3.F` has 497 paths. No proof should follow them. But
nearly all of the branching is **control**:
- class tests: exponent 0 or 2047, fraction zero, sign equality;
- range tests on `d`: 0, 1, ≤ 56, > 56;
- the carry test (bit 56);
- the rounding test (`(x & 7) ≠ 0`, `(x & 15) ≠ 4`).

And nearly all of the computing is **data**: the aligned sum, the jam, the
round, the pack.

The abstraction re-expresses each lifted routine, and its spec, as a
**control function into a finite leaf set** plus **one data expression per
leaf**:
`F x y = data_{ctl(atoms(x, y))}(x, y)` and `S x y = data'_{ctl'(atoms(x, y))}(x, y)`.
- The atoms are a fixed, small vocabulary of *field predicates* on the
  unpacked operands (sign, `bexp = 0`, `bexp = 2047`, `frac = 0`, ordering of
  `bexp`s, `d` range, ordering of fracs).
- The **control plane** (that `ctl` and `ctl'` agree up to a leaf map) is a
  closed `decide` over atom valuations, B's mesh in level-(b) clothes.
- The **data plane** (each leaf's data expressions are equal) is D's stamp
  rewriting, or `rfl` for the special values (NaN gives `qnan`, ∞, signed
  zero).

For the comparison routines (`__ledf2`/`__gedf2`/`__eqdf2`: 67/67/19 paths,
no shifts, constant outputs), the data plane is empty. **The whole level-(b)
equation is the control-plane `decide`.**

### Core rules

1. **Atom extraction lemmas** (once, shared by all routines). For the field
   expressions the ELF uses:
   - `(x >>> 52) &&& 0x7ff`, which is `bexp x`;
   - `x &&& (2^52 − 1)` and `(x <<< 12) >>> 12`, which are `frac x`;
   - `x >>> 63`, which is `sign x`;
   - `x <<< 3` scaling;
   - comparisons of those with literals.

   Each is a `BitVec` to `F64`-field equation with **literal** shift amounts,
   so `omega` and `BitVec.toNat_*` succeed. **Decided by** syntactic match
   (`simp only [atoms]` on the node bodies).
   - Flag: L5-stage found node bodies alpha-variant and permuted. The simp
     set is directed by head symbols, so the permutation is harmless. A
     compound guard (`addiw` then `andi 2046`, at `8002d96c`, testing
     `bexp + 1 ∈ {0, 2047}` mod 2048, i.e. "`bexp ∈ {0, 2047}`") needs its
     own atom lemma. **There will be a handful of such composite guards per
     routine. Count them; if > ~10 per routine, the atom vocabulary is
     wrong.**
2. **`ControlPlane`, a named structure for a routine.**
   ```
   structure Plane (α : Type) where
     atoms : List Atom                           -- closed
     ctl   : (Atom → Bool) → Leaf                -- closed: the guard tree, as data
     data  : Leaf → α → α → BitVec 64            -- per-leaf data expression
   theorem plane_eq (P Q : Plane) (μ : P.Leaf → Q.Leaf)
     (hctl : ∀ v ∈ feasible, μ (P.ctl v) = Q.ctl v)     -- decide, closed
     (hdata : ∀ ℓ x y, P.data ℓ x y = Q.data (μ ℓ) x y)  -- per leaf: D's rules or rfl
     : ∀ x y, P.run x y = Q.run x y
   ```
   **`F` and `S` must each be shown to *be* a `Plane.run`.**
   - For `S` (the `F64` op): by unfolding `cls` into atoms, once per spec op.
     That is `F64.add`'s 9 clauses: 9 leaves plus the finite clause's
     internal `normalize` test.
   - For `F` (the lifted function): **the generator emits `F` in plane form**,
     a guard tree over atom-lemma-normal guards, with leaves holding data
     expressions. `F = P.run` then holds by construction (the generator's
     node DAG *is* a tree of `if`s over guards, which becomes
     `ctl`/`data` by `rfl`).

   This changes the level-(a) generator's output shape, not its proofs. The
   data cut is at the pack join, the stage boundary L5-stage asks for.
3. **The feasibility filter** (as in B). Atom valuations are not
   independent: `bexp = 0` excludes `bexp = 2047`, and the orderings are
   transitive. `feasible` is a closed Boolean filter, proved sound once per
   atom family. Infeasible valuations are excluded before `decide`.
4. **The data plane for arithmetic leaves.** D's `rne_of_stamp` chain. For
   special leaves (NaN, ∞, zero), `rfl` after atom rewriting:
   `F64.qnan = 0x7ff8…` is `qnan`, and the machine's NaN result was checked
   bit for bit, NaN payloads included (L5-comm).
5. **Frame forwarding** (the 14 reloads). These are not this abstraction's,
   but the plane form requires them gone: `F` must not mention `m`. The
   generator forwards spills at emission (L5-stage suggests this). It is a
   read-over-write on the routine's *own* `writeMap8` frame chain, a closed
   offset list. One generic lemma, `readOwnFrame`, is applied by the
   generator (data), and `FrameMeta.memFrame_of_chain` already does the
   analogous thing.

**How it is decided.**
- Control: `decide` on closed `(atoms, ctl, ctl', feasible)`. Size
  `2^|atoms|` filtered. For `__adddf3` the atoms are about: 2 signs, 4 class
  tests, `d` range (5 classes as 3 Bool atoms), carry, the cancellation
  shift's range, and the rounding test's 2 bits. That is about 14 atoms,
  2^14 = 16k raw valuations before filtering.
  - **Check whether this `decide` fits 200k heartbeats.** If not, split
    `hctl` by the top-level sign atom and class atoms (≈ 16 sub-decides of
    ~1k each). The split follows the spec's 9 clauses, which are themselves
    the natural cut.
- Data: rewriting (D). Special leaves: `rfl`.

**Search.** None in the control plane. In the data plane, see D.

### What it makes free

- **`SoftFloatSpec.eqdf2`, `.ledf2`, `.gedf2`: all three, completely.**
  - Their 19/67/67 paths have constant outputs and only field atoms (no
    shifts, no data). So `F = S` is `plane_eq` with `hdata` by `rfl` on
    constants, and `hctl` by `decide` over about 8 atoms (2 signs,
    `bexp` = 0 and 2047 for each, fraction zero for each, `bexp`/`frac`
    ordering).
  - The ordering atoms must be compared lexicographically, `(bexp, frac)`,
    which is `F64.compare`'s `.then`. **Subnormal versus normal is
    consistent** because `bexp = 0` orders below any `bexp ≥ 1`. Check
    `F64.compare` on `.fin` uses `e` from `cls`, which for subnormals is
    `-1074` (the same as `bexp = 1`), so ordering on `e` ≠ ordering on
    `bexp`. The leaf data must use `cls`'s `m` (with the hidden bit).
    **Real hazard; check.**
- **`__fixdfdi` (6 paths) and `__floatsidf` (2)**: mostly control. Their
  data leaves are a shift by `bexp − 1075`. That is a variable shift, so
  D's `srs_rto` analogue (for truncation, `x / 2^k`, no sticky) applies.
- **`__adddf3`'s 8 non-finite clauses.** All control plus `rfl`.
- **The 497 paths.** They collapse to (number of `S` leaves × feasible
  `F` leaves mapping to them). Proof cost is linear in `F`'s leaves at the
  data plane, and closed computation at the control plane.

### What it costs

- **Setup.**
  - Atom lemmas (≈ 15 shared, plus composites).
  - `Plane` and `plane_eq` (≈ 50 lines).
  - Spec-side plane forms for `F64.add`/`sub`/`compare`/`eq`/`ofInt`/`truncInt?`
    (≈ 20 lines each, once).
  - The level-(a) generator emits plane form and forwards frame reloads.
    This is a **generator change** that touches 10k generated lines. Their
    proofs are regenerated, not hand-written. Risk: the generator's DAG
    shares subterms across branches (`N_` nodes reused). Turning the DAG
    into a guard tree can duplicate data leaves. The data leaves must stay
    *shared* (`data` is indexed by the join node, not the path), or the plane
    is exponential.
- **What gets harder.**
  - The "control first" form needs every data operation's *control
    dependence* to be on atoms. `__adddf3`'s rounding test reads bits of the
    *computed* sum (`(x & 7) ≠ 0`, `(x & 15) ≠ 4`). That is not an operand
    atom but a data-dependent guard.
  - Resolution: those guards stay *inside* the data plane. `rne3` is one
    stage function (L5-stage checked `roundPack = F64.round`), so its
    internal branches never surface as control. The plane cut must be at
    "pre-round value", not at instruction forks. **This is the subtle point,
    and the place the bake-off should probe first.**

### Theories

- **Control-plane and data-plane separation in networking** (known: e.g.
  Casado et al., *Ethane*, SIGCOMM 2007; the SDN literature **[recalled]**):
  forwarding decisions are a finite table computed apart from packet
  payload processing.
- **Predicate abstraction** (known: Graf & Saïdi, *Construction of abstract
  state graphs with PVS*, CAV 1997): the atoms are predicates, and the
  control plane is the abstract program. Here it is decided by a closed
  `decide`, not by a prover search.
- **Shannon expansion / if-then-else normal form** (known: Shannon 1949;
  Bryant 1986). `F = Σ_v [atoms = v]·data_v` is the Shannon expansion of the
  routine over its atoms.
- **Petrology: one rock, two minerals** (novel as an analogy): a composite is
  characterised by the separate laws of its phases plus the law of their
  interface (here, `μ`).

- **Level (b) held-out, `__subdf3`.** The same atoms, the same spec plane

  leaves (expect the same count).
- **The arms.** None; E is level (b) only. But B's `ForkTree` *is* the
  machine-level plane, so the two share `Mesh.valid`/`plane_eq`'s decision
  core. Build it once.
- **Smallest thing to build.** `Plane`, `plane_eq`, atom lemmas for the

- **A + B + C** cover the arm cluster.
  - C removes the per-register lines, B removes the replay and polarity

  - E's comparison-routine pilot is the cheapest falsifier for the whole
    level-(b) story.
  - D's `round_rto` is the go/no-go for the arithmetic routines.
  - `rto_sub_even`'s parity condition is my own derivation. Check it by
    evaluation before building on it.
- **Shared core.** B's `Mesh.valid` and E's `plane_eq` are the same closed
  decision ("two finite trees over shared atoms agree up to a leaf map, on
  feasible valuations"). They should be one definition.
